# Rebuilding the examples around creative model combinations

Build a small collection in which different models pass real artifacts to one another and produce something surprising: a room that becomes a mystery, a character that continues into another scene, and a miniature world that acquires motion and sound. Keep the September cull. Preserve the richer Sing composition. Recover useful components from the removed graphs by putting them to work inside complete creative workflows.

The user's clarification governs this rebuild: deleting the one-step slop was intentional. The request is for unique demonstrations of model combinations that create cool things. Early suggestions for generic productivity tools, clean product photographs, and familiar utility bundles do not settle that request. A graph earns attention through the transformation between its stages and the resulting artifact. Counting nodes, choosing the newest model, or making every example commercially useful does not answer that creative question.

This rebuild starts from awesome-noodles [`7661fc7`](https://github.com/nanoodlecom/awesome-noodles/commit/7661fc7), which includes the merged Sing restoration, and website revision [`eb57304`](https://github.com/nanoodlecom/nanoodle/commit/eb57304), which announces the five-workflow shelf. The repository now contains eight workflows, with six selected new sample runs identified in [selection.json](../evidence/selection.json). Their actual outputs, costs, and limitations are in the [evidence index](../evidence/README.md). This report connects those outcomes to the historical choices; a selected example does not imply every requested detail passed.

The forty-two-graph tree at `fcc72ff` makes the repetition concrete. Enumerating its `graphs/*.noodle-graph.json` files finds twenty-nine with exactly one node containing `fields.model`, eighteen containing an image, audio, or video upload node, and thirty-three containing the literal text `Volt` or `night-ride`. These are reproducible source observations, not measures of usefulness or audience response. A cutout model can be a useful component; repeating a radio scene across cutout, upscale, poster, speech, and video cards does not create thirty-three different creative demonstrations.

The same revision's `CURATION.md` repeatedly records no paid run, no committed sample, pending cover review, and README share-link round trips as the completed check. Several graphs required an upload without including the advertised starting artifact. Their notes were often candid about the limitation. The problem was promoting an unfinished model demonstration to the public shelf while its interesting result remained a follow-up.

Deslop also illustrates an unhelpful selection target. Revisions [`9d9d295`](https://github.com/nanoodlecom/awesome-noodles/commit/9d9d295) and [`0c689ae`](https://github.com/nanoodlecom/awesome-noodles/commit/0c689ae) changed the rewrite model around a reported ZeroGPT zero-percent result for one notice. The exploration also found substantial work outside the public five. The original main checkout remained at `5fc0a33` with uncommitted graphs and experiments while the cleanup worktree held a later catalog. This rebuild uses an isolated worktree so those earlier investigations remain intact. Paths below refer to that original monorepo research workspace where the material is still uncommitted; they are not claims that every artifact is published with this repository.

`awesome-noodles/experiments/agent-media/character-sprites/README.md` records a useful sequence of failures and a successful change of approach. Independently generated pose sheets repeated the leading leg. A colored guide did not establish opposite-leg motion. Image-to-video introduced unwanted camera or character rotation. Aligning the sheets reduced a measured helmet drift but did not repair the leg phases. The workable combination was a reference image, matching detached parts, and deterministic local rigging. Joint placement and animation could then change without regenerating artwork. The selected reference and parts reported $0.02 historically; successful research calls totaled $0.15, with unresolved eventual billing for failed requests. The later Iron Verdict game added local poses and code. Its playable result does not imply the graph generated the game itself.

The inventory-art prototype follows the same stronger principle. One generated item anchors the style of three image-edit branches; local packaging returns separate tiles, stable IDs, a manifest, and a preview. `inventory-demo/run-evidence.json` records four image calls and one text call, $0.04 reported exact, and a weak knife silhouette rather than a blanket quality claim. The fresh Last Light trial produced another four-item pack and integrated it into a small game. Its saved browser report contains seventeen passing checks. These are concrete local completion results, including an independent agent using the skill. They are not third-party adoption or proof that every future asset pack will work.

`experiments/agent-media/feature-demo/` contains a narrated screenshot film assembled locally, with a roughly twenty-one-second MP4, captions, and transcript. That distinction matters: generated speech plus deterministic composition can finish a convincing artifact while preserving the real source images. It does not need a video model to invent interactions. The old run lacked speech price metadata, so its cost remains unknown.

The local `album-release-kit` and `tabletop-encounter` drafts contain promising connections, but their curation note records a capture-script error that lost generated media before visual inspection. They therefore have no accepted image evidence from those calls. The tabletop notes also received the written brief without seeing the rendered map. That limitation directly informed Pocket mystery: the observation stage now reads the image before a writer invents an activity around its contents. The release kit's cover-to-banner reference reuse remains useful research, but it is not one of the three selected demonstrations for this request.

`experiments/multistep/README.md` records another instructive near miss: the prompt-change workbench executes two prompts and retains their real responses, but its reviewer made incorrect observations and claimed parsing behavior it did not execute. Actual JSON parsing in the harness supplied the schema evidence. This stays research. Similarly, the July `cool-ideas/` utility numbers are speculative prioritization, not adoption data. Its Art Director brief assigns a verdict before editing and returns that verdict beside an image the judge has never examined. A polished diagram does not make that a reliable approval step.

The three chosen additions use these lessons without turning the shelf into a collection of productivity forms:

| Example | Actual handoff | Artifact and decisive review |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Pocket mystery](../graphs/pocket-mystery.noodle-graph.json) | Muse generates a room; Gemini receives that image through `llm.img1`; GLM receives Gemini's landmark observations. | A room illustration, visible-landmark record, player handout, and spoiler-marked facilitator solution. Check the observations against the image and solve the clues independently. |
| [Storyboard relay](../graphs/storyboard-relay.noodle-graph.json) | Muse establishes a scene; Nano Banana Edit stages the next beat; Gemini compares both frames; Muse Edit receives the draft, original reference, and critique for one revision; a fresh Gemini pass reads the repaired pair. | Opening, draft, repaired frame, and concrete continuity notes. Compare identity, props, object counts, intentional state changes, and whether the repair actually helped. |
| [Tiny world film](../graphs/tiny-world-film.noodle-graph.json) | Muse creates a miniature; Wan animates the actual still; Mirelo receives the resulting video and generates foley. | First-frame and final-film graph outputs, with the silent intermediate retained by the capture harness. Watch both clips and listen for sound fitting what actually moved. |

Pocket mystery combines visual invention, visual observation, and game authorship. The Moon-Mender's Attic and Submarine Bakery produce different rooms and stories. The observer never receives the image-generation prompt; the writer receives only observations plus fixed design constraints. Requested-but-missing props have no direct path into the puzzle text. Models can still misobserve or invent details, so the raw observations remain visible. An initial poetic clue was ambiguous; the revised graph uses explicit precedence sentences with exact landmark labels. All six possible orders were independently enumerated for each selected case: both have one solution, matching their GM answer, with visible anchors and marked spoilers. The attic still calls its glowing moon dim, so factual prose adherence is imperfect. These are small narrated activities, not tested three-to-five-minute games. The [artifact review](pocket-mystery-review-notes.md) preserves both initial failures and final judgments.

Storyboard relay initially used three calls: generate, continue, and compare. Its first live orbital-delivery draft duplicated the story-critical seedling: one remained in the container while another appeared in the soil tray. The saved reader also flagged changed container geometry and a viewpoint that did not clearly move inside the greenhouse. Those observations justified a concrete extra handoff. The graph now makes five calls, adding one Muse Edit revision conditioned on the actual draft, original image, and critique, followed by a fresh Gemini comparison of the opening and repaired frame. The final reader receives the requirements without the earlier critique and inspects the new pixels. In the selected orbital run, the repair removes the duplicate plant and improves the planting pose; the hatch still appears closed. The additional stage helped without completing every requested change.

The alternate cut-paper crab and kite story also advances the action. Its repair restores the spool and pink sky while the kite's bow count and placement remain wrong; the final reader calls out remaining drift. Opening, draft, repaired result, and criticism remain inspectable. This is one fixed revision, not a conditional retry loop. The handoff addresses a flaw actually observed in an artifact, and the final review examines the image returned to the user. The useful result includes the imperfect repair and its limits rather than reviving a pre-edit verdict as proof of final quality.

Tiny world film recovers the removed video-foley component in a complete chain. Its walnut-shell watermill starts as an image, acquires visible motion, and gains sound from a model receiving the video itself. Text-to-sound from the original brief could describe an event the animation never produced. The silent take is an intermediate retained by the capture harness; the graph has no Combine leaf and advertises only first-frame and final-film sinks. Both selected films are five-second H.264 at 832×480 with AAC audio. Visual review finds the intended miniature motion with an orange-underside material drift in the alternate moth. Two model-assisted audio reviews describe fitting sounds without speech or music. They are not human listening sign-off, exact synchronization measurements, or seamless-loop proof. The artifacts make the sound model's actual contribution inspectable.

Pocket mystery and Tiny world film each contain three model calls; Storyboard relay contains five because the first critique earned a repair and the changed pixels needed fresh review. These are bounded choices, not node quotas or quality scores. Local sinks expose final artifacts, and the capture harness retains intermediates. Defaults and distinct alternate briefs live in `fixtures/`. All six selected final runs match current graph snapshots and have no execution or capture errors. The [evidence index](../evidence/README.md) also preserves earlier failures. File construction, API completion, catalog presence, and a model's own praise are insufficient output reviews.

The exploration made thirteen generation attempts, reporting $1.02045160 including unsuccessful attempts, and two model-assisted audio reviews reporting $0.00216386. Total reported spend was $1.02261546, about $1.02. These are historical account-specific observations, not a future quote or cap. The captured attempts preserve the raw precision and the reasons for reruns, including an array-capture bug and an invalid single-input Combine leaf removed from the film graph.

The surrounding surfaces have different jobs and should share source without dictating identical catalogs:

| Surface | Responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| `awesome-noodles/graphs`, README, and website Examples | Present a small, understandable collection of creative compositions, with honest inputs and actual sampled outcomes. A graph can be browser-worthy even when a hosted transport cannot accept its media. |
| Generated `recipes/` | Reproduce the committed graph through the JavaScript and Python executors. Include real input names and any source-install or local-media requirement; do not count a recipe as another example. |
| `noodle-skills/` | Add the instructions and local tools needed to put outputs into a project, as character rigging and inventory packaging demonstrate. Keep the graph's contribution distinct from agent-authored code. |
| `built-with-nanoodle/` | Show finished app experiences. Decodable app links are snapshots, not automatically current copies of their linked gallery graphs. |
| MCP | Expose suitable graphs with a usable contract and accurate transport support. Qualify limitations without erasing creative workflows from other surfaces. |

The existing app links demonstrate why this distinction needs maintenance. Decoding the five local `built-with-nanoodle` links found an older arena lineup, an older two-LLM song graph, and a surviving See, think, speak app. Link-loading tests cannot prove model availability, output quality, or parity. Its statement that an app uploads nothing anywhere also needs more accurate wording: model requests send supplied content directly to providers; nanoodle has no content-seeing backend. Keys stay on-device, share payloads travel in URL fragments, and this rebuild requires no on-site analytics. September 10 curation also recorded an MCP deployment serving thirteen older snapshots; that dated observation should not be repeated as a fresh deployment check.

Promising follow-ons are recombinations with a clear final artifact. Video foley now has its place in Tiny world film. Reference-conditioned item packs could become a small scene with inventory, portraits, and locally assembled cards; the successful parts-and-rig approach supports further animation experiments. Photo-to-song could combine visible observations with lyrics, an arrangement that consumes those lyrics, and the music model, while exposing where factual observation ends and fictional interpretation begins. Recover the creative chain from See, think, speak rather than simply replacing its TTS model.

Auto-dub deserves a separate recovery experiment after its real duration problem is fixed. The existing curation reproduces a one-second video being shortened to 0.35 seconds when replacement speech is shorter, in both headless runtimes, while the browser retains trailing silence. Preserve video duration and test silence padding before presenting translated voiceover as complete. Transcription uploads and embedded app preview need their own execution checks. This is not a reason to imply automatic sentence alignment or lipsync.

Shelve model-per-card wrappers, repeated setting variants, decorative controls, detector-score marketing, and judges that never see the artifact they supposedly approve. Retain their source as ingredients and research. A new card should answer three concrete questions: what unusual thing does the combination produce, what actual information or media crosses each meaningful handoff, and what saved output lets another person judge that contribution? If those answers differ only by model name, color palette, or fictional location, improve an existing workflow instead.

For future refreshes, retain the exact input, graph revision, model settings, and outputs of every featured sample. Keep the original sampled graph alongside a separately identified current runnable graph when a model changes. Do not reuse a Krea result as evidence for the replacement Flare contender, or a cover illustration as evidence that a video ran. Record failed attempts and observed limitations when they explain the final choice. A default case proves one artifact; an alternate case checks whether the editable inputs matter. Neither establishes external adoption. The immediate completed outcome is a small, compelling set of inspectable compositions that someone can open, change, and use to make another strange thing.
