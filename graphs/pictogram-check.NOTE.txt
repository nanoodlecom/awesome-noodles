# recipe-note
Every other picture in this catalog is audited on what it shows. This one is audited on what it SAYS — to a stranger who cannot read the local language and was never told the answers. A GLM director writes a PICTOGRAM CARD: one venue from a short menu (a trailhead visitor centre, a ferry terminal, a botanical garden, a ski lodge, a night market) and exactly five messages chosen from a closed list of things a sign must say — no swimming, first aid, exit to the left, no flash photography, fragile, hot surface, quiet please, recycling, no dogs, mind the step, drinking water, lost property — each with the canonical bold symbol that says it as a picture alone. A second GLM turns the card into a painter prompt: a straight-on board of five large square plaques in a row, each one flat high-contrast pictogram, and no letters, words or numbers anywhere, because a sign that needs its caption has already failed. Muse paints it. Then a blind Gemini reader stands where the stranger stands: for each plaque, left to right, it describes the glyph it actually sees and writes the MEANING it would take away. A GLM auditor scores the communication itself: MEANING (how many of the five messages the stranger received, in the stranger's own words), MISREAD (which plaques said something else, and what they said instead), and TEXT LEAK — letters, words or numbers anywhere on the board, the painter cheating with the one channel the flow forbids. The audit does not end the flow. A GLM editor turns it into concrete edit notes that may change only the failed plaques and must strip any leaked text, Muse Edit repaints, and a second blind reader and the same auditor re-score the corrected board against the same card. A ⚖️ Decide judge (PPLX Decider) headlines round 1 as a side output — **signs read clearly**, **meanings lost**, **text leaked**, or **wrong board** — but the deliverable is the corrected board.

Why this is not the hidden-word flow wearing a sign: Hidden word hides a spelling puzzle — real objects on a shelf whose initial letters spell a word, scored by naming the objects and checking their initials; only a viewer told the trick can play. A pictogram has the opposite contract: it must work on a viewer told nothing. That makes a failure available here that no presence or fidelity audit can state — the inverted meaning. A camera plaque drawn perfectly, but missing its red slash, does not merely drift: it tells the stranger photography is allowed, the exact opposite of its message. The glyph can be flawless and the sign still wrong, and only a blind decode — a reader who must commit to a meaning before seeing the card — can catch it.

Honest limits, in the open: the messages are chosen from a closed list of the world's most conventional signs, the ones with near-universal glyphs; an invented message with no canonical symbol would fail for unfair reasons, so the card never asks for one. The stranger is one vision model's reading, and symbol conventions vary by culture in ways a single reader flattens — a meaning scored KEPT means this reader received it, not every traveller would. Synonymy is the auditor's judgement call and it is instructed to be strict where a qualifier carries the message (no photography is not no *flash* photography). And the edit round repairs plaques one by one on a shared board; an edit that redraws the whole board to fix one slash can drift its neighbours, which is exactly what the blind re-read is there to catch rather than assume away.

Live proof, 2026-11-18 — SUCCESS end to end on the first attempt, all 16 nodes done, no errors, $0.0283 balance-verified. The director carded a botanical garden entrance: drinking water (tap arcing into a cup), first aid (bold plus in a rounded square), no dogs (dog on a lead under a red slash), recycling (three chasing arrows over an open bin), exit to the left (running figure over a left arrow). Round 1 is visually verified: five big bold plaques in a black frame on a pale garden wall, every glyph crisp, and not one letter anywhere — TEXT LEAK 0 on the first try. The blind stranger received four messages exactly (drinking water, first aid, no dogs, exit to the left — it added 'emergency' to the exit, which the auditor counted as the same message in the stranger's words) and scored MEANING 4 of 5; Decide v1.2 returned 'meanings lost'. The one lost point is recorded honestly as at least half the card's own fault: the card itself specified the recycling arrows 'over an open bin shape', the painter drew the bin faithfully, and the stranger reasonably read the plaque as 'recycling and trash disposal here' — the auditor held the strict line that an added disposal message is a different message. The editor's notes named plaques 1, 2, 3 and 5 as keepers and demanded plaque 4 show the arrows alone as the message; the corrected board is visually verified — the arrows are now the bold standard recycling symbol over a plain slatted bin, the four keepers and the wall are unchanged — and the blind re-read took 'recycling bin here' from it, re-auditing MEANING 5 of 5, MISREAD none, TEXT LEAK 0. One unscored wrinkle: the first-aid cross is black rather than the conventional red or green-on-white, and the stranger decoded it anyway — the flow scores the message received, not the colour convention, and the NOTE says so rather than quietly upgrading the claim.
