# recipe-note
A Stroop poster is one set of glyphs carrying two contradictory answers at once: the word RED printed in blue ink says RED with its letters and BLUE with its pigment, and a reader who is asked only for the ink must suppress the stronger channel - the meaning - to report the weaker one. Vision-language models show exactly this failure in the published Stroop literature: semantic legibility dominates ink perception, and on incongruent colour words a reader asked for the colour answers the word at a striking rate. That makes channel separation under interference a clean audit axis, and one no single read can certify: a reader who reports the words proves nothing about the inks, and a reader captured by the meanings produces a fluent, confident, wrong ink list that looks exactly like a misprint unless the two failure kinds are scored apart. This flow separates them by construction. Give it a plain backdrop and a GLM director picks one poster from a menu printed in its own prompt (SIGNAL GRID, HARBOUR GRID, ORCHARD GRID) and copies it onto a STROOP CARD: six cells in a 2-by-3 grid, each cell's WORD and INK named, four cells INCONGRUENT (ink differs from the word's meaning) and two CONGRUENT controls where word and ink agree. A second GLM turns the card into a painter prompt that spells every word letter by letter, names every ink, and forbids all other text - and is forbidden to 'correct' an incongruent cell. Muse prints the poster.

Two blind Gemini readers then work the same poster on separate channels, neither having seen the card. The WORD reader reports only the six words, cell by cell, and says nothing about colours. The INK reader reports only the six ink colours and is told, in its system prompt and its ask, that a word's meaning is not its ink and is a trap. A GLM auditor scores the channels separately against the card - WORDS exact per cell, INKS exact per cell, CONTROLS exact on both channels - and classifies every ink miss: a CAPTURE when the ink read equals the cell's word (the reader answered the meaning instead of the pigment - a reader-side interference event), a MISPRINT when the ink read matches neither the carded ink nor the word (the ink on the poster is genuinely wrong). The verdict follows: SEPARATED when every word and ink hits; CAPTURED when the words are perfect and the only ink misses are captures; MISPRINTED when a genuine misprint exists; GARBLED when a word misses. A Decide judge (PPLX Decider) headlines round 1 as a side output - **channels separated**, **reader captured**, **inks misprinted**, **words garbled**, or **not a poster** - but the deliverable is the corrected poster.

That verdict is not where this flow stops - the loop closes, per the standing rule that a created asset ends in the corrected asset, not in text. A GLM editor reads the card and the audit and writes ONE concrete repaint instruction, and it is gated in content, the rule this catalog learned from Molecule Check: on an audit with no misprints the editor must demand a verbatim unchanged copy. Captures get the opposite protection: the editor is forbidden ever to repaint a captured cell, because 'fixing' a capture means changing the ink to match the word - destroying the incongruence the poster exists to create, and converting a reader's failure into a painter's lie. Only genuine misprints are repainted, each named by its position in picture directions, its letters repainted in the carded ink with word and spelling held identical and every other cell left pixel-for-pixel alone. Muse Edit repaints, both blind readers re-read the corrected poster, and the same auditor re-scores it against the same card. The flow's final output is the corrected poster itself.

The honest limits are stated in the open. A capture is attributed to the reader because the word channel and the controls triangulate it - if the ink reader names the word's colour on an incongruent cell while the word reader reads that cell correctly and the controls pass, the pigment was legible and the meaning won; but the attribution is an inference, not a pixel measurement, and a poster whose ink is genuinely ambiguous between two colours can manufacture a false capture. The ink reader's colour naming is the softest score in the audit: purple against blue and orange against red are judgement calls at painterly resolution, and a strict reader can fail a correct ink. The congruent controls are what keep this honest - an ink reader that also fails its controls is not captured, it is unreliable, and CONTROLS says so. The three grids are menu variety, not coverage: each rotates which words carry the incongruence, and only the run that happens is evidence. One run proves the chain - director, brief, paint, dual blind channel reads, capture-aware audit, gated repair, re-audit - end to end on the grid the director happened to pick.
