# recipe-note
What is inside the thing is the one thing a picture of it cannot show - until you cut it open. Generators are happy to paint a handsome closed espresso machine, beehive, clock, ant farm or piano, and equally happy to invent a plausible-looking inside that no knowledgeable person would recognise. This flow makes the hidden interior a planned, measurable quantity - and then it does something about a miss. Give it a setting (one closed object on a plain background) and a GLM director writes a CUTAWAY CARD: one subject from a menu built for interiors (vintage espresso machine, glass observation beehive, mechanical tower clock, glass ant farm, grand piano with its lid raised), the exterior SHELL with the exact CUT that must open it, and exactly 5 canonical interior parts, each in a different named zone inside the shell (top-left through bottom-right) with a visible description, in functionally plausible positions. A second GLM turns the card into one painter prompt that demands the shell opened as carded, every part in place at its zone - not exploded, not laid out beside the object - and no labels, text, arrows or leader lines, because this is a picture, not a diagram. Muse paints it. A blind Gemini surveyor, who never saw the card, inventories only the parts actually visible INSIDE the shell, each with its zone, states whether the shell is OPEN, PARTIAL or CLOSED, and lists any interior-looking parts drawn outside the shell. A GLM auditor scores INTERIOR (X of 5 card parts present inside; a part found only outside earns nothing - it escaped), PLACEMENT (X of 5 present parts at their carded zone), SHELL (the surveyor's verdict, copied), MISSING and EXTRA (invented interiors, including escapees). A Decide judge headlines round 1: interior revealed, parts misplaced, shell closed, or wrong subject.

That verdict is not where this flow stops - the loop closes, per the standing rule that a created asset ends in the corrected asset, not in text. A GLM editor reads the card, the survey and the audit and writes ONE concrete edit instruction: the full target end state (all five parts in place at their zones, shell opened as carded, nothing invented, no labels) plus the audit's specific failures as extra demands - show the missing pump at bottom-left, move the misplaced part to its zone, remove the invented coil, open the shell if it stayed shut - naming the parts already right as keepers that must be left alone. Muse Edit applies those notes to the round-1 picture. The corrected cutaway is the flow's deliverable. A second blind surveyor re-inventories it, and the same auditor scores round 2 against the same card, so the fix is itself audited and the reader can see exactly what the edit bought - or cost. The Decide verdict stays, demoted to a side output on round 1.

Why this axis is new: the catalog's nearest neighbours audit things a viewer could in principle see. Exploded check separates the object's own exterior parts along one axis - separation is the success state there; here separation is failure, and the parts must stay in place inside a containing shell. Turnaround check predicts the occluded back exterior of objects - a surface that exists to be walked around to; an interior is not a surface at all, and no exterior view, front or back, shows it - it must be inferred from functional knowledge of how the thing works. That inference is what is being scored: does the painter know that the boiler sits above the group head, that the pump lives low, that a beehive's brood chamber is not its honey super? And SHELL CLOSED names the signature failure in advance - a beautiful, completely closed object with an interior score of zero - a failure every beauty judge passes and no presence audit on exterior content can even state.

Honest limits, in the open: interior parts are canonical, not manufacturer-exact - the card asks for the five parts a knowledgeable person would name for that kind of subject, not a service manual for a specific model, and the NOTE of a run should say so rather than upgrading the claim. Zone placement is a coarse nine-cell grid: it catches a boiler painted where the drip tray belongs, not a part shifted two centimetres. The surveyor is one visual reader; near-name calls (a 'motor block' for the card's 'pump') are absorbed by the auditor's same-part rule, and a borderline PARTIAL shell is the surveyor's judgement, recorded as such. The fix round has its own limit: an edit model asked to open a shell or add a missing interior part can drift the exterior or invent a sixth part - that is exactly what round 2's EXTRA and INTERIOR lines are for, and both audits are printed so the reader can see the trade. One repair round is unrolled, not a loop - if round 2 still misses, the flow shows the miss rather than spending again.

Round 1 is visually verified: the case stands open straight-on, the black anchor escapement sits top-centre, the brass movement frame fills the centre, the cable drum crowds the top-right, the iron weight hangs bottom-centre, and no text or labels appear anywhere. The blind survey scored SHELL OPEN, INTERIOR 5 of 5, PLACEMENT 4 of 5, MISSING none, EXTRA none - the one lost point is a zone judgement on the pendulum: its rod runs down the centre-left and its brass bob sits low-left, and the surveyor zoned the part by its rod (centre-left) against the card's bottom-left. Decide v1.2 returned 'interior revealed', which is more lenient than its own stated rule (that verdict required PLACEMENT 5 of 5) - recorded here rather than quietly upgraded; the GLM audit's 4 of 5 is the stricter, truer headline for round 1. The editor named the four placed parts as keepers and demanded the pendulum be moved down to bottom-left; the corrected picture is visually verified - the bob now rests at the very bottom-left of the case, case, escapement, movement, drum and weight unchanged - and the blind re-survey zoned it bottom-left, re-auditing SHELL OPEN, INTERIOR 5 of 5, PLACEMENT 5 of 5, MISSING none, EXTRA none. The loop repaired exactly the one positional miss it was handed, and the corrected cutaway is the deliverable.
