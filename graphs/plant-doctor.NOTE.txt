# recipe-note
Plant-care apps do one trick: upload a photo, get a generic diagnosis and a wall of advice — and nothing ever checks the advice against the plant, or the promised recovery against the symptoms. The diagnosis is a guess presented as a fact, and the "after" picture, if there is one, is a stock photo of a different plant. This graph splits the jobs across four model families and closes both loops: a Gemini vision node is the diagnostician (it writes a symptom card — species guess, pot and setting, then a numbered list of 5 to 8 visible symptoms with their positions on the plant, then an overall severity line — and it is forbidden from offering causes or treatments), a GLM node changes hats into a clinician (its recovery plan must tie every likely cause to specific numbered symptoms and mark each cause as a visual guess, never a certainty; its timed home-care steps may not require a new plant or a new pot; and it must name the plant's identity anchors on a "Keep exactly:" line), a second GLM compresses the plan into a single surgical edit instruction that starts with those anchors, and Muse Edit renders a simulation of the same plant after the plan has worked, from the original photo itself.

Then the simulation has to answer for itself, twice. A second Gemini vision node reads the recovery picture back and inventories only the health signs actually visible in it — it never sees the symptom card, so it cannot grade generously — a GLM recovery auditor scores the original card against that read-back symptom by symptom (RESOLVED / IMPROVED / STILL PRESENT, ending in an "X of Y symptoms resolved or improved" summary), and a ⚖️ Decide judge (PPLX Decider) gives the headline verdict from the card plus the simulation image: **recovered, same plant**, **healthier but drifted**, **still unwell**, or **different plant**. The graph exports the **Recovery — audited** verdict text, and the simulated recovery picture itself rides through the Decide node as its image output — open the Recovered plant node to see the goal picture the verdict is about.

Why the split matters: the model that diagnosed the plant is not the model that wrote the care plan, neither of them rendered the recovery, and neither grades it. The symptom card is written *before* any cause is proposed, so the plan cannot quietly redefine what was wrong; the read-back is written *without* the card, so the auditor compares two independent reads instead of one model's opinion of its own work.

Honest limits, in the open: a photo diagnosis is a visual guess, not ground truth — yellowing can mean overwatering, underwatering, light or food, and a picture cannot feel soil moisture or inspect roots, so treat the care plan as a starting point and check the soil and roots yourself before acting on it. The recovery picture is a simulation and a goal, not a prediction or a promise: real recovery takes weeks and may not match it. The read-back reads a shrunk copy of the simulation, so fine symptoms (a small spot, a single tip) are judgement calls, and the "( ? )" guesses flow straight into the score. Muse Edit re-renders rather than edits in place, so pot, viewpoint and setting can drift even when every symptom clears. And a generated staging photo was used for the live proof below — no real plant photo was staged in this workspace — disclosed here rather than smoothed over.

Expected cost per run: Muse Edit $0.011, the two Gemini vision reads roughly $0.005–0.007 each, the three GLM calls effectively $0.00–0.011 combined, and the Decide audit is not priced in the 2026-09-12 catalog snapshot — expect roughly $0.03–0.05 all in. Prices are observations, not caps.

Live proof, 2026-11-08: the full chain ran end to end for $0.0342 balance-verified across two spends ($43.0528 → $43.0185) — a generated staging photo of an unwell golden pothos ($0.0110 exact, Muse; no real plant photo was staged, as disclosed above), then ONE live run of the graph ($0.0232 metered: diagnosis $0.00461, care plan $0.00154, edit brief $0.00069, Muse Edit $0.011, read-back $0.00352, recovery audit $0.00185; the Decide audit unpriced in the report, ~$0.00001 by balance gap). The diagnostician read the staging plant almost exactly: golden pothos in a terracotta pot on a white windowsill, six symptoms — severely dry cracked caked soil, limp collapsed stems over the rim, widespread bleaching to pale yellow/straw, brown crispy tips and margins, bare leafless woody stems, and sparse foliage with a single healthy variegated leaf — overall 'Serious; likely saveable due to viable green growth at the top'. The clinician's 'Six-week rehydration reset' tied its causes to those numbers as visual guesses (chronic underwatering into hydrophobic soil; sun stress on drought-stressed vines; exhausted potting mix) and prescribed bottom-watering, filtered light, pruning to green tissue, and a soil refresh. Muse Edit's simulation is the same pothos in the same pot on the same windowsill, rebuilt: deep green-and-gold variegated leaves, firm arching stems, new centre growth, dark moist uncracked soil — visually verified. The two judges then split in the honest direction, and both are recorded: the Decide headline verdict was **recovered, same plant**, while the symptom-by-symptom audit scored **5 of 6 resolved or improved** — it marked the bare leafless woody stems STILL PRESENT (thin bare stem sections are arguably still visible among the new growth, and the read-back never certified them gone). That is the loop working as designed: the detailed audit is stricter than the headline judge, and its one holdout is a pruning judgement call, not a missed recovery.
