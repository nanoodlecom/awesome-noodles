# recipe-note
A thermal camera does not photograph what things look like - it measures how hot they are and paints the measurement in false colour: white for the hottest thing in the frame, down through yellow, orange, red and purple to blue and black for the coldest. Image models paint gorgeous thermal-style pictures that obey none of this: the shiny kettle comes out white-hot because it looks bright, the black frying pan comes out cold because it looks dark, and the ice glows cheerfully because nobody told the picture that colour here is a reading, not a decoration. This flow makes the reading a planned, measurable quantity - and then it does something about a violation. Give it a bench setting and a GLM director picks one thermal scene from a short menu printed in its own prompt (KITCHEN ROW, WORKSHOP ROW, CAMPFIRE ROW, BATHROOM ROW) - exactly five objects in a fixed left-to-right slot order, each with a thermal state cue (a burning flame, steam, a just-run motor, room temperature, frozen solid), a rank from 1 hottest to 5 coldest, and a band on the fixed scale - and copies it onto a THERMAL CARD. One object per card is a named DECOY: a black cast-iron skillet that is the second hottest thing in its scene, a polished chrome toaster or tap that is merely room temperature - objects whose ordinary-light appearance predicts exactly the wrong band. A second GLM turns the card into one thermal-camera prompt: the five objects in slot order on a near-black cold worktop, each in one dominant thermal colour equal to its carded band, identity carried by shape alone, no visible-light material colours, no text and no legend. Muse paints it. A blind Gemini reader, who never saw the card, reports per slot the object it sees and its dominant band, plus the background band. A GLM auditor scores OBJECTS (the right object in the right slot), BANDS (the exact band per object), RANK ORDER (the four adjacent rank pairs must descend the scale with no inversion and no tie), DECOY (the counter-stereotype object must be coloured by its heat, not its look) and BACKGROUND (the bench must read cold). A Decide judge headlines round 1: heat reads true, ranks shuffled, bands drifted, objects wrong, or not a thermal image.

That verdict is not where this flow stops - the loop closes, per the standing rule that a created asset ends in the corrected asset, not in text. A GLM editor reads the card, the read and the audit and writes ONE concrete edit instruction: the full target end state (the five objects in their slots, each in its carded band, named one by one) plus the audit's specific failures as recolour demands - repaint the skillet one uniform YELLOW, it read ORANGE and it is the second hottest thing here. The editor is gated in content, a rule this catalog learned the hard way from Molecule Check, whose flawless round-1 model was recoloured by a repair it never needed: on a perfect audit the editor must demand a verbatim unchanged copy and forbid anything being moved, added or recoloured. Muse Edit applies the notes to the round-1 picture. The corrected thermal image is the flow's deliverable. A second blind reader re-inspects it, and the same auditor scores round 2 against the same card, so the fix is itself audited and the reader can see exactly what the edit bought - or cost. The Decide verdict stays, demoted to a side output on round 1.

Why this axis is new: the catalog's nearest neighbours are palette-transplant, paint-mix-check and scale-check, and the differences are mechanical. In palette-transplant every colour is named on a card and transferred between pictures as decoration, scored present, shifted or missing - nothing is measured. In paint-mix-check a colour is derived, but from pigments physically present in the picture, by a mixing law the auditor computes. In scale-check an ordinal rank is audited, but of sizes that are plainly visible in the image itself. Here the painter must translate modalities: convert a scene described in ordinary visible-light terms into a false-colour measurement of a property that is invisible in the scene - temperature, inferred from semantic state cues like flame, steam and frost. The audit's centre is a monotonic law over the whole scene (any inverted adjacent pair fails the measurement even when every individual band is near), and its sharpest axis, the DECOY, scores whether heat beat appearance stereotype - whether the black pan came out hot and the chrome came out cold. That is a failure shape no colour flow in the catalog can state: RIGHT OBJECT, RIGHT SLOT, PLAUSIBLE COLOUR, WRONG PHYSICS. Not the closed plan, render and recount vein: nothing is counted in place and nothing is placed in a zone; a physical quantity is translated into colour, read blind off a fixed scale, and repaired by recolouring.

Honest limits, in the open: bands are judged as the single dominant colour per object in seven named bands, because finer thermal vocabulary turns the audit into naming arguments; a genuinely two-tone object (a candle whose flame is white over a yellow jar) is scored by the colour that covers most of it, and the reader is instructed to ignore small hotter cores and rims - a real thermal camera would show gradients everywhere, and this flow deliberately audits the simplified one-object-one-reading claim that thermal posters and product pictures actually make to a human viewer. The five carded bands are spaced on the scale (WHITE, YELLOW, RED, PURPLE, BLUE) so adjacent ranks are at least one named band apart and a one-band reading error cannot silently pass as the right order. The state cues are semantic, not sensed - the flow audits whether the painter honoured the carded physics, not whether a real skillet would be that hot. One repair round is unrolled, not a loop - if round 2 still shuffles a rank, the flow shows the shuffle rather than spending again.
