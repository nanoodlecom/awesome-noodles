# recipe-note
Depth in a one-point perspective picture is not painted object by object; it is ruled by a single point. Every receding parallel in the scene — both top edges, both ground edges, every row of bays, shelves, trees or lamps — must, extended, pass through the same vanishing point, and where that point sits (its height is the horizon; its sideways offset is where the viewer is really standing) decides the whole geometry. Generators are famously loose here: they paint a plausible corridor whose lines quietly aim at two or three different points, or at none — a flat facade wearing perspective shading. This flow makes the point a planned, measurable quantity. Give it a setting (the default is any straight, level location seen from its exact centre line) and a GLM director writes a PERSPECTIVE CARD: one scene from a menu of strong one-point locations (railway platform, market arcade, library aisle, canal towpath, cloister walkway, glasshouse path), a vanishing point chosen from deliberately OFF-CENTRE coordinates (VP X one of 35, 42, 58, 65 percent of the width — never 50; VP Y one of 38, 46, 54 percent of the height), and exactly 5 side objects with distinct colours in front-to-back order, alternating sides. A second GLM turns the card into one painter prompt that states the vanishing point in plain words, and Muse paints the scene. A blind Gemini surveyor, who never saw the card, then does the projective work: it lists the side objects, extends the four named receding edges in its mind, estimates the X and Y where they best meet, and judges each edge CONVERGES, PARALLEL or ABSENT, plus a DEPTH line. A GLM auditor subtracts planned from measured and scores IDENTITY (X of 5 objects in position), VP ERROR (the two absolute differences), VP HIT / NEAR / MISS (both axes within 8 / within 15 / beyond), CONVERGENCE (X of 4 edges), and FLAT (the surveyor's DEPTH is NONE or no edge converges). A ⚖️ Decide judge (PPLX Decider) sees the picture and all the texts and gives the headline verdict: **true perspective**, **point drifted**, **flat facade**, or **wrong scene**.

Why the loop matters: the catalog's other spatial audits each measure a quantity carried by things — scale check measures height shares inside one frame, stereo check measures displacement between two frames, shadow check a shadow against its caster. Convergence is a quantity carried by LINES THAT ARE NEVER DRAWN: the vanishing point usually sits in empty space or behind a distant object, and no object inventory can state it; the surveyor has to extrapolate four edges and intersect them. The off-centre card is the second mechanism: a centred vanishing point is the generator's default composition, so it proves nothing — a point at 42 percent across and 46 percent down can only appear if the painter actually followed the plan, which makes VP ERROR a plan-fidelity measurement, not a vibes grade. And FLAT FACADE names the signature failure in advance — receding lines that stay parallel, the picture-book corridor that never actually recedes — a failure a beauty judge would pass every time.

Honest limits, in the open: the surveyor's vanishing point is a visual estimate from extending edges by eye, not a fitted line intersection — expect several points of reader noise, which is why HIT carries an 8-point band on both axes, NEAR stretches to 15, and CONVERGENCE asks only whether each edge passes near the surveyor's own meeting point, not the card's. The sign convention is scored, unlike stereo check's unsigned disparity: a vanishing point mirrored to the wrong side is a real plan failure, and the X error catches it. One-point perspective is also the only projection tested; a two-point street corner would need a second vanishing point and a different audit, and is deliberately out of scope. The side objects are scored for identity and order only — their shrinking toward the point is implied by the convergence scores, not measured separately, so a painter could in principle rule perfect lines and still draw same-size lamps; the Decide judge sees the picture and can catch that, but the numeric audit does not.
