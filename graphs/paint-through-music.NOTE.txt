# recipe-note
Every flow in this catalog that crosses modalities crosses once. This one goes there and back. Muse paints a scene from your brief — the default is a harbour festival at golden hour, boats returning, a brass band on the quay, couples dancing. A Gemini sound scout reads that painting and writes a SOUND CARD in a closed five-axis vocabulary: MOOD, ENERGY, PACE, the instrument FAMILY that should lead, and the ARC the cue should follow, each word chosen from a fixed list, plus a SETTING line. A GLM composer turns the card into one music prompt and ElevenLabs Music performs a 20-second instrumental cue. Then the crossing back: a Qwen omni model LISTENS to the finished cue through the LLM node's audio port — it never sees the painting, the card, or the prompt — and writes a LISTENING REPORT in the same five axes, the instruments it actually heard, and a PICTURE line: one concrete scene this music could be the soundtrack to. A capped GLM painter turns that report alone into an image prompt — axes translated into light, weather and motion — and Muse paints the second painting. That repaint is the flow's deliverable: a scene that has been heard, not described. A Gemini read-back inventories the repaint, a capped GLM auditor scores the round trip as a side output (card against report axis by axis, MATCH / NEAR / OPPOSITE, plus whether the listener's PICTURE survived into the repaint), and a ⚖️ Decide judge (PPLX Decider) sees both paintings and gives the headline: **same scene heard back**, **same mood, new scene**, **mood lost**, or **silence or noise**.

Why the loop matters: the two nearest catalog neighbours each stop halfway. Score the still runs picture → card → music → blind listener and terminates in its audit verdict — the music is the end product and nothing is ever made from what was heard. Hear it, paint it runs the other crossing once, from a written sound script through foley effects to a painting — its source is words, and its audio is sound events, not composed music. Words only, repainted is the language-bottleneck cousin: pixels to words to pixels, one hop each way through a description. Here the bottleneck is not language at all but performed music: everything the second painter knows about the first painting arrived as timbre, tempo and mood, filtered through a listener who had to invent a scene for sounds. That makes the losses locatable in a way a description round trip cannot offer — an axis can be charged to the composer (the card asked, the report never heard it), to the listener (heard, but its PICTURE chose a different scene than the axes imply), or to the second painter (the PICTURE was right and the read-back shows something else).

Honest limits, in the open: nobody should expect the same scene back. Music carries feeling far better than inventory — no cue encodes "bunting between the masts" — so 'same mood, new scene' is the realistic success and 'same scene heard back' would be a small miracle; the Decide options are ordered to say so. The listener is the weak joint, as in every audio flow here: it can report the mood it expected rather than the one it heard, and its PICTURE line is an act of imagination, not a measurement — the audit scores that line against the repaint, never against the first painting, because the first painting is precisely what the listener could not have known. The five closed axes are a deliberate compression: they are what makes drift countable (X of 5 axes heard), at the price of flattening everything a real score does that five words cannot say. And the second painter paints a scene, not a sound: the read-back certifies what the repaint shows, not that it "sounds right" — that last judgement belongs to the Decide headline and to your own eyes on the pair.

Live proof, 2026-11-18 — SUCCESS end to end on the first attempt, all nodes done, no errors, $0.2306 balance-verified. The round trip kept the family and lost the party, and every hop's witness says exactly where. The first painting is the brief to the letter: golden-hour harbour, coloured sails, bunting, gulls, a brass band on the quay and couples dancing (visually verified). The sound card read it joyful / medium / moderate / brass / steady. The composer's prompt was faithful — "lilting, dance-like melody", "couples on the quay", "bright, resolved chord". But the performed cue came back to the blind listener as solemn and slow: report solemn / medium / slow / brass / swells and settles, HEARD trumpet and horn ensemble, and its PICTURE was "a misty dawn over a quiet stone bridge where soldiers stand at attention for a memorial service". The second painter, who knew only that report, painted exactly that — misty dawn, stone bridge, six soldiers at attention beside a flag and a memorial wreath (visually verified) — and the read-back matches the PICTURE line for line, so the auditor scored PICTURE KEPT: yes while the axes went Mood OPPOSITE, Energy MATCH, Pace NEAR, Family MATCH, Arc NEAR, "2 of 5 axes heard"; Decide returned 'mood lost'. The loss is charged where the texts put it: not the card, not the composer, not the painters — the performance itself (mellow horns at a steady arc are one short step from a memorial) or the listener's reading of it, the one joint the witnesses cannot separate, as disclosed above. Two honest wrinkles: the auditor printed an extra "Setting: OPPOSITE" line ahead of its five axis lines (the summary arithmetic is unaffected), and "solemn" is not in the listener's closed mood list — the model reached outside the vocabulary for the word the cue actually earned, and the auditor scored it OPPOSITE against joyful, which is the right call. The cue is a real 20-second MP3.
