Restore the Photo — Demand Watch ship 8 (cycle 12, 2026-11-22). Restoration is a standing paid
market: human studios charge $20–85 per photo (Thumbtack's average job is $78; severe work runs
$150–500), Fiverr tiers sit at $15/$35/$75, and even AI apps charge $4.49 a single. This flow runs
the whole job for about $0.04: a vision survey inventories the damage first, Seedream 5.5 repairs
it under a hard no-inventions rule, a Pruna upscale restores print size (2896×2896 from a 1k
repair), and an identity guard compares the result against the original and prints a repair
ledger — repaired / rebuilt / left alone — with a RESTORE: HELD or DRIFTED verdict.

The demo photo is a generated 1940s portrait with synthetic damage applied locally (three
vertical scratches, one crossing the face; a torn bottom-left corner; a stain; a crease; sepia
fade; dust) so the survey and ledger could be graded against known ground truth: the vision
survey found every planted defect, and the saved run's ledger keys to it item by item.

Honest edges, all found in probing: (1) the first probe restore invented a necklace the guard
initially passed — the restore prompt now carries a hard no-additions clause and the guard
checks neck/ears/wrists/background adversarially; the guard is a check, not a guarantee, and the
guide says so. (2) The guard only keyed its ledger to the survey once the survey text was wired
as its prompt with the instructions in the system field; an llm node fed on the text port alone
twice failed to use it (once blind to its images, once inventing its own survey). (3) A leading
llm node with a wired image intermittently receives no image at all (the model asks for an
upload) — the survey therefore uses the vision node type, which was reliable in every run.
(4) Topaz's upscale rejects text prompts (Bloom algorithms only); Pruna's upscale route takes
the image cleanly at $0.01. (5) A monochrome original stays monochrome — no colorize step.
