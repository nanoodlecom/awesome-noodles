# recipe-note
Time in a still picture is a direction, and image models get it backwards as often as forwards. Asked for a before-and-after, a generator paints two handsome panels - and the candle in the later panel is taller, the snowman fresher, the sandcastle grander, because each panel is painted to look its best and nothing in a single panel knows which way time runs. The arrow only exists BETWEEN the panels: the after state is derived from the before state by an irreversible process (snow melts, wax burns, sand washes - none of them run in reverse), so the second panel's subject must be a diminished remnant of the first's, by a carded amount, while the things the process cannot destroy - a top hat, a brass holder, a plastic bucket - must still be there, displaced, and the change must leave its trace on the ground. This flow makes that arrow a planned, measurable quantity - and then it does something about a violation. Give it a diptych setting and a GLM director picks one transformation from a menu printed in its own prompt (MELTING SNOWMAN, BURNING CANDLE, WASHED SANDCASTLE) and copies it onto a METAMORPHOSIS CARD: the subject, its BEFORE state at REMAINING 100, the AFTER state physics derives with its target REMAINING (15, 35 or 20), the RESIDUE items that must survive into the after panel, and the TRACE the change leaves. A second GLM turns the card into one painter prompt demanding the same scene, viewpoint and backdrop in both panels, and Muse paints the diptych. Two blind Gemini readers then split the evidence by panel - neither can borrow the other's panel to invent a trend: the LEFT reader reports only the left panel (subject, COMPLETENESS 0-100, loose objects, trace) and the RIGHT reader does the same for the right panel alone. A GLM auditor subtracts the reads against the card: BEFORE, AFTER, the DROP and its DIRECTION, whether the after state lands in its derived band, and RESIDUE X of Y - TRANSFORMED only when time ran forward, by roughly the carded amount, on the same subject with its indestructibles surviving; REVERSED when the subject grew, FROZEN when nothing changed, UNDERDONE or OVERSHOT when the amount is wrong, IDENTITY LOST when the before panel failed, the subject changed, or no residue survived. A ⚖️ Decide judge (PPLX Decider) sees the picture and all the texts and gives the headline verdict on round 1 as a side output.

Why the loop matters: the catalog's other two-state audits each hold one quantity fixed - turnaround check predicts the unseen back of a fixed object, stereo check measures displacement between two viewpoints of an unchanging scene, storyboard relay tracks a story across generations. None of them audits a state CHANGE whose correct size and direction are derived before painting: here the ground truth is not what either panel looks like but the difference between them, a quantity no single-panel read can state and no prettier panel can fake. Per the owner's end-in-the-asset rule, the audit is not the deliverable: a gated GLM editor turns any violation into concrete repaint notes for the RIGHT panel only (a transformed audit demands a verbatim copy; the left panel is never diminished to fake a drop), Muse Edit repairs the diptych, and both blind readers plus the same auditor re-score the corrected picture, which is the flow's final output.

What it does not prove: completeness is a blind reader's estimate, not a measurement - the bands are wide (direction needs a 25-point drop, the after band is ±20) precisely because a vision model's percentage is a judgement, and a transformation near a band edge can be honestly argued either way. Residue matching runs on the reader's object names, so a survivor the reader names strangely can score as lost. One run proves the chain - director, brief, paint, dual blind panel reads, direction audit, gated right-panel repair, re-audit - end to end on the transformation the director happened to pick.
