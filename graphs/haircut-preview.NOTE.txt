# recipe-note
Haircut-preview apps do one trick: upload a selfie, tap a style, get a render of *a* person with that haircut — and the classic failure is that the haircut is lovely and the person is gone: the freckles smooth away, the mole migrates, the face subtly becomes someone else, and nothing in the app ever checks the preview against you. The opposite failure is just as common: the "new cut" is the old hair with a filter on it. This graph splits the jobs across four model families and closes both loops: a Gemini vision node is the identity clerk (it writes a face card — a numbered list of 5 to 8 face anchors with side and position, your current hair on its own line as the one thing allowed to change, then a vibe read from your expression), a GLM node changes hats into a stylist (it picks ONE specific haircut because it suits that face shape and your brief, its plan must repeat every numbered anchor on a "Keep exactly:" line, and the brief's hard constraints — no dye, no straightening — win over the stylist's taste), a second GLM compresses the plan into a single surgical edit instruction that starts with the keep-list, and Muse Edit renders the new cut on the original photo itself, not on a text description of you.

Then the preview has to answer for itself, twice. A second Gemini vision node reads the preview back and inventories only the features actually visible in it — it never sees the card, so it cannot grade generously — ending with a Hair line describing the cut as rendered. A GLM identity auditor scores the card against that read-back anchor by anchor (KEPT / CHANGED / LOST, ending in an "X of Y face anchors kept" summary) and states plainly how the rendered hair differs from the card's current-hair line. And a ⚖️ Decide judge (PPLX Decider) gives the headline verdict from the card plus the preview image: **same person, new cut**, **face drifted**, **barely a haircut**, or **different person**. The graph exports the **Preview — audited** verdict text, and the preview itself rides through the Decide node as its image output — open the Preview node to see the cut the verdict is about.

Why the split matters: the model that rendered the preview is not the model that wrote the face card, and neither of them grades the likeness. The card is written *before* any cut is chosen, so the stylist cannot quietly redefine what counts as you; the hair sits on its own line precisely so the audit can demand the face stay fixed while requiring the hair to change — a check a single "did it work?" prompt would blur into one shrug.

Honest limits, in the open: the face card is a photo read, not ground truth — the clerk can misread a mole's side, and every downstream check then faithfully defends the wrong card, so read the card before you trust the audit. The read-back reads a shrunk copy of the preview, so fine anchors (a small mole, faint freckles) are judgement calls, and the "( ? )" guesses flow straight into the score. Muse Edit re-renders rather than edits pixels, so expression, clothing and background can drift even when every anchor survives — the audit scores the face and hair, not the jumper. And the preview is a simulation of how a cut might sit, not a promise from a salon chair: your hair's actual behaviour is between you and your stylist. A generated staging photo was used for the live proof below — no real portrait was staged in this workspace — disclosed here rather than smoothed over.

Expected cost per run: Muse Edit $0.011, the two Gemini vision reads roughly $0.005–0.007 each, the three GLM calls effectively $0.00–0.011 combined, and the Decide audit is not priced in the 2026-09-12 catalog snapshot — expect roughly $0.03–0.05 all in. Prices are observations, not caps.

Live proof, 2026-11-09: the full chain ran end to end for $0.0300 balance-verified across two spends ($30.9954 → $30.9654) — a generated staging portrait of a copper-red, freckled sitter ($0.0110 exact, Muse; no real portrait was staged, as disclosed above), then ONE live run of the graph ($0.0190, metered exactly matching the balance delta: face-card read $0.00433, Muse Edit $0.011, preview read-back $0.00371, the three GLM calls $0.00 at flash rates, Decide unpriced in the report). The identity clerk read the staging portrait almost exactly: oval face, green/hazel almond eyes, dense golden-brown freckles, and the small dark mole placed precisely — above the right corner of the upper lip, viewer's left. The stylist chose a jaw-grazing wavy bob (roughly ten inches off, internal layers, centre part kept, no fringe, colour and waves untouched per the brief), and Muse Edit's preview is visually verified as the same woman in the same sweater at the same café window with exactly that bob. The judges honestly split: Decide returned 'same person, new cut', while the anchor audit scored 5 of 7 kept — Face shape CHANGED (card 'rounded chin' vs read-back 'soft square jawline') and Lips CHANGED (card 'balanced fullness' vs read-back 'medium fullness'), both wording strictness on near-matches rather than moved features; the audit's Hair line confirmed the cut landed: chin-length bob versus chest-length hair, colour, parting and no-fringe all preserved.
