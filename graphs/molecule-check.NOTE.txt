# recipe-note
A molecule is not a picture of balls - it is a graph with a law. Every atom carries a valence budget (carbon spends exactly 4, oxygen 2, hydrogen 1, counting a double bond twice), so a model can look gorgeous and still be chemically impossible: one rod too many on a carbon and the thing on the poster is not a molecule at all. This flow makes that law a planned, measurable quantity - and then it does something about a violation. Give it a model setting and a GLM director picks one molecule from a short menu (METHANOL, ETHANOL, ACETIC ACID, BENZENE) whose full spec is printed in its own prompt - exact atom counts per element, the exact bond list as element pairs with orders, the ring - and copies it onto a MOLECULE CARD, so the card cannot invent chemistry. A second GLM turns the card into one painter prompt that demands a ball-and-stick model in CPK colours (carbon charcoal, hydrogen small white, oxygen red), one rod for a single bond and two parallel rods for a double, and no element letters, labels or name anywhere, because print would cheat the colour code: the molecule must exist only as spheres and rods. Muse paints it. A blind Gemini reader, who never saw the molecule or the card, enumerates every sphere in reading order with its element by colour, and every rod connection with its rod count, listed from both ends. A GLM auditor rebuilds the graph from that read and scores ATOMS per element, BONDS as an element-pair-and-order multiset, VALENCE atom by atom (each sphere's rod-count sum against its element's budget, violators named by reader number), RING size, the DECODED FORMULA the spheres actually spell, and LABEL LEAK. A Decide judge headlines round 1: chemically exact, atoms drifted, bonds drifted, label cheat, or wrong molecule.

That verdict is not where this flow stops - the loop closes, per the standing rule that a created asset ends in the corrected asset, not in text. A GLM editor reads the card, the read and the audit and writes ONE concrete edit instruction: the full target end state (the card's atom counts, every carded bond at its carded order, every atom's valence met, the carded ring, no print anywhere) plus the audit's specific failures as extra demands - add the missing white sphere on the oxygen, draw the second parallel rod between the two carbons, remove the rod that pushes a carbon to five - locating each fix by the reader's own description of the sphere. Muse Edit applies those notes to the round-1 picture. The corrected model is the flow's deliverable. A second blind reader re-enumerates it, and the same auditor scores round 2 against the same card, so the fix is itself audited and the reader can see exactly what the edit bought - or cost. The Decide verdict stays, demoted to a side output on round 1.

Why this axis is new: the catalog's nearest graph neighbour is metro-map-audit, and the difference is mechanical. A metro map's edges are unweighted and its audit is station order along two lines plus one interchange - an edge is either there or not. A molecule's edges carry order (a double bond is two rods and spends two valence), and every node carries an arithmetic budget the auditor must sum: a bond can sit in exactly the right place and the molecule still be impossible, a verdict no order, topology or counting audit in the catalog can state. Braille encodes by dot position and red-reveal by colour channel; neither has weighted edges or a per-node conservation law. This is also the failure the chemistry literature keeps measuring in the open - MolLangBench (ICLR 2026) puts even GPT-5 at 43% on molecule generation, and a 2026 Journal of Chemical Education study found under half of basic-prompt chemistry images directly usable - image models draw plausible-looking structures that are not the molecule. Here that gap is not a vibe: it is ATOMS, BONDS and VALENCE, each with the violators named.

Honest limits, in the open: this is connectivity chemistry only - bond angles, 3D geometry, stereochemistry and lone pairs are deliberately not scored, and a flat, valence-correct model is the claim, nothing more. Atoms are identified by sphere colour alone, so a painter that renders an oxygen pinkish or a carbon blue-grey puts the read on a naming border; the read records the colour call it made. Hydrogens are small white spheres and the easiest atoms for both painter and reader to drop or merge - the ATOMS line is where that shows. The auditor's graph work is multiset and per-atom arithmetic, not full graph isomorphism: a rearranged molecule with the same atom counts, the same bond-type multiset, valid valences and the same ring would score exact even if its connectivity differed in a way those four scores cannot see (for this menu - two chains, one branched acid, one ring - that blind spot is narrow, and the NOTE of a run should not upgrade the claim beyond it). The fix round has its own limit: an edit model asked to add or remove one rod can shift a neighbour - that is exactly what round 2's BONDS and VALENCE lines are for, and both audits are printed so the reader can see the trade. One repair round is unrolled, not a loop - if round 2 still violates, the flow shows the violation rather than spending again.

Live proof, 2026-11-19 - SUCCESS end to end on the first attempt, all 16 nodes done, no errors, $0.0336 balance-verified ($33.0598 -> $33.0263, exactly the metered $0.033565: Muse paint $0.011, Muse Edit $0.011, Gemini readers $0.00490/$0.00667, GLM $0.00; Decide unpriced in report, cost_exact=false). The director carded METHANOL and copied its printed spec exactly - CH4O, carbon 1 / hydrogen 4 / oxygen 1, five single bonds, no ring. Round 1 is a clean sweep, and it is pixel-true, not just judge-true: the painted model is a glossy charcoal carbon joined by single rods to three small white hydrogens and one red oxygen, the oxygen carrying the fourth white hydrogen - the blind reader's graph matched the card connection for connection, the auditor scored ATOMS 6 of 6, BONDS 5 of 5, VALENCE VALID, RING MATCH, LABEL LEAK none, and Decide v1.2 returned 'chemically exact'. Two small wobbles, recorded rather than rounded away: the auditor's DECODED FORMULA line printed 'C1H4O1' (it kept the 1s its own spec says to omit) while its Summary decoded CH4O correctly, and the reader wrote its rod lists without spaces ('R3x1'), which the auditor parsed without complaint. Then the honest headline, and it is about the fix round, not the paint: the editor's notes on a perfect audit were exemplary - they restated the verified end state and said in so many words 'there are no failures to fix: add no spheres, remove none, change no bond orders' - and Muse Edit recoloured the model anyway. The corrected picture is geometrically identical (same six spheres, same five rods, same layout, pixel-verified) but three hydrogens came back metallic gold, the oxygen came back blue - nitrogen's CPK colour - and the carbon went black. The blind re-reader honestly refused those colours ('other' x4), and the re-audit scored ATOMS 2 of 6, BONDS 0 of 5, DECODED FORMULA 'C1H1 + other x4', while VALENCE stayed VALID and the ring stayed MATCH - the structure survived and the identity did not, because in this flow colour IS identity. The lesson is written into both audits for any reader: the repair round is a liability on an already-exact asset, and an edit model asked to change nothing still repaints. A future variant should gate the fix on a non-exact round-1 audit; this run shows exactly why, with the no-op instruction quoted in the run's report.
