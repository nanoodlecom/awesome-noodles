# recipe-note
A clock is a picture that encodes a number in two angles at once - and the two angles are not independent. The minute hand's position is the minutes, exactly; the hour hand's position is the hours plus the minutes again, smeared continuously across the span between two numerals. That coupling is what makes a painted clock so often wrong in a way nobody notices at a glance: a minute hand at 37 minutes with the hour hand parked dead on the 4 is a clock that cannot exist, because at 4:37 a real hour hand has already travelled three-fifths of the way to 5. Image models paint clocks from clock-shaped memory - hands at photogenic angles, ten-past-ten symmetry - and the result looks like a clock while telling no particular time, or a time nobody asked for. This flow makes the requested time the contract, in minutes, and then does something about a miss. Give it a poster setting and a GLM director picks one clock from a short menu printed in its own prompt (HARBOUR BELL 7:24, TEATIME 4:37, NIGHT TRAIN 10:08, MARKET OPEN 2:51) and copies it onto a CLOCK CARD: the target time plus both hand positions, each one derived from the time by the dial's own law and stated in plain position words - the minute hand at its exact minute mark, the hour hand at the matching fraction between its numerals.

A second GLM turns the card into a painter prompt that repeats those position words verbatim - short thick hour hand, long thin minute hand, no second hand, no third hand, all twelve numerals, nothing else printed - and Muse paints the poster. Then the flow's central discipline: a blind Gemini reader who never saw the card reads the painted clock the way a person does, naming where each hand points and stating the time the clock shows. A GLM auditor scores that read against the card in the dial's native unit - ERROR MINUTES, the absolute difference between the read time and the target on a 720-minute dial, recomputed by the auditor from the two time lines - plus the hour hand's span and the minute hand's mark judged separately, because the two hands fail differently and the fix differs. A Decide judge headlines round 1: tells the time, minutes drifted, hour hand wrong, hands swapped, or not a clock.

That verdict is not where this flow stops - the loop closes, per the standing rule that a created asset ends in the corrected asset, not in text. A GLM editor reads the card, the blind read and the audit and writes one concrete edit instruction: the full target end state with the card's hand-position words repeated verbatim, plus each failed hand named with the move it needs. The editor is gated in content, a rule this catalog learned the hard way from Molecule Check, whose flawless round-1 model was recoloured by a repair it never needed: on an exact audit (error 0, both hands correct, numerals complete, nothing extra) the editor must demand a verbatim unchanged copy of the poster. Muse Edit moves the hands, a blind re-read reads the corrected clock, and the same auditor re-scores it against the same card, so the fix is itself audited in minutes and the reader can see exactly what the edit bought - or cost. The corrected clock is the flow's deliverable; the Decide verdict stays, demoted to a side output on round 1.

Why this axis is new: the catalog's nearest neighbours are scale-check, which audits static proportions between separate objects, and balance-check, which audits torque from positions and weights. A clock's hands are angles that encode a single coupled quantity - the hour hand's correct position is a function of the minute value, so one hand's error can be diagnosed from the other, and the audit's unit is not a count, a region or a ratio but minutes of time, a unit no earlier check scores in. The failure menu is also new: hands swapped in length is a failure no placement audit can state, and a clock can be internally inconsistent - each hand plausible alone, impossible together - which only a coupled audit catches.

Honest limits, in the open: the auditor judges the reader's reading, not the pixels - a hand drawn near a boundary (the 8-minute mark sits between two numerals) is scored as the reader honestly placed it, and the two blind reads are independent, which is the only guard. The reader reads to the nearest minute it can defend; a painter who lands a hand within a minute or two of the mark may still be read as exact, which is why the auditor's minute-hand tolerance is 2 minutes and the headline figure is the read time's error, not the hand's true angular error. The editor's repair faces the known edit-model limit: asked to move one hand, Muse Edit repaints the whole face, and the hand that was right can drift while the wrong one is fixed - the re-audit exists to catch exactly that trade, and it is reported as read. And the menu's four times keep both hands well separated and off the numerals' exact centres, deliberately: a clock at exactly 3:00 or with both hands overlapping would test eyesight, not time-telling.
