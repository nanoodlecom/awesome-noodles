# recipe-note
A seamless tile is a promise a picture makes to its own edges: repeat me forever and nothing will jar. The promise has two halves. The unit must repeat identically - the same motif, the same elements, the same orientation, copy after copy - and the edges must agree with each other: whatever walks off the right side of the tile must walk back in on the left at exactly the same height, and whatever the top edge cuts in half must continue at the bottom. Image models are fluent at pattern-looking pictures and quietly break both halves: the fourth copy of the motif drifts, a tendril that exits one edge re-enters two centimetres lower, and the "seamless" texture seams the moment anyone actually tiles it. This flow makes the promise a planned, measurable quantity - and then it does something about a violation. Give it a pattern setting and a GLM director picks one tile design from a short menu printed in its own prompt (MIDNIGHT VINE, CITRUS GROVE, WAVE AND GULL, KITE PAPER) - a background, one main motif, exactly three supporting elements with their positions inside the tile, and two named EDGE CROSSINGS, one right-to-left and one top-to-bottom, each with its coordinate stated - and copies it onto a TILE CARD. A second GLM turns the card into one painter prompt for a flat pattern shown as a 2 by 2 repeat of that single tile: four identical copies, same orientation, no rotation, no mirroring, no grid lines, the crossing elements continuing across the centre seams and wrapping at the outer edges. Muse paints it. A blind Gemini reader, who never saw the card, reports each quadrant's motif and elements, whether elements crossing the vertical and horizontal centre seams continue aligned across them, and whether the elements cut at the outer edges WRAP - a right-edge exit matching a left-edge entry at the same height, a top exit matching a bottom entry. A GLM auditor scores COPIES, MOTIF and ELEMENTS against the card, and SEAM V, SEAM H and WRAP against the crossings. A Decide judge headlines round 1: tiles seamlessly, copies drifted, seams broken, wrap broken, or not a pattern.

That verdict is not where this flow stops - the loop closes, per the standing rule that a created asset ends in the corrected asset, not in text. A GLM editor reads the card, the read and the audit and writes ONE concrete edit instruction: the full target end state (the card's design identical in all four quadrants, both crossings continuing across their seams and wrapping at the frame edges) plus the audit's specific failures as extra demands - redraw the bottom-right quadrant's missing star, shift the star cut at the top edge so its lower half at the bottom edge lines up. The editor is gated in content, a rule this catalog learned the hard way from Molecule Check, whose flawless round-1 model was recoloured by a repair it never needed: on a perfect audit the editor must demand a verbatim unchanged copy and forbid anything being moved, added or restyled. Muse Edit applies the notes to the round-1 picture. The corrected pattern is the flow's deliverable. A second blind reader re-inspects it, and the same auditor scores round 2 against the same card, so the fix is itself audited and the reader can see exactly what the edit bought - or cost. The Decide verdict stays, demoted to a side output on round 1.

Why this axis is new: the catalog's nearest neighbours are mirror-check and spot-the-difference, and the difference is mechanical. Mirror-check's second image is a reflection the scene's own optics produce - the painter composes it once and physics copies it; spot-the-difference compares two separate renders for planned changes. Tile check's four copies are all designed, all in one canvas, and the picture is scored against ITSELF, copy against copy - a self-consistency demand no other flow makes. And edge continuity is a law no other audit can state: an element exiting one edge must re-enter the opposite edge at the same coordinate, tested twice - at the internal seams, where tile meets tile, and at the outer wrap, where the pattern would continue past the frame. Four perfect copies can still fail as a tile because their crossings break; a pretty pattern that cannot repeat is exactly the failure a beauty judge passes every time.

Honest limits, in the open: the reader judges seam alignment by eye from the whole picture, not by pixel measurement - a crossing offset by a few pixels can read as CONTINUES, which is why the audit's OFFSET band exists and why small misalignments will be under-reported rather than over-reported. Only translation is tested: rotations and mirror repeats (the other classic wallpaper symmetries) are deliberately out of scope, and a painter that rotates one copy fails COPIES rather than earning a symmetry point. The wrap verdict compresses two edge pairs into one line, so a pattern whose horizontal wrap fails while its vertical wrap holds scores PARTIAL without saying which pair broke - the SEAM lines carry the same information at the internal joins, and between them the failure is locatable. One repair round is unrolled, not a loop - if round 2 still breaks a seam, the flow shows the break rather than spending again.

Live verification (2026-11-20, the run that proved the flow): one live run, SUCCESS end to end, all 18 nodes done, no errors, $0.0436 balance-verified ($31.0577 -> $31.0141, exactly the metered sum; Muse paint $0.011, Muse Edit $0.011, Gemini readers $0.01346/$0.00812, GLM $0.00; Decide unpriced in the report, cost_exact=false). The director carded MIDNIGHT VINE and copied it exactly. Round 1 is visually verified as a genuinely handsome pattern: deep teal, four identical cream moonflowers, a curling vine running across the top of every tile and exiting both side edges, small gold stars, and one large gold star cut in half at the top centre continuing at the bottom centre - the top-bottom wrap is right there on the pixels. The audit scored it strictly: COPIES 0 of 4, MOTIF 0 of 4, ELEMENTS 2 of 3, SEAM V CONTINUES, SEAM H BREAKS, WRAP PARTIAL, Decide 'seams broken'. Two of the lost points are reader/auditor strictness, recorded rather than hidden: the carded pair of sage leaves IS present in every quadrant as the flower's own stem leaves, but the blind reader never named them as a separate pair, and the flowers sit centre-low in their quadrants against the carded centre-left, which the auditor scored as a position miss in all four. The SEAM H break is real: nothing crosses the horizontal centre line - the vine repeats inside each tile instead of crossing the seam. The editor aimed at exactly the scored failures (add the leaf pair everywhere, centre-left flowers, a star crossing the horizontal seam, complete wraps), and round 2 shows the trade this catalog keeps meeting: the fix landed a large faceted gold star dead centre, crossing both seams (re-audit SEAM H: CONTINUES, up from BREAKS), but it cost the edges - the top/bottom star halves vanished, the small stars thinned out (gone entirely from the bottom-left quadrant), a vertical vine border grew down the right side, and WRAP fell to BROKEN, all pixel-verified; the re-audit also renamed the unchanged cream bloom a 'peony' and held MOTIF at 0 of 4 on the name, a naming call recorded here - on pixels the flower is the round-1 bloom, unmoved and unrecoloured. The re-audit caught the fix round's regressions exactly as designed; the flow shows the trade rather than spending again.
