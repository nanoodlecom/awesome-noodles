# recipe-note
Fold a cross of six squares into a cube and geometry writes the exam: in a cross net - a CENTRE square, UP, DOWN, LEFT and RIGHT squares on its four edges, and a TAIL square hanging below DOWN - the faces that end up opposite each other are fixed by position alone. UP lands opposite DOWN, LEFT opposite RIGHT, CENTRE opposite TAIL, and opposite faces can never be visible in the same view, so any view of one corner shows exactly three faces, one from each opposite pair. Nobody has to guess the pairs, but an image model does not fold: it decorates. Left alone it shows a cube wearing whichever motifs look good together - a sun beside the moon it should be hiding, the same motif twice, a fourth face peeking round the edge. This flow makes the fold a derived, measurable quantity - and then it does something about a violation. Give it a plain backdrop and a GLM director picks one cube from a menu printed in its own prompt (HARBOUR, GARDEN, WORKSHOP, ARCTIC - six distinct picture motifs on the same cross layout) and copies it onto a CUBE CARD: the motif at each net position, the three opposite pairs those positions force, the corner the cube is viewed from named only in net positions, and the FRONT, TOP and RIGHT motifs that corner forces. Every carded view was verified against the cube's geometry in code before being put on the menu: each view holds exactly one face from each opposite pair, and a real rotation of the folded cube carries its front, top and right faces to the carded positions (checked with the cross-product rule for right-handed frames), including the two flipped cubes - WORKSHOP viewed from its tail corner and ARCTIC with its DOWN face on top - where no upright-cube stereotype can substitute for folding. A second GLM turns the card into a painter prompt - the net stated in full, position by position, because the net is data, and the folding law stated once and abstractly (faces across the centre become opposites and can never be seen together; exactly the three faces meeting at a corner can be seen together) - and is forbidden to state which motif lands on the front, top or right of the folded cube: the painter must fold the net itself, which is exactly where the signature failure lives, because a painter that decorates instead of folding shows opposite faces together. Muse paints one wide picture: the flat net on the left, the folded cube on the right. A blind Gemini reader, who never saw the card, reports the motif at each of the six net positions and the motif on each of the cube's three visible faces. A GLM auditor re-derives the opposite pairs from the net positions itself and scores NET (all six positions), VIEW SET (the cube shows exactly the three faces that meet at the carded corner), POSITIONS (front, top and right each the forced motif), OPPOSITES (no opposite pair co-visible - an impossibility axis, not a preference) and FACES (exactly three). A ⚖️ Decide judge (PPLX Decider) headlines round 1 as a side output - **folds true**, **opposites together**, **wrong corner**, **net misdrawn**, or **not a net and cube** - but the deliverable is the corrected picture.

That verdict is not where this flow stops - the loop closes, per the standing rule that a created asset ends in the corrected asset, not in text. A GLM editor reads the card, the read and the audit and writes ONE concrete edit instruction: the full target end state (the net's six positions named with their motifs, the cube's front, top and right named with theirs) plus the audit's specific failures as motif-repaint demands - repaint the cube's top face, which shows the MOON that sits opposite the visible STAR and can never share a view with it; swap the two net motifs the painter exchanged. The editor is gated in content, a rule this catalog learned the hard way from Molecule Check, whose flawless round-1 model was recoloured by a repair it never needed: on a perfect audit the editor must demand a verbatim unchanged copy and forbid anything being repainted or moved, and whichever half - net or cube - passed completely must be left exactly as it is. Muse Edit applies the notes to the round-1 picture. The corrected picture is the flow's deliverable. A second blind reader re-inspects it, and the same auditor scores round 2 against the same card, so the fix is itself audited and the reader can see exactly what the edit bought - or cost. The Decide verdict stays, demoted to a side output on round 1.

Why this axis is new: the catalog's nearest neighbours are turnaround-check, exploded-check and circuit-check, and the differences are mechanical. In turnaround-check a second view must be the same objects seen from behind - identity across a viewpoint change, scored object by object; here the second view is not the same arrangement from elsewhere but a different mathematical object, a 3D solid folded from the 2D diagram beside it, and the scoring relation is derivability: every visible face is forced by the net, and one whole class of answer - any opposite pair on show - is not merely wrong but geometrically impossible. In exploded-check parts must separate along one axis in assembly order; here nothing separates - the constraint is co-visibility, a negative relation between faces that never touch in the drawing of the cube yet are locked together by the net. In circuit-check per-bulb states are derived from drawn wiring topology in one flat picture; here the topology lives in a flat net but the derived quantity only exists in the folded solid, in a second region of the picture, and the auditor's ground truth (the opposite pairs) is re-derived from positions in the first region. The prey is also its own: the thematic pair shown together (the painter pairs motifs by meaning, and meaning is exactly what opposites ignore), the duplicated motif, and the fourth face. Not the closed plan, render and recount vein: nothing is placed in a zone and no objects are counted into existence - a fold is derived, produced, read blind and repaired.

Honest limits, in the open: the audit scores which motifs are visible and where, not the cube's perspective geometry - a cube drawn with wobbly edges but the right three faces in the right positions passes, because motif identity is the vocabulary a blind reader can report reliably. Motif matching is by depicted object, ignoring colour words, so a reader that calls the crescent moon 'moon' and the auditor's card 'MOON' agree; a motif drawn so ambiguously that the reader names a different object altogether scores as a wrong face, which is strict but honest - an unreadable face has not been folded into the picture in any usable sense. The view is always a three-quarter view from slightly above of one corner; side and bottom faces are never asked for, so the flow proves corner folds, not arbitrary orientations. And an edit round that repaints one cube face can drift a neighbour or the net beside it; the re-audit's NET and POSITIONS axes are what catch that, and a repair that redecorates instead of correcting will show up there. Both GLM calls that carry the card are capped (maxTokens 900/1200, reasoningEffort low, the pairing this catalog's repair history landed on) so a detailed card cannot run a generation past the HTTP timeout.
