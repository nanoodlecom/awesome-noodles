# recipe-note
Cake-decorating tools do one trick: type a theme, watch a render of *a* decorated cake — and the classic failures are that the piping lands in the wrong place, the rosette ring has eleven rosettes where you pictured eight, a "Happy Birthday" scrawl appears when you never asked for lettering, or the "same cake, decorated" is quietly a taller, pinker, different cake on a different plate, and nothing in the tool ever counts the render against the plan or checks it against the original cake. This graph splits the jobs across four model families and closes both loops: a Gemini vision node is the cake clerk (it writes a cake card — exactly 5 numbered zones in a fixed order, top surface centre to plate and setting, each with what is currently in it, a Fixed line for the cake's shape, tiers, size, smooth base frosting colour, plate, angle, background and lighting that must NOT change, and a decoration-now line as the baseline that may be added to), a GLM node changes hats into a decorator (it plans ONE scheme for your brief citing those zone numbers — at least 3 zones get new decoration, zone 5, the plate and setting, always stays KEEP, every zone is accounted for exactly once, every countable item carries an exact count between 3 and 12, and the brief's hard constraints, no lettering and no candles in the shipped brief, win over the decorator's taste), a second GLM compresses the plan into a single surgical edit instruction that starts with the keep-fixed list and repeats every count verbatim, and Muse Edit decorates the original photo itself, not a text description of a cake.

Then the render has to answer for itself, twice. A second Gemini vision node reads the render back and inventories only the decoration actually visible in it, zone by zone, counting every countable item as it goes — it never sees the card or the plan, so it cannot grade generously — ending with its own Fixed line. A GLM decoration auditor runs two passes over the card, the plan and that read-back: pass 1 scores every planned decoration line APPLIED / WRONG COUNT / WRONG ZONE / MISSING (a countable item only counts as applied when the read-back count equals the planned count exactly, and a zone the plan marks KEEP counts as applied only when the read-back still shows it unchanged), and pass 2 compares the Fixed lines, ending in an "X of Y planned zones applied, counts A of B exact, fixed list kept/changed" summary. And a ⚖️ Decide judge (PPLX Decider) gives the headline verdict from the card plus plan plus the render image: **faithfully decorated**, **counts drifted**, **barely decorated**, or **different cake**. The graph exports the **Decoration — audited** verdict text, and the render itself rides through the Decide node as its image output — open the Decoration node to see the cake the verdict is about.

Why the split matters: the model that decorated the cake is not the model that wrote the cake card, and neither of them grades the result. The card is written *before* any piping is chosen, so the decorator cannot invent a sixth zone to hide a miss in, and the zone numbers plus the exact counts make both cheats — a decorator planning work for zones that do not exist, and a renderer piping the right rosettes in the wrong number or the wrong ring — mechanically checkable rather than vibes. Counting is the sharp end: image models are notoriously loose with "eight of something", and this graph is built to catch exactly that, in the open, instead of letting a pretty render imply precision it never had.

Honest limits, in the open: the cake card is a photo read, not ground truth — the clerk can misjudge a frosting finish (smooth vs satin is a judgement call at bakery-photo distance), and every downstream check then faithfully defends the wrong card, so read the card before you trust the audit. Counting small repeated items from a three-quarter photo is genuinely hard: rosettes at the back of the ring are partly occluded, pearl dots merge, and the read-back's "( ? )" guesses flow straight into the score — a WRONG COUNT can be a reader's miscount rather than a renderer's mis-pipe, and the audit cannot tell the two apart. Muse Edit re-renders rather than repaints pixels, so frosting texture, shadows and the plate can drift even when every zone lands — pass 2 and the Decide judge watch for exactly that, but they watch at read-back resolution. And a rendered decoration is a visual preview, not a piping plan: real buttercream behaviour, food safety and whether those berries exist in your kitchen are between you and a bakery. A generated staging photo was used for the live proof below — no real cake photo was staged in this workspace — disclosed here rather than smoothed over.

Expected cost per run: Muse Edit $0.011, the two Gemini vision reads roughly $0.005–0.007 each, the three GLM calls effectively $0.00–0.011 combined, and the Decide audit is not priced in the 2026-09-12 catalog snapshot — expect roughly $0.03–0.05 all in. Prices are observations, not caps.

Live proof, 2026-11-09: the full chain ran end to end for $0.0314 balance-verified across two spends ($30.7529 → $30.7215, exactly matching the metered sum) — a generated staging photo of a plain smooth ivory round cake ($0.0110 exact, Muse; no real cake photo was staged, as disclosed above), then ONE live run of the graph ($0.0204 metered: cake card $0.00406, Muse Edit $0.011, read-back $0.00529, GLM calls $0.00; Decide unpriced in the report). The clerk's card was exact against the staging cake — round single-tier, smooth ivory, all five zones bare, decoration: none. The decorator planned "Pastel Garden Posy & Shell Border": a top-centre cluster of 6 edible violas, 5 raspberries and 8 piped sage leaves, a continuous blush-pink shell border on the top edge ring, 5 butter-yellow blossoms on the side wall, a sage bead border at the base, plate KEEP. Muse Edit's render is visually verified: the same cake, plate, table and angle, beautifully decorated, no lettering, no candles. And the counting audit caught exactly the failure this graph exists to catch: the read-back counted 7 violas (planned 6), 6 raspberries (planned 5) and 9 side blossoms (planned 5), with the leaves given no count at all — so the auditor scored 3 of 5 zones applied, counts 0 of 4 exact, fixed list KEPT, and Decide agreed with 'counts drifted'. A prettier render would have implied a precision the render never had; here the drift is measured, not smoothed over.
