# recipe-note
Every other audio audit in this catalog runs through Whisper, and Whisper can only judge words. This flow audits music itself. Muse paints a still from your scene brief, and a Gemini sound scout reads it into a SOUND CARD: a SETTING line, then five axes in a closed vocabulary fixed before any music exists — MOOD (one of eight words), ENERGY (low, medium, high), PACE (slow, moderate, fast), FAMILY (the one instrument family that should lead: solo piano, strings, brass, folk acoustic, electronic, percussion-led, choir), and ARC (steady, builds, fades, swells and settles). A GLM composer turns the card into a single instrumental prompt, and ElevenLabs Music composes a 20-second cue from it — the cue is the final artifact, never muxed onto the picture, so the soundtrack node's known duration trap never gets a chance to cut anything. Then a Qwen omni model LISTENS to the finished cue through the LLM node's audio input — the `llm` audio port, which no other catalog graph uses. The listener is blind by construction: it receives the audio and an instruction, never the picture, the card or the prompt, and it writes a LISTENING REPORT in the same five axes plus a HEARD line naming the instruments actually present. A GLM sound auditor scores the report against the card axis by axis — MATCH, NEAR (a close cousin: an adjacent energy step, an overlapping family) or OPPOSITE — checks whether the card's family is among the instruments heard, and ends in "X of 5 axes heard". A ⚖️ Decide judge (PPLX Decider) sees the still, the card and the report and gives the headline verdict: **sounds like the picture**, **partly sounds like it**, **wrong mood**, or **silence or noise**.

Why the loop matters: asking a music model for "the sound of this picture" is a cross-modal leap that text prompts usually grade by vibes, because nobody in the chain ever hears the result. Here the ground truth is written down before the leap (the card), the landing is reported by an independent listener in the same words (the report), and the score is the distance between them — so a miss is attributable instead of mysterious: the card misread the picture, the composer misread the card, the music model ignored the prompt, or the listener misheard the cue. The nearest catalog neighbour, a composer that watches your film, writes a timed brief from frames and lays two scores on the clip but never listens back — its music is judged by nobody, and its job is synchronisation to timed picture events. This flow's music stands alone and its job is evocation: does the sound of the cue match the sound the still was asking for?

Honest limits, in the open: the listener is a language model with ears, not a spectrum analyser — it reports tempo and instrumentation as impressions in a coarse vocabulary, and two adjacent words (tense vs dramatic, moderate vs fast) are judgement calls the NEAR grade exists to absorb. Twenty seconds is enough for mood, pace and family, and barely enough for an arc; a "builds" that needs a minute will read as "steady" and be scored honestly as a miss. The card is read from the rendered still, so if Muse paints a calmer sea than the brief described, the card honestly cards the calmer sea and the cue is graded against the picture that exists. And the axes are a deliberate simplification: a cue can match on all five and still be a dull piece of music — the audit certifies evocation, not quality.

First live attempt, 2026-11-13 — FAILED at the listener, repaired the same session. The card and composer worked exactly as designed (the card read the storm still as dramatic / high / fast / strings / swells-and-settles, and ElevenLabs composed the cue for $0.20), but the original listener, Gemini 3.8 Flash, rejected the audio outright: 400 "No available route for this model supports audio input" — its catalog entry lists audio as an input modality, but no NanoGPT route for it accepts an `input_audio` part. Isolated probes on the actual failed cue (all $0.00 except the last, $0.00004) showed Gemini 3 Flash Preview has the same gap ("No permitted provider supports this model and media input"), while Qwen3.8 Omni Flash — a dedicated omni model — heard the cue and described it correctly in one line ("dramatic and tense, fast tempo, strings and brass"), once its unsupported `reasoningEffort: low` field was removed (it accepts only none/high). The listener is therefore Qwen3.8 Omni Flash in the shipped graph. One verification run of the repaired graph is owed; the flow is not live-proven until it lands.

Expected cost per run: the ElevenLabs Music cue dominates — $0.01 per second, $0.20 for 20 seconds — plus the Muse still at $0.011, the Gemini card and listening report at roughly a cent or two together, the GLM calls at effectively $0.00, and the Decide audit unpriced in the run report; roughly $0.24 all in. Prices are observations, not caps.

Change the **Scene brief** to any scene with a strong sonic character — weather, water, crowds, machinery, celebration, grief — anything where a stranger could hum what the picture should sound like. Calm, featureless scenes card to low/slow/steady and give the listener nothing to get wrong, which is exactly why the default is a storm.
