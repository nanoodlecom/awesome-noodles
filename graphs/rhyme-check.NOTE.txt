# recipe-note
A poem can say all the right things and still not be a poem. What makes a four-line children's verse a verse is form: the line endings chime in pairs, and every line lands on the same beat count. Language models write verse that feels like this and quietly is not - the second line ends near the rhyme instead of on it, one line runs to eleven syllables while its neighbours hold eight, and nobody notices until the poem is read aloud, which is exactly where form lives. This flow makes the form a planned, measurable quantity on two channels at once - and then it does something about a miss. Give it a poem setting and a GLM director picks one spec from a short menu printed in its own prompt (LIGHTHOUSE CAT, MARKET GARDENER, LITTLE TRAIN, WINTER FOX) - every spec scheme AABB, exactly 8 syllables per line, and four carded line-ending words, the two rhyme pairs spelled out - and copies it onto a POEM CARD. A GLM poet writes exactly four lines on the theme, each exactly 8 syllables, each ending with its carded word. MiniMax Speech reads the poem aloud, and Whisper transcribes the reading blind, never having seen the poem. A GLM auditor then scores FORM, the written poem against the card (END WORDS in place, METER counted syllable by syllable, line by line), and EAR, the blind transcript against the poem (the four end words heard in order, WORDS KEPT through the voice). A Decide judge headlines round 1: rhymes true, rhyme slipped, meter broke, voice garbled it, or not a poem.

That verdict is not where this flow stops - the loop closes, per the standing rule that a created asset ends in the corrected asset, not in text. A GLM editor reads the card, the poem, the transcript and the audit and outputs the corrected poem itself: only the failing lines are rewritten, each back to its carded end word at exactly 8 syllables, and a line that passed FORM but failed EAR - written right, heard wrong - is rewritten in simpler, clearer words around the same end word, because the fix for a mumbling channel is plainer diction, not a new rhyme. The editor is gated in content, a rule this catalog learned the hard way from Molecule Check, whose flawless round-1 model was recoloured by a repair it never needed: on a perfect audit the editor must reproduce the poem verbatim, line for line. MiniMax Speech re-reads the corrected poem, Whisper re-transcribes it blind, and the same auditor re-scores both channels against the same card, so the fix is itself audited and the reader can see exactly what the edit bought - or cost. The corrected spoken poem is the flow's deliverable; the Decide verdict stays, demoted to a side output on round 1.

Why this axis is new: the catalog's nearest neighbours are say-it-back, which audits a spoken announcement for FACT survival through the same speech-to-transcript loop, and cover-version, which audits a song for lyric IDENTITY through a music cover. Neither can state this flow's failure. A poem can keep every fact and every word's topic and still fail here twice over - an end word swapped for a near rhyme (identity kept, form broken) and a line swollen to eleven syllables (content kept, meter broken) - because the audited object is formal structure: which endings must sound alike, in AABB pairing, and how many beats each line may hold. The two-channel audit is the second mechanical difference: say-it-back's single channel cannot distinguish a script that was written wrong from a script that was merely heard wrong, while this auditor's FORM and EAR scores can, and the editor's repair depends on the distinction. And the repair round re-creates in the audited modality itself - a re-spoken poem, re-heard and re-scored - rather than repainting a picture.

Honest limits, in the open: syllable counting is the auditor's judgment, not a phonetic dictionary - GLM counts vowel sounds by the rules printed in its prompt, and a genuinely ambiguous word (fire, hour, poem itself) can be counted either way by poet and auditor; a one-line METER miss on such a word is a counting call, recorded as read, not a certain fact about the poem. Whisper's transcript is likewise a hearing, not ground truth: it writes what the reading sounded like, so an EAR failure can be the voice's diction, the listener's ear, or both, and the audit cannot separate them - the editor's plain-diction rewrite is the pragmatic answer either way. The rhyme test is deliberately strict: only the card's exact end words score, so a poet who lands a prettier true rhyme of its own scores a miss - the card is the contract. And the flow audits the poem it carded, not poetry in general: enjambment, imagery and whether the verse is any good are left, deliberately, to the human listener.
