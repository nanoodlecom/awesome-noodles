# recipe-note
A Venn diagram is a logic theorem drawn as two circles: the left circle is the set of RED things, the right circle is the set of ROUND things, and where an object belongs is not a matter of taste - it is derived. Red and round belongs in the overlap. Red and not round belongs in the left circle only. Not red and round belongs in the right circle only. And an object that is neither red nor round belongs in no circle at all: it must sit outside both, on the background, in the region most picture-makers forget exists. Image models draw handsome Venn posters that obey none of this - blue squares drift into the red circle, round things pile into the overlap whatever their colour, and the outside region stays empty because everything got stuffed into a circle. This flow makes the logic a planned, measurable quantity - and then it does something about a violation. Give it a poster setting and a GLM director picks one set from a short menu printed in its own prompt (BUTTONS AND BLOCKS, PICNIC, WORKSHOP, SEASIDE) - exactly six simple flat icon objects, each carded with a colour, RED or BLUE, and a shape, ROUND, SQUARE or TRIANGULAR - and copies it onto a VENN CARD with each object's region derived by the law. A second GLM turns the card into one flat-poster prompt, naming every object with its colour, its shape and its region: two large overlapping circles labelled RED and ROUND, one icon wholly inside each derived region. Muse paints it. A blind Gemini reader, who never saw the card, reports every object it can see with its colour, its shape and the region it sits in. A GLM auditor recomputes every expected region from the attributes alone and scores CARD LAW, OBJECTS, ATTRIBUTES and REGIONS. A Decide judge headlines round 1: logic holds, regions slipped, attributes flipped, objects missing, or not a venn.

That verdict is not where this flow stops - the loop closes, per the standing rule that a created asset ends in the corrected asset, not in text. A GLM editor reads the card, the read and the audit and writes ONE concrete edit instruction: the full target end state (the card's six objects in their carded colours and shapes, each wholly in its derived region) plus the audit's specific failures as object-level demands - move the blue square tile wholly outside both circles onto the background. The editor is gated in content, a rule this catalog learned the hard way from Molecule Check, whose flawless round-1 model was recoloured by a repair it never needed: on a perfect audit the editor must demand a verbatim unchanged copy and forbid anything being moved, added, recoloured or reshaped. Muse Edit applies the notes to the round-1 picture. The corrected poster is the flow's deliverable. A second blind reader re-inspects it, and the same auditor scores round 2 against the same card, so the fix is itself audited and the reader can see exactly what the edit bought - or cost. The Decide verdict stays, demoted to a side output on round 1.

Why this axis is new: the catalog's nearest neighbour is floor-plan-audit, and the difference is mechanical. In floor-plan, an object's zone is the carded fact itself - the auditor compares the read zone against the carded zone and computes nothing. Here no region is a free carded fact: each region is a theorem derived from two independent attribute channels, colour and shape, under AND, AND-NOT, NOT-AND and NOR. The auditor must recompute the theorem object by object, and it can convict the card itself - the CARD LAW axis checks the director's printed regions against the law before any pixel is scored, an axis no placement flow has. The NEITHER case demands a negative placement, an object that must sit outside both circles, which no zone audit can express, because every zone in a floor plan is a positive place to be. And a painter that sorts by only one channel - all the red things on the left, say - fails exactly half the logic in a shape no placement audit can state: its picture looks sorted and is logically wrong in the overlap and the outside at once.

Honest limits, in the open: the reader judges region by where the whole object sits - an icon that straddles a circle outline is reported STRADDLING and never scores as a match, so a poster whose icons merely touch the outlines bleeds region points even when a human can see the intent. Colour is deliberately binary (RED against BLUE) and shape ternary, because finer palettes turn the attribute channels into naming arguments instead of logic; the flow tests Boolean membership, not colour vocabulary. The two circle labels, RED and ROUND, are printed on purpose - they are the set names that define the theorem, not the audited code; the audited code is the objects' placement, which no label gives away. One repair round is unrolled, not a loop - if round 2 still slips a region, the flow shows the slip rather than spending again.

Live verification (2026-11-21, the run that proved the flow): one live run, SUCCESS end to end, all 18 nodes done, no errors, $0.0247 balance-verified ($30.9511 -> $30.9264, exactly the metered sum; Muse paint $0.011, Muse Edit $0.011, Gemini readers $0.00133/$0.00135, GLM $0.00; Decide unpriced in the report, cost_exact=false). The director carded SEASIDE and copied it exactly, and the auditor's new axis fired clean: CARD LAW VALID - every printed region equals the region the law derives from its own colour and shape lines. Round 1 was a near-clean sweep on the flow's first outing: OBJECTS 6 of 6, REGIONS 6 of 6, REGION ERRORS none, EXTRA none, Decide 'logic holds'. Both posters are pixel-verified: two large circles labelled RED and ROUND on cream, a red triangle and a red square in the left circle only, a blue ring and a blue disc in the right circle only, the blue square ice box wholly outside both circles at the bottom right, and the red buoy in the overlap - the NEITHER region populated exactly as the law demands, the case this flow exists to test. The single lost point is ATTRIBUTES 5 of 6, and it is a reader strictness call, recorded honestly: the buoy's body is a plain red disc on the pixels, but its small top fitting made the blind reader report its shape as OTHER ('circle with bump') rather than ROUND in round 1. The editor aimed at exactly that call, demanding a proper round buoy with a domed top and a short stem; Muse Edit delivered a larger, unmistakably buoy-shaped red disc in the same overlap, every other object and region untouched - and the re-reader still scored its shape OTHER ('circle with tabs'), so the re-audit stands at OBJECTS 6 of 6, ATTRIBUTES 5 of 6, REGIONS 6 of 6. The repair round is thus proven to hold the logic perfectly while the one contested point is shown to be a naming judgement about a buoy's fittings, not a placement or colour failure - the flow shows the disagreement rather than spending again.
