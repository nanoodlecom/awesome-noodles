# recipe-note
Every other video flow in this catalog either generates a clip from text or analyses a clip somebody uploaded. This one directs a performance: Muse paints a still from your scene brief, a Gemini stage manager reads that still as rendered and writes an ACTION CARD — the ACTOR (who they are, where they stand, what they hold), the STAGE, and every STATIC that must not move, change or vanish, each with its count — and your motion brief names the ONE action the actor must perform. The card and the brief meet in a GLM director, who writes a single locked-camera motion prompt for the image-to-video model: the action described concretely, every static named as staying put. MiniMax H3 then animates the actual still — the `ivideo` node, which no other catalog graph uses. Five frames are sampled from the finished clip and a second Gemini compares the first frame against the last: what the actor is doing at each end, what MOVED, what is STILL THERE with counts. A GLM motion auditor scores the performance against the card and the brief — the action PERFORMED, PARTIAL or NOT PERFORMED, each static KEPT, MOVED or LOST, INVENTED lines for anything new, ending in "statics X of Y kept". A ⚖️ Decide judge (PPLX Decider) sees the card, the brief and the clip's last frame and gives the headline verdict: **performed**, **partly performed**, **nothing moved**, or **scene changed**.

Why the loop matters: an image-to-video model's real contract is double — do the one thing asked, and do nothing else. Its classic failures are exactly the second half: a bystander morphs, a prop vanishes, the sleeping cat wakes up and walks off, the camera drifts. Watching the clip cannot separate those from the performance, because everything on screen is moving pixels; the audit can, because the card freezes the still's truth before any motion exists and the read-back is a first-vs-last comparison, not an impression of the whole clip. The nearest catalog neighbour, clip-to-comic, samples frames from an uploaded real clip and checks that its event order survived being flattened into a still comic page; here nothing is flattened and nothing is uploaded — the clip itself is the artifact under audit, and the score is change against stasis across time, an axis no earlier flow scores.

Honest limits, in the open: the read-back sees five sampled frames, not the motion itself — a mid-clip excursion that returns by the last frame (the cat stretches at second 2 and resettles) is invisible to it, and "the water flows for the whole clip" is graded from its presence at the ends, not its continuity. The Decide judge sees only the last frame, so it grades the end state; the auditor's text score, built on the reader's first-vs-last report, is the motion evidence. The card is read from the rendered still, so if Muse paints 4 sunflowers for a brief that asked for 5, the card honestly says 4 and the performance is graded against the still that exists, not the brief that was written. And one directed action at 480p for 5 seconds is the deliberate scope: a second action, a moving camera or a longer clip multiplies the ways to fail faster than the audit can attribute them.

Expected cost per run: the MiniMax H3 image-to-video call dominates — 480p at the $0.05-per-second quote rate, $0.25 for 5 seconds — plus the Muse still at $0.011, two Gemini reads at roughly a cent together, the GLM calls at effectively $0.00, and the Decide audit unpriced in the run report; roughly $0.28 all in. Prices are observations, not caps.

Change the **Scene brief** and the **Motion brief** together: the scene must contain one actor mid-pose for a single performable action and a few countable statics worth guarding, and the brief should name exactly that action and nothing else. A pour, a wave, a lid lifting, a flag unfurling — one verb, visibly different between the first frame and the last.

Live proof, 2026-11-13 — one live run, SUCCESS end to end, all nodes done, no errors (metered $0.2705: Muse still $0.011, Gemini card $0.0051, the MiniMax H3 performance $0.25 — exactly the $0.05-per-second quote rate — Gemini read-back $0.0044, GLM calls $0.00, Decide unpriced in the report). The still matched the brief — gardener with the can raised and no water flowing yet, exactly 3 terracotta pots, exactly 5 sunflowers, the tabby cat asleep on the bench — and the card read it back faithfully, splitting the bench and the cat into separate statics (5 in all). The performance is real and visually verified: the last frame shows a clear stream pouring from the can, same framing, same gardener, cat still asleep. Two honest catches, both surfaced by the chain itself: the stream lands in the MIDDLE pot, not the first pot the brief named — a targeting drift the reader caught in its own LAST line ("streaming water into the middle terracotta pot") while both headline judges still landed on performed — and the GLM auditor scored the pots MOVED and flagged the wet ground patches as INVENTED, a true consequence of the directed action scored as an invention; its summary stands as printed: action PERFORMED, statics 4 of 5 kept, with the sunflowers, bench, cat and fence all KEPT.
