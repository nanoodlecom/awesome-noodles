# recipe-note
Tattoo-preview tools do one trick: pick a design, watch a render of *an* arm wearing it — and the classic failures are that the tattoo lands a hand's breadth from where you pointed, runs across the arm instead of along it, comes out twice the size you pictured, grows a second design you never asked for, or the "same arm, previewed" is quietly a different arm with different skin and your freckle gone, and nothing in the tool ever measures the render against the placement or checks it against the original skin. This graph splits the jobs across four model families and closes both loops: a Gemini vision node is the skin clerk (it writes a skin card — exactly 5 numbered zones in a fixed order, inner wrist to hand and fingers, each with what is currently on the skin, a Fixed line for the arm, pose, skin tone and texture, every existing freckle, mole and scar in its place, sleeve, background and lighting that must NOT change, and a tattoos-now line as the baseline that may be added to), a GLM node changes hats into a tattoo artist (it plans ONE placement for your brief citing those zone numbers — exactly one zone gets the tattoo and the other four stay KEEP, the tattoo line states its orientation relative to the arm's long axis, its approximate length as a percent of the zone, and exact counts between 3 and 8 for at least two countable elements, and the brief's hard constraints, black ink only, no lettering and no second tattoo in the shipped brief, win over the artist's taste), a second GLM compresses the plan into a single surgical edit instruction that starts with the keep-fixed list and repeats the zone, orientation and every count verbatim, and Muse Edit tattoos the original photo itself, not a text description of an arm.

Then the render has to answer for itself, twice. A second Gemini vision node reads the render back and inventories only what is actually visible in it, zone by zone — which zone carries a tattoo, its motif, orientation and approximate length, counting every countable element as it goes — it never sees the card or the plan, so it cannot grade generously — ending with its own Fixed line. A GLM placement auditor runs two passes over the card, the plan and that read-back: pass 1 scores every planned placement line APPLIED / WRONG ZONE / WRONG COUNT / WRONG FIT / MISSING (the tattoo only counts as applied in the right zone, at the planned orientation and roughly the planned length, with every read-back count equal to its planned count exactly, and a zone the plan marks KEEP counts as applied only when the read-back still shows it bare), and pass 2 compares the Fixed lines, ending in an "X of Y planned zones applied, counts A of B exact, fixed list kept/changed" summary. And a ⚖️ Decide judge (PPLX Decider) gives the headline verdict from the card plus plan plus the render image: **faithfully placed**, **placement drifted**, **barely tattooed**, or **different arm**. The graph exports the **Placement — audited** verdict text, and the render itself rides through the Decide node as its image output — open the Placement node to see the arm the verdict is about.

Why the split matters: the model that tattooed the arm is not the model that wrote the skin card, and neither of them grades the result. The card is written *before* any placement is chosen, so the artist cannot invent a sixth zone to hide a miss in, and the zone numbers plus the orientation, length and exact counts make the cheats — a renderer inking the right sprig in the wrong zone, across the arm, at twice the size, or with nine leaves where six were planned — mechanically checkable rather than vibes. Placement is the sharp end: a tattoo is permanent, its preview is the one moment a drift can still be caught for free, and this graph is built to catch it in the open instead of letting a pretty render imply a precision it never had.

Honest limits, in the open: the skin card is a photo read, not ground truth — the clerk can misplace a freckle by a zone or miss a faint scar, and every downstream check then faithfully defends the wrong card, so read the card before you trust the audit. Judging a tattoo's length as a percent of a zone from a photo is approximate, skin curves, and the read-back's "( ? )" guesses flow straight into the score — a WRONG FIT can be a reader's misjudgement rather than a renderer's misplacement, and the audit cannot tell the two apart. Muse Edit re-renders rather than repaints pixels, so skin texture, the sleeve and the light can drift even when the placement lands — pass 2 and the Decide judge watch for exactly that, but they watch at read-back resolution. And a rendered tattoo is a visual preview, not a stencil: how fine linework ages, spreads and heals on real skin is between you and a tattooist. A generated staging photo was used for the live proof below — no real forearm photo was staged in this workspace — disclosed here rather than smoothed over.

Expected cost per run: Muse Edit $0.011, the two Gemini vision reads roughly $0.005–0.007 each, the three GLM calls effectively $0.00–0.011 combined, and the Decide audit is not priced in the 2026-09-12 catalog snapshot — expect roughly $0.03–0.05 all in. Prices are observations, not caps.

Live proof, 2026-11-09: the full chain ran end to end for $0.0391 balance-verified across two spends ($30.7215 → $30.6824, exactly matching the metered sum) — a generated staging photo of a bare inner forearm palm-up on a wood table, sage sleeve at the elbow ($0.0110 exact, Muse; no real forearm photo was staged, as disclosed above), then ONE live run of the graph ($0.0281 metered: skin card $0.00865, Muse Edit $0.011, read-back $0.00846, GLM calls $0.00; Decide unpriced in the report). The clerk's card was exact against the staging arm — all five zones bare, tattoos: none. The artist planned a "Fine-line olive sprig" for zone 2, the mid inner forearm only: stem along the arm's long axis pointing toward the elbow, about 35 percent of the zone's length, exactly 7 leaves and 4 berries, every other zone KEEP. Muse Edit's render is visually verified: the same arm, pose, sleeve, table and light, with exactly that sprig in exactly that zone and no second tattoo. The judges honestly split: Decide said 'faithfully placed', while the placement auditor scored 4 of 5 zones applied and counts 0 of 2 exact — the read-back counted 9 leaves (?) against the planned 7 and 3 berries against the planned 4 (on the pixels the sprig carries roughly ten small leaves and four berry dots, so the drift is real in the leaves and arguable in the berries) — and flagged the fixed list CHANGED on wording-level items the pixels do not support: it called the arm 'right' where the card said 'left', and sage-green 'olive-green'. The placement, orientation and zone all landed; the element counts drifted, which is exactly the failure this graph exists to measure rather than smooth over.
