# recipe-note
Every other flow in this catalog starts from words or pictures. This one starts from sound. A SOUND SCRIPT — four sound events in a fixed order, a rooster crowing, a church bell tolling, rain beginning, a steam train passing — is performed by ElevenLabs Sound Effects V3 as a 24-second soundscape. Then a Qwen omni model listens to the finished audio through the LLM node's audio port. It never sees the script, and what it writes is a SOUND-EVENT CARD: a SETTING line guessing the place and time, the events numbered in the order it actually heard them, and a LOUDEST line naming the most prominent source. That card is the only thing that crosses. A capped GLM painter turns the card alone into one image prompt — every carded source in a single scene, the loudest source largest and most central — and Muse paints it, having heard nothing and read no script. A Gemini clerk then inventories only the sound-making things visible in the painting, and a capped GLM auditor scores the journey in two hops: script against card (each scripted event HEARD or MISSED, any ADDED events, the order kept or changed) and card against painting (each carded event PAINTED or MISSING, any EXTRAS), ending in "heard X of Y events, painted Z of X". A ⚖️ Decide judge (PPLX Decider) sees the painting and all three texts and gives the headline: **sounds became the picture**, **partly painted**, **heard wrong**, or **silence or noise**.

Why the loop matters: "paint what this sounds like" is normally judged by vibes, and when the picture is wrong nobody can say where the sound got lost. Here each hop leaves a text witness, so a loss has an address: the effects model never made the sound (the card cannot contain it), the listener never heard it (it is in neither card nor painting, though the script asked for it), or the painter never painted it (carded, but missing from the read-back). The nearest catalog neighbour, score the still, runs the opposite direction — a picture is carded into music and a blind listener audits the cue on five mood axes. This flow's audit is not about mood at all: it is an inventory of events with their order, checked twice, and its ground truth is not a picture but a performance — the script as actually rendered into audio by one model and actually heard by another. Words only, repainted is the other bottleneck cousin: there a description carries a scene across one modality round trip, pixels to words to pixels, and the same scene is the target. Here the information crosses two modality jumps — sound to language to pixels — and the scene in the painting was never specified anywhere except as sounds.

Honest limits, in the open: a listener model is not a microphone with a transcript. It can hallucinate a plausible event a village soundscape "should" have, miss a quiet one under a loud one, or report the order it expected rather than the order it heard — which is exactly why the card is scored against the script instead of trusted, and why ADDED and ORDER lines exist. The effects model is the other weak joint: asked for four separated events in 24 seconds it may blend them, drop the last, or let rain swallow the bell, and the audit will charge those losses to the hearing hop only if the listener reports honestly. The painter, finally, paints sources, not sounds: a bell can be shown but its tolling cannot, so the read-back certifies that the sound-makers are visibly present and placed, not that the picture "sounds right" — that last judgement is the Decide headline's job, and it is a judgement, not a measurement. Four events is a deliberate simplification: a dense real soundscape would swamp both the listener's ordering and the painter's canvas.

Live proof, 2026-11-14 — SUCCESS end to end on the first attempt, all nodes done, no errors, $0.0642 balance-verified. The chain's verdict on itself was honest and unflattering in the most useful way. The blind listener carded three events: a rooster crowing (three times, against the script's two), a church bell ringing twice (against three tolls), and — where the script's third event should be — "water stream, flowing continuously in the background". The rain was scored MISSED with the stream as an ADDED event, and the steam train, the script's fourth event, was MISSED outright: it appears in neither the card nor the painting, so either the effects model dropped the last event in its 24 seconds or the listener lost it under the water — the texts alone cannot separate those two, which is the attribution limit working as disclosed. Hop 2 was flawless on paper: the painter put every carded source in the scene, the LOUDEST line was honoured to the letter (the rooster is enormous, central, mid-crow on a fence post), and the audit scored painted 3 of 3 — with one wrinkle, that Muse painted the church with two bell towers where the prompt asked for one bell, so the read-back listed the bell twice and the checker scored it PAINTED twice. The setting also drifted across the hops, dawn in the script becoming a bright sunny morning in the painting, following the listener's "during the day". Decide returned 'heard wrong', the headline correctly charging the loss to the hearing hop even though the painting hop was perfect: summary as printed, heard 2 of 4 events, painted 3 of 3. The painting is visually verified, and the soundscape is a real 24-second MP3.
