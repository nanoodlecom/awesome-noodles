# recipe-note
Pour water into one of several glass vessels joined at the base and physics writes the picture: the liquid flows through the connecting tube until the surface in every joined vessel rests on one and the same horizontal line. The line's height is set by how much liquid the system holds and by nothing else - not by a vessel's shape, not by its width, not by its height. A tall narrow cylinder and a short round flask hold their surfaces at exactly the same height, the cylinder looking emptier, the flask looking fuller. That is strong enough to be counterintuitive, and image models, which paint what looks balanced rather than what hydrostatics forces, break it in a signature way: they fill each vessel to the same fraction of its own height, so the surfaces stair-step up and down a row of differently tall vessels, and they helpfully level the one vessel that was never connected at all. This flow makes the law a planned, measurable quantity - and then it does something about a violation. Give it a plain bench backdrop and a GLM director picks one row from a menu printed in its own prompt (EMBER, AZURE, VIOLET, ROSE - four connected vessels in four different orders of the same four shapes at four different heights, one coloured liquid, and a fifth vessel standing apart) and copies it onto a LEVEL CARD: the liquid, the four connected vessels in left-to-right order with their shapes and relative heights, the single horizontal base tube, the middle zone the common surface must rest in, and the isolated decoy vessel with its own HIGH or LOW fill. A second GLM turns the card into a painter prompt - the vessels stated in full, in order, because the row is data, and the fill stated as an event (one generous measure poured into the first vessel only, then settled) with the hydrostatic law stated once and abstractly: surfaces in base-joined vessels rest on one horizontal line at one absolute height, a vessel that is not joined keeps its own level - and is forbidden to state how full any connected vessel should look or where its own surface sits: the painter must equalise the absolute heights itself, which is exactly where the signature failure lives, because a painter that fills by proportion staggers the surfaces and a painter that levels by tidiness levels the decoy too. Muse paints one wide picture: the row of four connected vessels on one bench line, the tube under them, the isolated vessel apart on the right. A blind Gemini reader, who never saw the card, reports each vessel's shape and the vertical position of its liquid surface as a percentage of the picture height. A GLM auditor re-derives the law itself and scores SHAPES (all five vessels), SPREAD (the gap in picture-height points between the highest and lowest connected surfaces), EQUALITY (a spread of 8 points or less), ZONE (the common line in the middle band), DECOY (the isolated surface at least 12 points clear of the common line, on its carded side, with no tube touching it - an isolation axis, not a preference) and TUBE (one tube joining all four bases). A Decide judge (PPLX Decider) headlines round 1 as a side output - **levels true**, **levels uneven**, **decoy levelled**, **not connected**, or **not vessels** - but the deliverable is the corrected picture.

That verdict is not where this flow stops - the loop closes, per the standing rule that a created asset ends in the corrected asset, not in text. A GLM editor reads the card, the read and the audit and writes ONE concrete edit instruction: the full target end state (the four vessels in order with their shapes, the tube, one common surface line set at the audit's median height) plus the audit's specific failures as surface-repaint demands - lower the tall cylinder's surface to the common line, raise the short flask's to meet it, move the decoy apart and repaint its surface well clear of the line. The editor is gated in content, a rule this catalog learned the hard way from Molecule Check, whose flawless round-1 model was recoloured by a repair it never needed: on a perfect audit the editor must demand a verbatim unchanged copy and forbid anything being repainted or moved, and whichever vessels already sit on the common line must be left exactly as they are. Muse Edit applies the notes to the round-1 picture. The corrected picture is the flow's deliverable. A second blind reader re-inspects it, and the same auditor scores round 2 against the same card, so the fix is itself audited and the reader can see exactly what the edit bought - or cost. The Decide verdict stays, demoted to a side output on round 1.

Why this axis is new: the catalog's nearest neighbours are settle-check, balance-check and scale-check, and the differences are mechanical. In settle-check five liquids stack inside ONE vessel in density order - a vertical ordering derived from a per-liquid property; here one liquid rests across SEVERAL vessels and the derived quantity is an equality, not an ordering: four surfaces at one absolute height, a relation between separate containers that no single vessel shows. In balance-check torques on one beam decide which side drops - a comparison with a winner; here there is no winner, and the control is negative: the fifth vessel must NOT join the equality, so the audit scores a non-relation (isolation kept) alongside the relation. In scale-check objects are measured against their real-world heights - a ratio to an outside standard; here the only standard is the row itself, each surface measured against the others, and the signature failure is a painter substituting a per-vessel proportion (same fraction of each height) for the cross-vessel absolute. Not the closed plan, render and recount vein: nothing is placed in a zone and no objects are counted into existence - a level is derived, produced, read blind and repaired.

Honest limits, in the open: surface positions are a blind reader's estimates against the whole picture height, good to a few points, so the equality band is 8 points and the decoy gap 12 - a surface two or three points off the line passes, and is meant to: the prey is the stair-stepped row and the levelled decoy, not sub-centimetre meniscus work. The audit scores surface heights, shapes and the tube, not glassware realism - a row drawn with wobbly glass but level surfaces and the right shapes passes, because surface height is the vocabulary a blind reader can report reliably. All four connected vessels stand on one bench line at one base height, which the law needs; a row on staggered shelves would hold staggered levels and is deliberately out of scope. And an edit round that repaints one surface can drift a neighbour or the decoy; the re-audit's SPREAD and DECOY axes are what catch that, and a repair that tidies every surface onto one line - decoy included - will show up there. Both GLM calls that carry the card are capped (maxTokens 900/1200, reasoningEffort low, the pairing this catalog's repair history landed on) so a detailed card cannot run a generation past the HTTP timeout.
