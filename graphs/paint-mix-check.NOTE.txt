# recipe-note
A mixing chart is a physics claim drawn as four rows of paint: mix blue and yellow and you get green, mix red and white and you get pink, tilt the ratio and the hue tilts with it. Image models paint handsome mixing charts that obey none of this - the result blob comes out marbled with both source colours still showing, or tinted like coloured light instead of pigment, or a cheerful colour that no mixture of the two tubes could ever produce. This flow makes the mix a planned, measurable quantity - and then it does something about a violation. Give it a chart setting and a GLM director picks one chart from a short menu printed in its own prompt (FIRST MIXES, TINTS AND SHADES, TILTED MIXES, QUIET COLOURS) - exactly four rows, each two tube pigments drawn from BLUE, RED, YELLOW, WHITE and BLACK plus a ratio, EQUAL, A-HEAVY or B-HEAVY - and copies it onto a MIX CARD. The card is the trick: it cards only the sources and the ratio. The result colour is named nowhere in the flow - not on the card, not in the painter's brief, not in any label on the poster. A second GLM turns the card into one chart-poster prompt, describing each result blob only as the single uniform colour produced by thoroughly mixing the row's two paints in the carded proportion. Muse paints it. A blind Gemini reader, who never saw the card, reports per row both source colours and the result blob's hue family, its lightness band and its form - UNIFORM, MARBLED or SPLIT. A GLM auditor holds the pigment law - primaries mix to secondaries, white tints and black shades, yellow plus black turns olive, and a heavy side pulls the hue one wheel-step toward itself - derives every expected family and lightness itself, and scores SOURCES, MIXED, HUES and LIGHTNESS. A Decide judge headlines round 1: mixes true, hues drifted, left unmixed, sources wrong, or not a chart.

That verdict is not where this flow stops - the loop closes, per the standing rule that a created asset ends in the corrected asset, not in text. A GLM editor reads the card, the read and the audit and writes ONE concrete edit instruction: the full target end state (the card's four rows, each result blob the uniform colour the law derives, named by the editor from the law) plus the audit's specific failures as row-level demands - repaint row 2's result one uniform mid purple, it is still marbled red and blue. The editor is gated in content, a rule this catalog learned the hard way from Molecule Check, whose flawless round-1 model was recoloured by a repair it never needed: on a perfect audit the editor must demand a verbatim unchanged copy and forbid anything being moved, added or recoloured. Muse Edit applies the notes to the round-1 picture. The corrected chart is the flow's deliverable. A second blind reader re-inspects it, and the same auditor scores round 2 against the same card, so the fix is itself audited and the reader can see exactly what the edit bought - or cost. The Decide verdict stays, demoted to a side output on round 1.

Why this axis is new: the catalog's nearest neighbours are palette-transplant and venn-check, and the differences are mechanical. In palette-transplant every colour is named on a card and transferred between pictures by proportion - a colour can only be present, shifted or missing, and nothing is ever computed. In venn-check a region is derived from two attributes by Boolean law - but the derived thing is a place, and every colour in it is still carded. Here the derived thing is the colour itself. The painter must produce a hue it was never told by simulating a physical process - subtractive pigment mixing in a stated ratio - the reader must name a hue nobody specified, and the auditor must compute the expected answer from the mixing law before it can score a single row. That makes failure shapes statable that no colour flow in the catalog can express: UNMIXED, where the painter hedges and leaves both source colours visible in one blob; LIGHT-MIXED, where the answer is the additive one coloured light would give instead of the pigment one; RATIO-DEAF, where an equal-mix hue appears despite a heavy side carded; and TINT-FOR-HUE confusion, which is exactly why HUES and LIGHTNESS are separate axes - pink is red that moved in lightness, not in hue, and an audit that cannot tell those apart cannot say what to fix. Not the closed plan, render and recount vein: nothing is placed in a zone and nothing is counted in place; a pigment outcome is derived, produced, named blind and repaired.

Honest limits, in the open: hue families are judged in fourteen named families plus three lightness bands because finer colour vocabulary turns the audit into naming arguments instead of mixing judgements - a result on a family boundary (a teal between GREEN and BLUE-GREEN) will be scored by the reader's call and shown as such. The source blobs carry printed pigment labels on purpose - they anchor the rows for painter, reader and editor; they are not the audited code, because the audited thing, the result colour, is the one colour on the poster with no name printed anywhere near it. The law the auditor holds is the schoolroom pigment law (blue plus yellow makes green), not a spectral simulation - that is the claim a mixing chart makes to a human reader, so it is the claim audited. One repair round is unrolled, not a loop - if round 2 still drifts a hue, the flow shows the drift rather than spending again.

Live verification (2026-11-21, the run that proved the flow): one live run, SUCCESS end to end, all 18 nodes done, no errors, $0.0249 balance-verified ($17.8451 -> $17.8203, exactly the metered sum; Muse paint $0.011, Muse Edit $0.011, Gemini readers $0.00136/$0.00152, GLM $0.00; Decide unpriced in the report, cost_exact=false). The director carded TINTS AND SHADES exactly, so the derivations exercised were the tint and shade rows, including the law's PINK special case. Round 1 was a clean sweep on the flow's first outing: CARD LAW VALID, SOURCES 8 of 8, MIXED 4 of 4, HUES 4 of 4, LIGHTNESS 4 of 4, Decide 'mixes true' - RED+WHITE read back PINK PALE, BLUE+WHITE BLUE PALE, RED+BLACK RED DARK, BLUE+BLACK BLUE DARK, every result one uniform colour. Both charts are pixel-verified: four clean rows on off-white, pure labelled sources, plus and equals signs, and result blobs that are visibly the mixed paints - a pale pink, a pale sky blue, a dark maroon red and a dark navy, none marbled, none split. Because the audit was perfect, the gated editor demanded a verbatim unchanged copy (the Molecule Check rule), Muse Edit reproduced the chart, and the blind re-read plus re-audit scored it perfect on every axis again. Two honest limits, recorded rather than smoothed: the run exercised only the tint/shade chart - the two-primary secondary mixes (blue plus yellow to green) and the ratio-shift rows in the other three charts are law-covered but were not the rows this run's director picked; and the round-2 reader listed the chart's own printed source labels and dividing rules under EXTRA, where the round-1 reader had said none - a reader strictness wobble about features the brief itself demands, carried visibly in the re-audit text while every score stayed perfect.
