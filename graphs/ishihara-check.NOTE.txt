# recipe-note
An Ishihara plate is a number that exists in exactly one channel: the dots that form it differ from the dots around it in colour and in nothing else - not in brightness, not in size, not in outline. That is the whole trick of the colour-vision test, and it is a trick image models fail in a specific, invisible way: they paint the red dots bright and the green dots dark (or the digit dots bigger, or they quietly draw the digit's outline), so the number is perfectly visible in a greyscale copy where it should vanish. Nobody notices, because nobody converts the plate to greyscale and tries to read it there. This flow does exactly that. Give it a plate setting and a GLM director picks one plate from a short menu printed in its own prompt (42 in reds and oranges on greens, 74 reversed, 35 in oranges and browns on blue-greens, 96 in yellow-greens on reds and browns) and copies it onto an ISHIHARA CARD: the two-digit number, the figure hue family, the ground hue family, and two rules copied word for word - dot lightness is random across the whole plate with the same dark-to-light spread in figure and ground, and dot sizes are random everywhere with the same spread. A second GLM turns the card into one plate prompt: a large circle of hundreds of dots, the digits formed only by dot colour, no outline, no printed digits, no text. Muse paints it. A blind Gemini reader reports the number it can see in colour, its certainty, the hues, and the CUE - whether the number betrays itself by brightness, size or outline rather than colour alone. Then the flow performs the test the trick depends on: Muse Edit converts the plate to an exact greyscale copy (same dots, same positions, colour removed, lightness kept), and a second blind Gemini reader tries to read a number in shading alone. A GLM auditor scores HUE READ (the colour reader's number against the card), LUMINANCE LEAK (SEALED if the grey reader sees no number, PARTIAL if it half-sees one, FULL if it reads a complete number in shading), CUE LEAK (the colour reader's cue was anything but colour only) and DOT FIELD. A Decide judge headlines round 1: hue only, leaks in grey, hue unreadable, cued not coloured, or not a dot plate.

That verdict is not where this flow stops - the loop closes, per the standing rule that a created asset ends in the corrected asset, not in text. A GLM editor reads the card, both reads and the audit and writes ONE concrete edit instruction: the full target end state (the same circle of dots, the carded number in the carded hues, lightness and size randomised plate-wide) plus the audit's specific failures as dot-level demands - darken some figure dots and lighten some ground dots until the digits' average lightness equals the ground's, keeping every hue family unchanged. The editor is gated in content, a rule this catalog learned the hard way from Molecule Check, whose flawless round-1 model was recoloured by a repair it never needed: on a perfect audit the editor must demand a verbatim unchanged copy. Muse Edit applies the notes to the round-1 plate. The corrected plate is the flow's deliverable - and it is tested the same way: colour re-read, a fresh greyscale conversion, a grey re-read, and the same auditor re-scoring against the same card, so the reader can see exactly what the edit bought or cost. The Decide verdict stays, demoted to a side output on round 1.

Why this axis is new: the catalog's nearest neighbours are red-reveal-check and squint-test, and the differences are mechanical. Red-reveal hides TWO messages in two colour channels and wants the hidden one REVEALED by its filtered view - its red-acetate conversion is a key that unlocks a second reading. Here there is one message and the greyscale view is not a key but a void test: the conversion must DESTROY the reading, and the flow fails precisely when the second view succeeds. Squint-test hides a pattern in spatial frequency, revealed by downscaling - one transform, one hidden quantity (shape at low resolution). Here the hidden quantity is carried by a perceptual channel (hue) and the audit separates three independent leak channels that can each betray it - luminance, dot size, outline - only one of which (luminance) the greyscale view can measure, which is why the colour reader's CUE report exists as its own axis. No other catalog flow audits an absence: every other check scores something present in the render against something present on the card; this one scores a number that must be present in one viewing condition and provably absent in another. Not the closed plan, render and recount vein: nothing is counted or placed; a channel separation is written, read in two viewing conditions, and repaired at the level of individual dots' lightness.

Honest limits, in the open: the greyscale view is a model's conversion, not a colourimetric transform - Muse Edit is instructed to keep every dot and its lightness and change nothing else, and the audit judges the leak by what the grey reader could actually read in that conversion, so a conversion that invents contrast would surface as a leak the painter never made; the flow shows both views so a human can judge which side cheated. Isoluminance is judged as a reading outcome, not measured per dot: the card's rule demands the same lightness SPREAD in figure and ground, which is the classic plate design, but a plate could satisfy the spread and still leak through average lightness - that is exactly what the grey read catches. The hue axis is the red-green family the real test uses, in four plates; finer colour-vision distinctions (tritan blue-yellow plates) are menu room, not new mechanism. One repair round is unrolled, not a loop - if round 2 still leaks, the flow shows the leak rather than spending again.
