# recipe-note
A written announcement can be perfect on the page and wrong in the ear: the voice model flattens a name into a different name, swallows a number, or reads a time as another time, and nobody notices because nobody ever listens back against the script. This graph makes the announcement answer to what was actually heard. A GLM writer turns a fact-carrying brief into one short spoken script — 55 to 70 words of plain sentences, using only the brief's facts and copying every name, date, time, number, price and place exactly (the default brief announces a fictional repair cafe's winter opening: a host's name, a Saturday date, a 9:30 door time, a hall number, a street address, a 3-item limit, a 5-dollar entry, a 12:45 close) — MiniMax Speech 2.8 HD reads the script aloud, and then Whisper transcribes the audio back to text without ever seeing the script. A GLM fact auditor scores the script against that transcript fact by fact — HEARD, GARBLED (with what was heard instead), DROPPED, plus INVENTED lines for anything the listener hears that was never written, ending in an "X of Y facts heard" summary — and a ⚖️ Decide judge (PPLX Decider) gives the headline verdict: **broadcast-ready**, **facts garbled**, **facts dropped**, or **barely intelligible**.

Why the loop matters: every other audit in this catalog reads a render back with eyes. This one listens. Text-to-speech failures are invisible in the text itself — the script the TTS model was handed stays flawless no matter what came out of the speaker — so the only honest check is a second model, from a different family, hearing the audio cold and writing down what a listener would take away. Splitting the jobs is what makes it a check rather than a self-grade: the writer is not the voice, the voice is not the listener, and the listener never sees the script it is implicitly grading.

Honest limits, in the open: the audit measures the script-to-listener chain, not either model alone — a GARBLED line can be the voice mispronouncing or the listener mishearing, and the graph cannot tell you which; play the audio yourself before blaming either. Numbers heard as words count as the same value and small wording drifts are ignored, so the audit scores facts, not diction: a stiff or oddly paced read can still score perfectly. The script is capped at about 70 words on purpose — the transcribe step has a clip-size ceiling (about 3.5 MB), and a much longer announcement in a lossless format could hit it. The brief's facts are invented for the default — this proves an announcement's facts can survive being spoken, not that any real cafe opens anywhere. Both GLM calls are capped (maxTokens 300/400, reasoningEffort low, the pairing this catalog's repair history landed on) so a detailed brief cannot run a generation past the HTTP timeout.

Expected cost per run: MiniMax Speech roughly $0.01–0.02 for a script this length, Whisper transcription a few tenths of a cent, the two GLM calls effectively $0.00, and the Decide audit is not priced in the 2026-09-12 catalog snapshot — expect roughly $0.02–0.04 all in. Prices are observations, not caps.

Live proof, 2026-11-12: the full chain ran end to end on its first attempt for $0.0417 balance-verified ($20.9105 → $20.8688; the run report prices the GLM calls at $0.00 and leaves the speech, transcription and Decide calls unpriced, cost_exact=false, so the balance delta is the verified spend), no staging input needed — the brief is text. The writer's script (63 words) carried all eight brief facts; MiniMax Speech read it in about 26 seconds of clean MP3; Whisper's cold transcript matched the script fact for fact — the only differences were value-preserving shapes a listener hears identically (9:30 heard as "9.30", 12:45 as "12.45", 5 dollars as "$5"). Both judges at the top: the fact audit scored **9 of 9 facts heard** (it counts the cafe's name as a ninth fact) with no GARBLED, DROPPED or INVENTED lines, and Decide returned **broadcast-ready**. One honest margin: a clean sweep on a clear voice reading plain facts is the easy case — the audit earns its keep on names and numbers a voice can genuinely mangle, which is exactly what a swapped brief tests. Change the **Announcement brief** input to announce anything — a gallery opening, a timetable change, a lost-and-found — but keep the facts explicit and countable (names, dates, times, numbers, prices, places), or the auditor has nothing to hold the voice to. Keep the auditor and the Decide judge wired to the join of the script and the transcript, or the score stops meaning anything; swap the voice in the Announcement node's ⚙️ settings if you prefer a different presenter.
