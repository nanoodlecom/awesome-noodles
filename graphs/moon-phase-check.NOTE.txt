# recipe-note
A moon in a picture is not a decoration: it is a geometry claim. The bright limb of the disc is the side the sunlight hits, so a real moon's lit side always faces the sun - at dusk it points toward the warm glow the set sun left low on the horizon, at dawn toward the pale glow of the rising one. Paint the lit side facing away from the glow and the moon is mirrored: a handsome, confident crescent the sky never shows, wrong in a way no single feature betrays, because every part of it - sliver, curve, glow - is plausible on its own. The phase fixes the rest of the geometry too: how much of the disc is lit (a crescent is a quarter-band sliver, a gibbous moon is three quarters lit) and which way the terminator, the inner light/dark boundary, bows - toward the lit limb for a crescent, toward the dark side for a gibbous moon. Image models paint moons as glowing props, lit by vibe on whichever side suits the composition. This flow makes the geometry a planned, measurable quantity - and then it does something about a mirrored moon. Give it a plain night-landscape backdrop and a GLM director picks one phase from a menu printed in its own prompt (EVENING CRESCENT - waxing, glow LEFT, lit LEFT, quarter band; MORNING CRESCENT - waning, the same crescent mirrored by dawn, glow RIGHT, lit RIGHT; EVENING GIBBOUS - waxing, glow LEFT, lit LEFT, three-quarter band, terminator bowing the other way) and copies it onto a PHASE CARD. A second GLM turns the card into one painter prompt demanding a single wide night photograph: a still lake, a pine treeline, one large moon, the horizon glow lingering low on the carded side and the disc lit exactly as carded. Muse paints it. A blind Gemini reader, who never saw the card, reports only what it can see - which side of the disc is lit, the shape class, the lit band, which way the terminator bows, and which side of the picture holds the glow. A GLM auditor scores the picture against the card and the law: SIDE, SHAPE, BAND and TERMINATOR against the card, and the SUN LAW from the read alone - HOLDS only when the painted lit limb faces the painted glow, a relation between two things inside the same render that copying the card cannot fake. PHASE TRUE only when all of them hold; WRONG SIDE when the lit limb faces away (the mirrored moon); WRONG AMOUNT when the side is right but the shape, band or terminator drifted; INCOMPLETE when the moon cannot be read. A Decide judge (PPLX Decider) sees the picture and all the texts and gives the headline verdict on round 1 as a side output.

Why the loop matters: the catalog's other sky and light audits each score a property the painter chose - shadow check scores cast shadows against a carded sun, refraction check scores how stripes bend through glass, thermal check ranks colours by heat. Here the certificate is a relation the painter did not choose separately: the glow and the lit limb are two independent painting decisions, and the phase is true only when they agree with each other and with the card. That split is what makes the failure taxonomy mechanical: a moon can carry the carded shape at the carded band and still be a mirrored moon (the side relation failed), or face the sun correctly with the wrong amount of light (a crescent swollen toward half), and the two diseases take different repaints. Per the owner's end-in-the-asset rule, the audit is not the deliverable: a gated GLM editor turns any failure into concrete repaint notes for the moon's light and the glow only (a true phase demands a verbatim copy; the lake, the treeline and the moon's place in the sky are never repainted to fake a match), Muse Edit repairs the photograph, and a blind re-read plus the same auditor re-score the corrected picture, which is the flow's final output.

What it does not prove: lit side, glow side and terminator bow are a blind reader's judgements on painted pixels, scored in picture-plane terms - LEFT and RIGHT as the viewer sees them - and a real moon's lit limb points toward the sun's actual position, which a flat photograph only approximates when the glow sits at the frame's edge on the moon's own side. The lit bands are coarse by design (quarter vs three-quarter), so this flow certifies the phase's geometry class, not its exact age in days; a moon lit 40 percent would be scored against the quarter band and found wanting, honestly. One run proves the chain - director, brief, paint, blind light read, law audit, gated moon repaint, re-audit - end to end on the phase the director happened to pick.
