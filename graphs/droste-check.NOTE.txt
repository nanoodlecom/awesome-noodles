# recipe-note
Every picture in this catalog is one picture. This one is two: a scene, and the same scene again inside a framed painting hanging in a plain room — a Droste picture, one level deep. A GLM director writes a DROSTE CARD: one scene from a short menu (a harbour, a mountain lake, a desert oasis, an old town square, a snowy clearing) with exactly five bold elements in left-to-right order, plus the frame that must host it. A second GLM turns the card into a painter prompt — a straight-on photograph of the room, the frame large and central, and inside it a flat painting of exactly the carded scene, element for element, in order, while the room itself stays empty of everything the scene contains. Muse paints it. Then a blind Gemini surveyor inventories the canvas in three zones — the room outside the frame, the frame itself, and the inner picture's elements left to right — and a GLM auditor scores the recursion: FRAME present or absent, INNER (how many of the five carded elements survived inside the frame), ORDER, and LEAK, the containment failure no other flow here can state: a carded element found loose in the room, the scene escaping its frame. The audit does not end the flow. A GLM editor turns it into concrete edit notes whose permitted scope is the inside of the frame — the room and the frame must not change, except that leaked duplicates in the room are removed — Muse Edit repaints the framed picture's contents, and a second blind surveyor and the same auditor re-score the corrected picture against the same card. A ⚖️ Decide judge (PPLX Decider) headlines round 1 as a side output — **recursion landed**, **inner drifted**, **no frame**, or **wrong scene** — but the deliverable is the corrected picture.

Why this is not the mirror flow wearing a frame: Mirror check reads a scene and its reflection, and the reflection is something the scene's physics produces for free — the painter draws one shore and the water copies it. Here the second depiction is a separate act of composition the painter must perform deliberately, at a smaller scale and in a different register (a flat painting inside a photograph), and it can fail in ways a reflection cannot: the frame can be missing or angled, the inner painting can quietly become a different scene, and the scene can leak — the lighthouse standing on the side table instead of inside its painting. The three-zone survey is what makes those failures separately chargeable: room, frame and inner picture are inventoried apart, so a missing element is known to be missing from the painting and not merely from the canvas.

Honest limits, in the open: one level is the honest depth. A true Droste picture recurses without end; this flow asks for exactly one nesting and the surveyor even reports whether the painter smuggled a second frame inside the first (INNER FRAME), which is recorded but not scored — a bonus, not a requirement. Small scale is the enemy: five elements must stay recognisable inside a painting that itself occupies only part of the canvas, which is why the card bans tiny details and the frame is demanded large and straight-on. The leak axis cuts both ways — a painter that puts a generic plant on the side table has not leaked anything, and the auditor only counts room objects that match a carded element. And an edit round bounded to the inside of a frame can repair a drifted painting, but it cannot invent a frame that round 1 never drew: a 'no frame' round 1 is a failure the fix round can only partially reach, and the re-audit will say so rather than paper over it.

Live proof, 2026-11-18 — SUCCESS end to end on the first attempt, all 16 nodes done, no errors, $0.0271 balance-verified. The director carded a harbour: lighthouse (white, red-banded), breakwater, red-hulled trawler, blue rowing boat, single seagull, in that order, inside a large gilt frame. Round 1 is visually verified: a plain white room, one small wooden side table, and the gilt frame large, centred and straight-on, holding a flat harbour painting with all five elements and nothing of the scene loose in the room. The blind surveyor found FRAME PRESENT, all five elements inside, plus one uncarded extra — distant hills across the water. The auditor scored INNER 5 of 5, ORDER 2 of 4, LEAK 0, EXTRA distant hills; Decide v1.2 returned 'inner drifted'. The two lost order pairs are real on the pixels: the breakwater pier extends left of the lighthouse that stands on it (the card listed the lighthouse first), and the seagull flies above and slightly left of the blue boat's centre. The editor's notes demanded the full correct sequence and the hills' removal, frame and room untouched. The corrected picture is visually verified: the hills are gone — plain water and pale sky to the horizon — the lighthouse now reads first at the far left on its pier, the room, frame and table are unchanged, and the re-audit scored FRAME PRESENT, INNER 5 of 5, EXTRA none, LEAK 0 — but ORDER stayed 2 of 4, because the re-surveyor still lists the seagull before the blue rowboat: the gull hangs in the sky above the boat's left edge, so its left-to-right position against the boat below it is a genuine overlap judgement call, and the edit round did not (and arguably could not cleanly) move a flying bird past the boat it flies over. The fix round repaired exactly the failure that was cleanly repairable — the invented hills — and the re-audit keeps the ambiguous one visible instead of resolving it by fiat.
