# recipe-note
Shake sand on a vibrating square plate and physics writes the picture: the sand is thrown off every part of the plate that moves and settles only where the plate is perfectly still - on the nodal lines. For a plate driven in mode (M,N) those lines are not a design choice: the plate vibrates in M columns and N rows of moving cells, so exactly M-1 straight vertical nodal lines divide its width into M equal parts and exactly N-1 straight horizontal lines divide its height into N equal parts, and every cell interior - an antinode, where the plate moves most - must stay bare. Nobody has to arrange the figure, but an image model does, and image models arrange by looks: the famous Chladni pictures in its memory are circular plates with rings, mandalas and scattered ornamental sand, so it paints those. This flow makes the figure a derived, measurable quantity - and then it does something about a violation. Give it a plate setting (a bare square plate) and a GLM director picks one plate from a menu printed in its own prompt (FOUR SQUARES (2,2), THREE BANDS (3,1), SIX CELLS (3,2), NINE CELLS (3,3)) and copies it onto a CHLADNI CARD: the mode indices, the vertical and horizontal line counts and positions the indices force, and the M-by-N cell count. The carded counts were verified against the mode arithmetic in code before being put on the menu (vertical = M-1 at the k/M divisions, horizontal = N-1 at the j/N divisions, cells = M times N). A second GLM turns the card into a painter prompt - the plate, the mode indices, and the nodal law stated once and abstractly (sand rests only where the plate does not move, on the straight lines between the antinode columns and rows) - and is forbidden to state any line's count or position: the painter must work the grid out from the two indices, which is exactly where the signature failure lives, because the count of nodal lines is one less than the count of moving bands in each direction and a painter that counts bands instead of boundaries paints one line too many. Muse paints the plate from directly above. A blind Gemini reader, who never saw the card, counts the straight vertical and horizontal sand lines that run edge to edge, judges their spacing, and reports curved sand, stray sand inside the cells and any sand frame along the rim. A GLM auditor derives the expected counts from the mode indices itself and scores VERTICAL, HORIZONTAL, CELLS, SPACING, ANTINODES (the cell interiors must be empty) and INTRUDERS (curved lines and rim frames the law forbids). A ⚖️ Decide judge (PPLX Decider) headlines round 1 as a side output - **pattern true to mode**, **lines missing**, **lines added**, **sand off the lines**, or **not a chladni plate** - but the deliverable is the corrected plate.

That verdict is not where this flow stops - the loop closes, per the standing rule that a created asset ends in the corrected asset, not in text. A GLM editor reads the card, the read and the audit and writes ONE concrete edit instruction: the full target end state (the carded grid named exactly - so many vertical lines at their carded positions, so many horizontal lines at theirs, every cell interior bare) plus the audit's specific failures as sand-repaint demands - remove the extra vertical line so exactly two remain at the third positions; clear the scattered sand from the cell interiors, keeping only the lines. The editor is gated in content, a rule this catalog learned the hard way from Molecule Check, whose flawless round-1 model was recoloured by a repair it never needed: on a perfect audit the editor must demand a verbatim unchanged copy and forbid anything being repainted or moved. Muse Edit applies the notes to the round-1 picture. The corrected plate is the flow's deliverable. A second blind reader re-inspects it, and the same auditor scores round 2 against the same card, so the fix is itself audited and the reader can see exactly what the edit bought - or cost. The Decide verdict stays, demoted to a side output on round 1.

Why this axis is new: the catalog's nearest neighbours are weave-check, negative-space-check and tile-check, and the differences are mechanical. In weave-check a designed grid's crossings must obey a parity law the planner wrote down thread by thread; here nothing is designed - the pattern's topology is forced by two integers through a physics law, the card's counts are arithmetic (M-1, N-1, M times N) that the auditor re-derives from the indices rather than copies from a spec, and a single extra or missing line convicts the render the way one wrong digit convicts a Sudoku grid. In negative-space-check a shape must exist as an absence between silhouettes - one bounded region that must stay empty of ink; here the negative constraint is distributed and law-derived: EVERY cell interior is an antinode and must stay empty of sand, while the positive marks are confined to lines of zero width between them, so the audit scores presence and absence against the same law at once. In tile-check a motif must continue across seams; here there is no motif at all - the characteristic failures are ones no motif audit can state: the off-by-one line (bands counted instead of boundaries), the curved ring imported from the famous circular-plate pictures, and sand scattered across cells that physics says must be bare. Not the closed plan, render and recount vein: nothing is placed in a zone and nobody counts objects - a resonance topology is derived from two indices, produced, read blind and repaired.

Honest limits, in the open: the flow cards the idealised textbook nodal grid for a mode - straight, evenly spaced lines at the exact k/M and j/N divisions. Real Chladni figures on a free-edged square plate bend with the plate's material and drive point (lines curve, diagonals and closed loops appear at higher modes), and this flow scores the idealisation for the four low modes on its menu, disclosed here rather than hidden - the same stance field-check takes toward its schoolbook dipole. Spacing is judged in two coarse states (EVEN or UNEVEN) because finer position vocabulary turns the audit into naming arguments; a line a few percent off its third still reads EVEN, and the audit says so by design. A line that fades or stops halfway is not counted by the reader, which is strict but honest: a nodal line that does not reach the edge is not the mode's line. And an edit round that repaints sand can drift a line's spacing or thin a neighbour; the re-audit's SPACING and count axes are what catch that, and a repair that decorates instead of correcting will show up there. Both GLM calls that carry the card are capped (maxTokens 900/1200, reasoningEffort low, the pairing this catalog's repair history landed on) so a detailed card cannot run a generation past the HTTP timeout.
