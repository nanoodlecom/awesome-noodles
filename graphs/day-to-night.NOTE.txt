# recipe-note
One clip between two keyframes. Muse paints a paper-craft seaside village at midday, Muse Edit repaints the same shot at night, and MiniMax H3 Max gets the day still on its image port and the night still on its end-frame port. The model has to start on the first picture and land on the second, so you choose both ends of the shot and it fills in the change.

Rewrite **Day keyframe · prompt** for a different place and **Night keyframe · instruction** for a different change (winter, a storm, a finished room). Keep "same camera, same framing" in the instruction so the frames line up. Or swap either Image node for a 📷 Image input to morph between your own before and after. **Transition · motion prompt** describes what happens in between. The graph exports the **Day → night · H3 Max (start + end frame)** video.

Defaults: Muse 16:9 and Muse Edit 16:9, then MiniMax H3 Max at 768p for 5 seconds. On 19 October 2026 the saved run reported $0.422: $0.011 + $0.011 for the stills and $0.40 for the clip. At 480p the clip is about $0.25. Prices are observations, not caps.

The saved run is in the nanoodle gallery (https://nanoodle.com/examples/gallery/#day-to-night). The camera holds still while the paper sun sinks behind the trees, the sky goes from blue through orange and pink to navy, the windows light up and the lighthouse switches on, and the clip ends on the night keyframe. Flaws: the night keyframe turned the paper waves into glossy real-looking water, so the water changes material during the clip. The moon and stars pop in during the last second instead of fading in, and the lighthouse glows rather than sweeping a beam. Inspired by @Audrius1454663's open-source rebuild of Higgsfield's VFX presets, Day→Night among them (https://x.com/Audrius1454663/status/2111177673842012349). No media from that post is used.
