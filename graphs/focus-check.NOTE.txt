# recipe-note
Focus in a photograph is not a mood; it is a plane. At a wide aperture only one distance is truly sharp, and sharpness falls away on both sides of that plane - the near side softer, the far side softer still. Generators are famously deep-focus by default: they render every object in a scene with the same crisp edges, which is why AI pictures so often read as illustrations of a place rather than photographs taken in one. This flow makes the focal plane a planned, measurable quantity. Give it a setting (the default is any location with a long front-to-back depth row) and a GLM director writes a FOCUS CARD: one scene from a menu built for depth (garden path, harbour quay, market street, orchard row, country lane), exactly 5 objects front to back at fixed graded distances - 1, 3, 6, 10 and 15 metres - each a distinct colour, and a FOCAL object chosen from positions 2 to 4, never the nearest or farthest. A second GLM turns the card into one painter prompt that demands a shallow depth of field at f/1.8, the focal object tack sharp and every other object blurred in plain words, nearer ones softly, farther ones progressively more. Muse paints the scene. A blind Gemini surveyor, who never saw the card, grades each visible object's SHARPNESS - SHARP, SLIGHTLY SOFT, SOFT or VERY BLURRED - from edge crispness and texture, and names the single SHARPEST position (or NONE on a tie). A GLM auditor converts grades to points (3/2/1/0) and scores IDENTITY (X of 5 objects in position), FOCUS PEAK (the surveyor's sharpest position equals the card's focal number), FALL-OFF (X of 4: every non-focal object strictly softer than the focal object, with points never increasing as you move away from the focal plane on either side), and ALL SHARP (all five graded SHARP). A Decide judge (PPLX Decider) sees the picture and all the texts and gives the headline verdict: **focus landed**, **focus missed**, **all sharp**, or **wrong scene**.

Why the loop matters: the catalog's other optical audits each measure a quantity carried by position or content - scale check measures height shares, stereo check displacement between two panels, squint test whole-image legibility at a second scale. Sharpness is a quantity carried by NO object and NO position: it is a per-object texture property that only exists relative to a plane none of the objects sits visibly on. The two-sided card is the second mechanism: a simple front-to-back blur gradient is the generator's one focus trick, and it puts the peak at the front by construction - a focal object at position 3 with a blurred object NEARER than it can only appear if the painter actually placed a focal plane mid-row, which makes FOCUS PEAK a plan-fidelity measurement, not a vibes grade. And ALL SHARP names the signature failure in advance - the deep-focus default where nothing is blurred at all - a failure every beauty judge passes and no other catalog audit can even state.

Honest limits, in the open: the surveyor's sharpness grades are a visual judgement from one reader, not a measured modulation transfer function - expect one-grade wobble on borderline objects, which is why FALL-OFF asks for a strict ordering rather than exact grades, and why a tie for sharpest is scored as a missed peak rather than rounded in the plan's favour. Distances on the card are scene directions to the painter, not surveyed metres; the audit scores position order and relative sharpness, never absolute distance. Only the five row objects are graded - background blur beyond the row is implied by the verdict but not scored separately.
