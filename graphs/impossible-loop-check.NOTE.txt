# recipe-note
A Penrose tribar is three straight beams joined in a triangle that cannot exist: at every corner the joint is an ordinary, buildable right angle, yet the beam that passes in front changes at every corner, so following the loop around, each beam is in front of one neighbour and behind the other, and nothing is in front overall. The figure is locally consistent everywhere and globally impossible - Penrose's own framing - and that split is precisely what image models cannot hold. A generator asked for the figure draws three locally plausible corners and quietly resolves the contradiction: it flips one corner's overlap so the three depth relations rank the beams in a single consistent order, one beam on top throughout, and delivers a handsome, completely buildable triangle. The impossibility is not lost in a blur or a melted joint; it is edited out by tidiness, one flipped overlap at a time, and a viewer who does not trace the loop corner by corner will never notice. This flow makes the contradiction a planned, measurable quantity - a directed graph of three over-relations that must form a strict cycle - and then it does something about a violation, without ever allowing the repair to tidy the impossibility away. Give it a plain studio backdrop and a GLM director picks one figure from a menu printed in its own prompt (PAINTED TIMBER, POLISHED STEEL, CARVED STONE, GLAZED CERAMIC - three square-section beams in three colours each, and an occlusion cycle that runs clockwise on two figures and counter-clockwise on two) and copies it onto a LOOP CARD: the material, the three beams by side and colour (LEFT down the left side, RIGHT down the right side, BOTTOM along the ground), the three corners, and at each corner the one beam that passes OVER the other. A second GLM turns the card into a painter prompt that states the beams and each corner's over-relation in full as local drawn facts - the corners are data - and is forbidden to rank the beams, to state any global front or back order, or to call the figure impossible: the painter must draw three ordinary local overlaps whose sum cannot exist, which is exactly where the signature failure lives, because a painter that draws one global depth order instead flips a corner. Muse paints one square picture: the upright triangular loop standing on its bottom beam alone on a plain studio ground, no supports. A blind Gemini tracer, who never saw the card, traces the loop corner by corner and reports each beam's colour, each corner's joint (CLEAN, MELTED, BROKEN or TWISTED) and the beam in front at each corner, plus any supports - judging each corner on its own and reporting what it sees even when the three answers cannot all be true of one buildable object. A GLM auditor derives the law from the card itself (CARD LAW: each beam over at exactly one corner and under at exactly one) and scores BEAMS (the three colours by side), JOINTS (corners with a clean square right-angle joint), FRONTS (corners whose drawn front beam is the card's over beam), CYCLE (CYCLIC only when all three drawn fronts reproduce the carded cycle, every beam in front at one corner and behind at the other; BROKEN when the drawn fronts rank the beams transitively - one beam in front at both its corners, another behind at both - because then the drawn figure could actually be built; UNREADABLE when a front cannot be read) and SUPPORTS (anything propping the loop up disambiguates it, so a prop is a leak, not a kindness). A Decide judge (PPLX Decider) headlines round 1 as a side output - **impossible and clean**, **made buildable**, **corners melted**, **held up**, or **not a loop** - but the deliverable is the corrected figure.

That verdict is not where this flow stops - the loop closes, per the standing rule that a created asset ends in the corrected asset, not in text, and here the closing has a twist no other flow in the catalog carries: the repair must preserve the impossibility. A GLM editor reads the card, the trace and the audit and writes ONE concrete edit instruction: the full target end state (the three beams by side and colour, the carded front beam at each corner) plus the audit's specific failures as corner-level repaint demands - repaint the flipped corner alone so the carded beam's end passes visibly in front there, rebuild the melted corner as one clean square right-angle joint, remove the prop and leave the loop standing on its bottom beam alone - while every corner that already shows its carded front and a clean joint is left exactly as drawn. The editor is gated in content, a rule this catalog learned the hard way from Molecule Check, whose flawless round-1 model was recoloured by a repair it never needed: on a perfect audit the editor must demand a verbatim unchanged copy. And it is forbidden, in terms, to demand that the figure be made consistent, buildable or realizable, to rank the beams, or to flip a passing corner - the three fronts must remain a cycle. Muse Edit repaints the failing corners, a blind re-trace reads the corrected figure, and the same auditor re-scores it against the same card. The flow's final output is the corrected figure itself.

The honest limits are stated in the open. The audit certifies the drawn occlusion graph, not a viewer's perception: a render whose three fronts form the carded cycle reads as a tribar to a human tracer, but this flow's tracer is itself a model, and a corner whose overlap is genuinely ambiguous will be read one way or the other - the UNCLEAR verdict exists for exactly that case and scores the cycle UNREADABLE rather than guessing. Colour-coding the beams is a deliberate scaffolding: it makes each beam's identity traceable around the loop, which is what lets a flipped corner be attributed to a corner rather than to a vague sense of wrongness, and it means the flow tests occlusion bookkeeping, not material realism - an unpainted wooden tribar would need a different identity scheme. The two cycle directions and the four materials are menu variety, not coverage: a painter could hold the cycle on a clockwise timber figure and flip a counter-clockwise ceramic one, and only the run that happens is evidence. One run proves the chain - director, brief, paint, blind trace, cycle audit, gated repair, re-audit - end to end on the figure the director happened to pick; it does not prove every figure on the menu survives its painter.
