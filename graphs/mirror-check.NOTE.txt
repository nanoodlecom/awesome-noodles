# recipe-note
A reflection is a second rendering of the same scene, drawn under strict rules the first rendering sets — and that is exactly what image models do not do. They pattern-match what reflections usually look like, so the water in a generated picture quietly invents: a landmark loses its reflection entirely, a reflected boat changes colour, a shape appears in the lake that stands nowhere on the shore, the order scrambles. Prompt guides name this the fastest way to spot an AI image, and the research literature lists mismatched object–mirror reflections among diffusion models' persistent inter-feature rule failures. Give this flow a shore brief (the default lines five landmarks along a still lake) and a GLM director turns it into one image prompt that demands the hard version of the scene: a level waterline across the middle, a perfectly glassy lake filling the whole lower half, every landmark's reflection directly below itself, vertically flipped, a little darker and softer. Muse paints scene and reflection in a single render. Then the same picture is read twice, by two Gemini readers who never see the brief and never see each other: one inventories only the real landmarks above the waterline, the other only the reflections in the water below it, each strictly left to right with colours. A GLM mirror auditor lines the two cards up position by position — MIRRORED, COLOUR SHIFTED, WRONG OBJECT, MISSING REFLECTION — plus an EXTRA line for reflections with nothing above them and an ORDER line (SAME, REVERSED or SCRAMBLED), ending in "X of Y landmarks mirrored". A ⚖️ Decide judge (PPLX Decider) sees the picture and all three texts and gives the headline verdict: **true mirror**, **reflections drifted**, **half a mirror**, or **no reflection**.

Why the loop matters: every other audit in this catalog compares a render against something outside it — an authored plan, a recipe, a first picture, a transcript. This one compares a render against itself. There is no plan to recount and no second generation to blame: the direct card is the source of truth and the reflection card is the claim, and both are read from the same pixels, so a failure belongs to exactly one place, the painter's grasp of the reflection rule. That also makes the audit's two directions asymmetric in a way no state audit is: a missing reflection and an extra reflection are different lies — one is the water forgetting the shore, the other is the water inventing one — and the score keeps them on separate lines. Spot-the-difference is the nearest relative, but it compares two separate pictures for changes an editor was ordered to make; here nothing was ordered to change, and the comparison runs between two regions of one frame related by a physical transform, not between two frames related by an edit.

Honest limits, in the open: the readers split the picture at the waterline by instruction, not by a mask — Nanoodle has no crop-to-region node — so a reader who strays across the line pollutes the other card, and the audit can only score what the cards say, not re-draw the line. Colour in water is genuinely slippery: a reflection is supposed to read darker and softer, so the auditor allows that shift and only flags a clear change of hue, which makes COLOUR SHIFTED a judgement call at the margin, recorded with the reflection's colours so you can judge it yourself. Small or distant landmarks reflect as streaks a reader may honestly miss, which is why the default brief uses five big, bold, differently coloured landmarks and a glassy lake — rippled water would make the whole audit mush, and the flow says so rather than pretending to score it. And the director's own faithfulness to the brief is not what is scored: if the painter drops a landmark from the shore itself, both cards lose it together and the mirror can still score perfectly — the flow audits the reflection, not the scene.

Live proof, 2026-11-14 — SUCCESS end to end on the first attempt, all nodes done, no errors, $0.0310 balance-verified (Muse $0.011, direct read $0.01239, reflection read $0.00758, GLM $0.00; Decide unpriced in the report, cost_exact=false). The director kept the brief's five landmarks in order — red-and-white striped lighthouse, yellow dome tent, blue sailboat, tall pine, white boathouse — and Muse's render is a genuinely glassy dawn lake: every landmark stands on the shore with its reflection directly below it, sails and lighthouse stripes flipped in the water. Both blind readers carded exactly five items in the same order; the mirror audit scored 5 of 5 MIRRORED, EXTRA none, ORDER: SAME, and Decide v1.2 returned 'true mirror' — a clean sweep the human eye agrees with. Honest catches, recorded rather than rounded away: the painter gave the sailboat two sails (mainsail and jib) against the brief's "one white sail" — a director-faithfulness slip the mirror audit correctly does not score, since both cards see the same boat; and the boathouse reflection is almost implausibly crisp, a second building floating in the lake — but its colours and position are right, so MIRRORED is the honest score. The flow's failure modes (a dropped or invented reflection) did not occur in this render; the audit's value is that it would have caught them position by position.
