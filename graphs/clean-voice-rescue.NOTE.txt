# recipe-note
A voice recording made somewhere loud is usually written off: the words are in there, buried under the room, and a single transcript of the raw take reads like guesswork. This graph proves the rescue instead of asserting it. Seedance 2.5 films the noisy source itself — a baker at a busy farmers-market stall shouting one exact, fact-carrying line ('Fresh sourdough, still warm from the oven - two loaves for five dollars, today only at stall twelve!') into the camera with the crowd, the espresso machine and the market's clatter in its native audio — the local Extract audio node lifts the take's own sound out, and Whisper transcribes it cold. Then the graph's one irreplaceable node runs: Clean voice (ElevenLabs Audio Isolation) strips everything that is not her voice and hands back a cleaned recording, which Whisper transcribes again, just as cold. A GLM auditor scores the line against BOTH transcripts word by word — RECOVERED (wrong or missing in the raw hearing, right in the clean one), CLEAR BOTH, STILL GARBLED, LOST IN CLEANING — ending in 'Raw: X of Y words heard. Clean: A of Y words heard.', and a ⚖️ Decide judge (PPLX Decider) gives the headline verdict: **rescued**, **partly clearer**, **no better**, or **voice damaged**.

Why the chain matters: noise removal is normally a faith claim — the cleaned clip *sounds* clearer, so everyone nods. Here the improvement is a measurement, not an impression: the same listener, from a different model family than the cleaner, transcribes both versions without ever seeing the line, and the delta between the two hearings is the score. The cleaner cannot grade itself, the listener cannot see the answer key, and the line's facts (two loaves, five dollars, stall twelve) are exactly the kind of content noise eats first. Splitting the jobs is what makes it a check: Seedance performs, ElevenLabs cleans, Whisper listens twice, GLM scores, Decide labels.

Honest limits, in the open: the clean-voice node can only drink from a public link — NanoGPT downloads the source itself, so it refuses clips uploaded or recorded in the browser (they have no URL). That is why the noisy take is generated inside the graph: a generated video is a hosted file. To rescue a real recording, host it somewhere public and paste its link into the Clean voice node's link field (or wire a generated video in, as here). The audit measures the rescue for a *listener*, not waveform purity — a cleaned clip can sound thinner yet transcribe better, and that trade is scored as a win here, which is the right call for voice memos and the wrong one for music. Both transcripts come from the same listening model on purpose: any consistent mishearing cancels out in the delta, but a word Whisper simply cannot hear in either version lands as STILL GARBLED even if a human could catch it. And the default take is synthetic — it proves the rescue loop on a controlled line, not that any particular real-world recording will survive its own noise.

Expected cost per run: the Seedance take is the bill, and Seedance 2.5 is far pricier than its predecessor — observed live at $1.08 for the 6-second 480p take with native audio (about $0.18 a second, vs about $0.024 a second for the retired 1.5 Pro), so budget roughly $1.10 per run — ElevenLabs Audio Isolation observed $0.0123, two Whisper transcriptions a few tenths of a cent between them, the GLM audit effectively $0.00, and the Decide verdict is not priced in the 2026-09-12 catalog snapshot.
