# recipe-note
One main photo plus up to three reference pictures go into a single edited scene. The instruction names each picture by number — Picture 1 is the main photo, Picture 2 the outfit, Picture 3 the accessory, Picture 4 the companion — and MiniMax H3-Image (minimax-h3-image) fuses them positionally; it takes up to 16 references this way. A Gemini survey writes the subject's anchors before the edit, a second Gemini reader describes the result blind (it never sees the originals), and a GLM checker compares instruction, anchors and re-read and reports LANDED / MISSING / SUBJECT: SURVIVED or DRIFTED.

Why this model, why now: H3-Image sat at #1 on Hugging Face trending for six straight days in November 2026 (~3.4M downloads across the two repos) and independent boards rank it the top open-weights image editor — but the open weights total ~135 GB and the MiniMax community license excludes the US, EU, UK and Korea, so most of the people downloading it cannot run it locally. Hosted here, it just runs in the browser. The example exists because the Demand Watch found ComfyUI users asking for exactly this edit shape all week.

One pin matters: the edit node's size is set to "1k". H3 accepts only 1K or 2K output and rejects pixel sizes — a graph that leaves the executor's 1024x1024 default in place fails with "MiniMax H3 Image supports only 1K or 2K output" (found by the first probe run, which cost $0.003 and stopped at the survey).

The graph exports the **Fuse the references — MiniMax H3-Image** image and the **Edit report — GLM grades the fusion** text.

Defaults are one H3 edit at 1K with three references ($0.04 + 2 × $0.008 = $0.056), two Gemini reads and a GLM check. The saved run reported $0.0705 all-in; an earlier probe with the size unset stopped after the survey ($0.003). Prices are observations, not caps.

The saved run is in the nanoodle gallery (https://nanoodle.com/examples/gallery/#edit-with-references): the woman from the main photo keeps her face, long dark hair, pose, jeans and the grey wall; the red leather jacket from Picture 2 is on her, worn open over the white t-shirt, belt and zip pockets carried over; the round gold-framed sunglasses from Picture 3 are on her face; the Dalmatian puppy from Picture 4 sits by her legs with its head tilt intact. Flaws, honestly: the checker graded the sunglasses and the open jacket "unclear in the re-read" — the blind reader hedged on fine detail that is plainly visible in the picture itself, which is the audit working as designed (it grades from text, not from the pixels). The puppy's gaze lands near the camera rather than straight into it. Swap any 📷 input for your own photos, keep the picture numbering, and rewrite the instruction to match.
