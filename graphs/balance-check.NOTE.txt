# recipe-note
A balance beam is not a picture of objects on a plank - it is an equation you can photograph. Every load on the beam pushes its side down with a torque, weight times distance from the fulcrum, and the beam has no choice about the result: the side with the greater total torque goes down, and equal torques hold it level. That law is strong enough to be counterintuitive - a light load far out on the beam out-torques a heavy load sitting close in - and image models, which draw what looks plausible rather than what arithmetic forces, routinely level beams that should tip and tip them toward whichever object looks biggest. This flow makes the law a planned, measurable quantity - and then it does something about a violation. Give it a rig setting and a GLM director picks one rig from a short menu (FAIR WEIGHTS, LEVERAGE UPSET, HEAVY SIDE DROPS, TWO AGAINST ONE) whose full spec is printed in its own prompt - every load as an object, a weight in kilograms and a resting zone, CLOSE, MID or FAR from the central fulcrum, each load's torque and each side's sum printed - and copies it onto a RIG CARD ending in the PREDICTION the law forces, so the card cannot invent physics. A second GLM turns the card into one photograph prompt: a long wooden beam on a single triangular fulcrum, the loads at their carded zones described in plain positions (just beside the fulcrum, halfway out, at the very end), the beam settled exactly as the prediction says - and no printed weights, numbers or labels anywhere, because print would cheat the physics: the weights must exist only as objects, and their effect only as tilt. Muse paints it. A blind Gemini reader, who never saw the card, reports only what it can see: which end of the beam sits lower, or LEVEL, and every object on each side with its zone. A GLM auditor recomputes every torque from the card, scores TORQUE MATH (the card's own arithmetic against the rule), OBJECTS and ZONES against the card, and TILT - the observed settle against the prediction the arithmetic forced. A Decide judge headlines round 1: physics holds, tilt wrong way, stuck level, loads misplaced, or rig unreadable.

That verdict is not where this flow stops - the loop closes, per the standing rule that a created asset ends in the corrected asset, not in text. A GLM editor reads the card, the read and the audit and writes ONE concrete edit instruction: the full target end state (the card's loads on their carded sides at their carded zones, the beam settled as the prediction demands, no print anywhere) plus the audit's specific failures as extra demands - move the watering can to the very end of the right half, tilt the beam so the right end rests lower, nearly touching the floor - locating each fix by the load's name. The editor is gated in content, a rule this catalog learned the hard way from Molecule Check, whose flawless round-1 model was recoloured by a repair it never needed: on a perfect audit the editor must demand a verbatim unchanged copy and forbid anything being moved, added or restyled. Muse Edit applies the notes to the round-1 picture. The corrected rig is the flow's deliverable. A second blind reader re-observes it, and the same auditor scores round 2 against the same card, so the fix is itself audited and the reader can see exactly what the edit bought - or cost. The Decide verdict stays, demoted to a side output on round 1.

Why this axis is new: the catalog's nearest neighbours are stack-check and scale-check, and the difference is mechanical. Stack-check reads a static support chain - which object rests on which, whether anything floats - and scale-check reads proportions against carded ratios inside a tolerance band; neither computes a quantity, and neither predicts anything. Balance check's card carries numbers that force a falsifiable prediction about a physical outcome before a single pixel exists, and the headline score is prediction against outcome under a quantitative law: the sum of weight times distance on each side. Zones here are not placement for its own sake - they are the distance factor in a product, which is why the menu's leverage upset matters: the 4 kg watering can at FAR (torque 12) must tip the beam against the 9 kg stone block at CLOSE (torque 9), so a painter that simply tips the beam toward the biggest-looking object fails in a way the audit can name. No other flow in the catalog asks a render to obey arithmetic.

Honest limits, in the open: this is torque fidelity only - friction, beam weight, load size versus load weight and the fulcrum's exact centre are deliberately out of scope; the menu uses three coarse zones and whole kilograms because each finer gradation is another channel the read must carry. The reader cannot see weight and is never asked to guess it: it reports objects, sides, zones and tilt, and the auditor supplies the numbers from the card - a load the painter drew at the wrong size still scores on its position, which is the channel the law uses. Tilt is judged as a three-way call (left down, right down, level), so a beam a degree off horizontal is a genuine border for the reader; the read records the call it made. The fix round has its own limit: an edit model asked to tilt a beam can slide a load along it - that is exactly what round 2's OBJECTS, ZONES and TILT lines are for, and both audits are printed so the reader can see the trade. One repair round is unrolled, not a loop - if round 2 still violates, the flow shows the violation rather than spending again.

Live verification (2026-11-20, the run that proved the flow): one live run, all 18 nodes done, no errors, $0.0297 metered. The director carded LEVERAGE UPSET - the menu's counterintuitive rig - and copied it exactly: stone block 9 kg at CLOSE (torque 9) against a metal watering can 4 kg at FAR (torque 12), PREDICTION: RIGHT DOWN. Round 1: TORQUE MATH VALID, OBJECTS 2 of 2, TILT MATCH, Decide 'physics holds' - the beam really does settle right-down, its right end nearly touching the floor, the light watering can at the beam's end out-levering the heavy stone, which pixel checks confirm sits about 40 percent of the way out on the raised left half. The one miss was that zone: ZONES 1 of 2, the blind reader calling the stone MID against the carded CLOSE - a defensible call on the pixels, recorded as scored. The editor's fix was on-design (slide the stone to CLOSE, keep the tilt), but Muse Edit overshot: the corrected picture shows the stone moved right up against the fulcrum yet sunk halfway into the beam's face instead of resting on top of it, so the blind re-read reported no load on the left at all and round 2 scores OBJECTS 1 of 2, ZONES 1 of 2, TILT still MATCH. The re-audit caught the fix round's regression exactly as designed; the flow shows the trade rather than spending again. Both pictures are clean, text-free studio photographs - one beam, one triangular fulcrum, no labels anywhere.
