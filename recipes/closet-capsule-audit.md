## Notes

Capsule-wardrobe and virtual-stylist apps do one trick: show them your closet (or don't) and get outfit ideas — and the classic failure is the phantom garment: the stylist builds the look around a silk scarf, white trainers or a belt you do not own, and nothing ever checks the suggestion against the clothes that actually exist. The render makes it worse, because the image model happily adds a hat or swaps the loafers for heels and the pretty picture buries the lie. This graph splits the jobs across four model families and audits both places a phantom can be born. A Gemini vision node is the closet clerk (it writes the inventory — every garment, shoe and accessory it can actually see, numbered, with colour, material and one distinguishing detail each, tops to accessories, "( ? )" where it is guessing), a GLM node changes hats into a capsule stylist (it builds ONE hero outfit for your brief using only inventory items, and every worn line must copy its item's description and cite its inventory number, so the plan carries its own receipts; it also names which pieces re-wear on the brief's other days and which numbers it left out), a second GLM compresses the slip into a single flat-lay prompt with the numbers stripped, and Muse renders the outfit as a flat lay from that text alone.

Then the outfit has to answer for itself, twice, at two different checkpoints. A second Gemini vision node reads the flat lay back and inventories only the garments actually visible in it — it never sees the slip or the closet, so it cannot grade generously. A GLM phantom auditor runs two passes over the three texts: pass one checks every slip item against the inventory line its number cites (a stylist inventing or recolouring a garment is caught here, before any rendering), and pass two scores the read-back against the slip item by item (WORN / MISSING, plus a PHANTOM line for anything rendered that is on neither list), ending in an "X of Y worn items rendered, Z phantoms" summary. And a ⚖️ Decide judge (PPLX Decider) gives the headline verdict from the inventory, the slip and the flat-lay image together: **closet only, complete**, **phantom item**, **pieces missing**, or **wrong occasion**. The graph exports the **Flat lay — audited** verdict text, and the flat lay itself rides through the Decide node as its image output — open the Flat lay node to see the outfit the verdict is about.

Why the split matters: the inventory is written before any styling happens, so neither the stylist nor the renderer can quietly expand the closet to make the outfit work; the number citations are what make the audit mechanical rather than vibes — a checker does not have to decide whether a garment "feels" owned, it compares line #4 against line #4. And the two checkpoints fail differently in an informative way: a plan phantom means the stylist cheated, a render phantom means the image model freelanced, and the audit tells you which.

Honest limits, in the open: the inventory is a photo read, not ground truth — a garment folded behind another is invisible to the clerk, and then the stylist is forbidden from using clothes you actually own; read the inventory before you trust the audit. The flat lay is rendered from text descriptions, not from your actual garments, so it shows the outfit's idea, not your blazer's real drape or wear — treat it as a styling proof, not a photograph of your clothes. Colours are wording-sensitive at every step (one model's "rust" is another's "burnt orange"), so expect an occasional strictness phantom on shade names rather than substance. And a capsule audit cannot judge fit, condition or whether you still like the thing — it only proves the outfit is buildable from what the camera saw. A generated staging photo was used for the live proof below — no real closet was staged in this workspace — disclosed here rather than smoothed over.

Expected cost per run: Muse flat lay $0.011, the two Gemini vision reads roughly $0.005–0.007 each, the three GLM calls effectively $0.00–0.011 combined, and the Decide audit is not priced in the 2026-09-12 catalog snapshot — expect roughly $0.03–0.05 all in. Prices are observations, not caps.
