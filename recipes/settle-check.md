## Notes

Pour five immiscible liquids into one glass and physics writes the picture: each liquid sinks or floats until the stack rests in density order, lightest on top, densest at the bottom. Nobody has to arrange it - but an image model does have to, and image models arrange by looks. The pale golden honey reads as the lightest thing in the jar and gets floated; the deep-dyed alcohol reads as heavy and gets sunk; bands get stacked in the order they were listed, or in rainbow order, or in whatever order looks pretty. This flow makes the settling a planned, measurable quantity - and then it does something about a violation. Give it a jar setting (an empty glass cylinder) and a GLM director picks one jar from a menu printed in its own prompt (RAINBOW, ORCHARD, LAGOON, EMBER) - five immiscible liquids, each with a distinct colour and a distinct density - and copies it onto a SETTLE CARD in pour order, deliberately NOT in settling order. One liquid per card is a named DECOY: the pale amber honey that is secretly the densest thing in the jar, or the deep violet alcohol that is secretly the lightest - the liquid whose appearance predicts exactly the wrong end of the tower. A second GLM turns the card into one cylinder prompt naming only the liquids and their colours, plus the settling law stated once and abstractly - the liquids rest in density order, lightest on top, densest at the bottom - and is forbidden to state any liquid's position or repeat any density number: the painter must work the order out from the liquids themselves. Muse paints the tower. A blind Gemini reader, who never saw the card, reports the bands top to bottom by colour alone, the layer count and the state of the interfaces. A GLM auditor derives the expected order itself - sorting the carded densities - and scores ORDER (the four adjacent pairs in the read must each step down in density), COLOURS (every carded colour present, no intruders), LAYERS, INTERFACES (flat and sharp, not wavy or mixed) and DECOY (the lying liquid must sit at its density-derived end). A Decide judge headlines round 1: settled by density, order wrong, layers mixed, colours off, or not a density tower.

That verdict is not where this flow stops - the loop closes, per the standing rule that a created asset ends in the corrected asset, not in text. A GLM editor reads the card, the read and the audit and writes ONE concrete edit instruction: the full target end state (the five bands named top to bottom in the derived density order) plus the audit's specific failures as re-layering demands - swap the blue water above the purple soap, it read the other way. The editor is gated in content, a rule this catalog learned the hard way from Molecule Check, whose flawless round-1 model was recoloured by a repair it never needed: on a perfect audit the editor must demand a verbatim unchanged copy and forbid anything being moved, added or recoloured. Muse Edit applies the notes to the round-1 picture. The corrected tower is the flow's deliverable. A second blind reader re-inspects it, and the same auditor scores round 2 against the same card, so the fix is itself audited and the reader can see exactly what the edit bought - or cost. The Decide verdict stays, demoted to a side output on round 1.

Why this axis is new: the catalog's nearest neighbours are paint-mix-check, stack-check and balance-check, and the differences are mechanical. In paint-mix-check an outcome is derived too, but it is a colour produced inside one blob by a mixing law; here the derived thing is a PERMUTATION - the vertical order of five whole bands - produced by a sorting law over five densities at once, and its characteristic failure is a single inverted adjacent pair inside an otherwise perfect tower. In stack-check the order of the tower is carded by the planner and the audit checks support and contact; nothing is derived and physics is only obeyed or broken at contact points. In balance-check one equation (torque) decides one binary outcome (which side dips); here the law must be applied five times and composed into a total order, and the DECOY axis scores whether density beat appearance at the extremes - whether the pale honey sank and the dark alcohol floated. Not the closed plan, render and recount vein: nothing is placed in a zone and the counting is trivial (five bands); a physical sorting is derived, produced, read blind and repaired.

Honest limits, in the open: the audit reads colours, not liquids - the card gives every liquid a unique colour precisely so a colour sequence is a liquid sequence, and a painter who renders two bands in near-identical golds (oil over honey) makes the read genuinely ambiguous; the menu keeps the five colours in a jar far apart for that reason. Densities are the textbook values for the named liquids and dyes are assumed not to move them; the flow audits whether the painter honoured the carded physics, not whether a real dyed alcohol still measures 0.79. Interfaces are judged in three coarse states because finer vocabulary turns the audit into naming arguments. One repair round is unrolled, not a loop - if round 2 still inverts a pair, the flow shows the inversion rather than spending again.
