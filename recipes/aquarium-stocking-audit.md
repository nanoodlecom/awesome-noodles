## Notes

Stocking calculators (AqAdvisor, App-aquatic, Aquabuildr) check a fish list as text: bioload, pairwise compatibility, a temperature window. Fish-shop inspiration goes the other way — a gorgeous planted tank in a photo, no list behind it, and no way to know whether the fish in it can actually live together or even which fish they are. This graph does both halves and then closes a loop neither closes: a GLM aquarist writes a 7-line stocking plan from a brief — one species or plant per water zone (a surface fish, two different midwater species, a bottom fish, bottom invertebrates, background plants, foreground plants), each line with an exact count, honouring the brief's hard constraints (the default brief is a peaceful 60-litre community tank and bans aggressive fish, fin-nippers and goldfish outright) — a second GLM turns the plan into a photo brief, and Muse renders the finished tank straight-on through the front glass. Then a Gemini vision tank checker inventories only what is actually visible, zone by zone with counts, a GLM auditor scores the original plan against that inventory line by line (STOCKED / WRONG ZONE / WRONG COUNT / MISSING, EXTRA lines for anything unplanned, ending in an "X of 7 stocked" summary), and a ⚖️ Decide judge (PPLX Decider) gives the headline verdict: **stocked to plan**, **counts drifted**, **zones drifted**, or **species missing**. The render is not decoration here; it is the claim, and two independent reads hold it to the plan.

Why the loop matters: image models are enthusiastic stockers. They fill every layer of the water with the prettiest fish, park the bottom-dwellers midwater because the composition breathes better there, turn a school of six into a vague shimmer, and slip a photogenic extra species in behind the plants. A plan alone cannot catch that, and a render alone looks convincing precisely when it is wrong. Splitting the jobs across four model families is what makes the check honest: the model that wrote the plan is not the model that rendered it, and neither of them grades it. The zone rule is the aquarium-specific twist — stocking calculators have tracked each species' swim level for years, because a fish can be present at exactly the right count and the tank is still stocked wrong: the surface fish cruising the gravel and the corydoras hovering at the top are a different, stressed tank.

Honest limits, in the open: the tank check reads a 512 px shrink of the render, so small fish half-hidden behind plants or each other are judgement calls, and the checker's "(?)" guesses flow straight into the audit — read the WRONG COUNT and WRONG ZONE lines against the render yourself before you trust them. Fish identification from a render is also fallible: two small silver schooling species can swap names between the render and the read-back, which becomes a false MISSING plus a false EXTRA. The auditor is a text comparison of two lists, so a zone the vision checker misjudges becomes a false WRONG ZONE. And the render is a goal picture, not a photograph of a tank anyone stocked: it proves the plan fits one tank and one water column, not that your real fish will hold their zones — real fish move, and the audit grades a still. And 7 lines is a design choice, not a law — edit the aquarist's system prompt and the auditor's summary line together if you stock bigger tanks. One deliberate guard from this catalog's own repair history: the photo-brief node is capped (maxTokens 300, reasoningEffort low, the pairing the mural-wall repair landed on), because an uncapped brief generation on a detailed plan once ran past the HTTP timeout three runs in a row.

Expected cost per run: Muse text-to-image $0.011, the Gemini tank check roughly $0.001–0.008, the three GLM calls effectively $0.00, and the Decide audit is not priced in the 2026-09-12 catalog snapshot — expect roughly $0.02–0.04 all in. Prices are observations, not caps.

Live proof, 2026-11-11: the full chain ran end to end for $0.0229 balance-verified ($24.4340 → $24.4111, exactly the metered sum; Muse render $0.011, Gemini tank check $0.01188, GLM calls $0.00, Decide unpriced in the report), no staging input needed — the aquarium brief is text. The aquarist honoured every hard constraint (peaceful community species only, nothing over 8 cm, no goldfish, schooling groups of eight) and planned 6 metallic-blue male guppies at the surface, 8 harlequin rasboras and 8 neon tetras midwater, 6 panda corydoras and 6 cherry shrimp on the bottom, 5 vallisneria clumps at the back and 4 dwarf sagittaria tufts in front. Muse's render is visually verified: exactly that tank, straight-on, surface line at the top, and every species in its planned zone — the zone system held perfectly on its first outing — with guppies and vallisneria at their exact counts. Both judges agree the counts drifted generous: Decide returned **counts drifted** and the audit scored **2 of 7 stocked** — rasboras read back 10 against 8 planned, tetras 11 against 8, shrimp 9 against 6, and corydoras 5 counted against 6 (a sixth sits half out of frame at the left edge). Two honest naming calls, recorded in the open: the read-back took the tetras for cardinal tetras, marked "(?)", which the auditor scored as a close colour match rather than a miss; and it named the four foreground grass tufts Cryptocoryne (?), which the auditor scored as dwarf sagittaria MISSING plus a cryptocoryne EXTRA — on the pixels they are the planned grass-like tufts, a plant-naming judgement call of the same family as the sneaker stripe. Run report and images: flow-hunt/runs/2026-11-11-aquarium-stocking-audit/ in the nanoodle-dev workspace (render saved as peaceful-community-tank.png).

Change the **Aquarium brief** input for tank size, style and hard constraints ("a 200-litre Amazon biotope, tetras and corydoras only", "a low-tech shrimp tank, no fish at all" — the aquarist will push back via the zone shape, which is itself informative), and the whole chain re-plans around it. Keep the auditor wired to both the aquarist's plan and the tank check, or the score stops meaning anything; swap the render model in the Aquarium node's ⚙️ settings if you prefer a different photographic look.
