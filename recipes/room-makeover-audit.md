## Notes

The room-redesign pipeline, rebuilt as a multimodel graph with a check at the end. Redesign apps (Roomika, AI Interior Design and friends) and ComfyUI interior toolkits (ArchAi3d-Qwen and its kin) do the same single-model trick: upload a room, name a style, get a render — and nothing verifies the render is still your room, which is the classic failure: a window moves, a doorway vanishes, the camera drifts and the "redesign" is a different house. This graph splits the four jobs across four model families, because each job is a different skill: a Gemini vision node is the surveyor (it splits the photo into a KEEP EXACTLY list — windows, doors, ceiling, camera viewpoint — and a CHANGEABLE list, and is forbidden from suggesting designs), a GLM node is the designer (it writes one plan that must repeat the keep-list verbatim in spirit and may only change CHANGEABLE items), a second GLM node changes hats and art-directs (it compresses the plan into a single surgical edit instruction that starts with the keep-list), and Muse Edit renders the redesign from the original photo itself, not from a text description of it.

The piece the apps do not have is the audit at the end. A Decide judge (PPLX Decider) receives the designer's plan as text and the redesigned room as an image, and has to label the render: **faithful redesign** (same architecture, most planned changes applied), **minor drift** (same room, one small detail or change off), **architecture moved** (a window, door, ceiling or the camera moved — the render failed at the one thing it had to do), or **barely changed** (architecture intact but the makeover never really happened). The graph exports the **Redesigned room — audited** verdict text, and the redesign itself rides through the Decide node as its image output — open the Redesigned room node to see the render the verdict is about.

Honest limits, in the open: the survey is a photo read, not ground truth — the surveyor can mislabel a door as a cupboard or miss a beam, and the designer's keep-list inherits any misread (the Redesign brief input is where you correct it: name the thing that must stay). The edit model re-generates the whole frame, so architecture preservation is an instruction it can disobey, never a pixel guarantee — that is exactly what the audit exists to catch rather than prevent. The audit judges a 512 px shrink of the render, plenty for "that window moved" and weaker for small finish disputes. And the render is a concept picture, not a contractor's plan: check real dimensions, products and prices before buying anything it shows.

Expected cost per run: Muse Edit $0.011, the Gemini survey roughly $0.001–0.007, the GLM calls $0.00–0.011 (the edit-brief call billed $0.0106 in the proof run — dearer than its sibling), and the Decide audit is not priced in the 2026-09-12 catalog snapshot — expect roughly $0.02–0.03 all in. Prices are observations, not caps. Live proof, 2026-11-08: the full chain ran end to end for $0.0294 metered ($43.0814 → $43.0520; balance-verified session total $0.0404 including the staging photo below, the Decide audit's ~$0.00002 unpriced in the report). Because no real room photo was staged, the proof input was itself generated with one Muse call ($0.0110) — a dated living room with a blinds window on the left wall, a wooden door on the right, a worn brown sofa, a scuffed coffee table and a dark TV stand. The survey read it back cleanly (window wall, door wall, flat beamless ceiling and camera viewpoint all in KEEP EXACTLY); the designer planned a warm Scandinavian makeover that repeated that keep-list; the render applied nearly every planned change — oatmeal linen sofa with sage cushions and a rust throw, sheer linen curtains for the blinds, light-oak coffee table and sideboard, cream woven rug with a rust-and-sage border, tripod lamp, rattan ceiling lights, the door repainted off-white in place — from the same viewpoint, with the remote still on the coffee table; and the audit verdict was **faithful redesign**, which a human look at the before/after agrees with. Run report and images: flow-hunt/runs/2026-11-08-room-makeover-audit/ in the nanoodle-dev workspace (staging: flow-hunt/runs/2026-11-08-room-staging/).

Change the **Room photo** input to any room photo taken from one corner, and edit **Redesign brief** for the style, budget and anything that must stay ("keep the piano", "the desk corner stays a workspace"). Swap the render model in the Redesigned room node's settings if you prefer a different look; keep the audit wired to the plan text, or the verdict stops meaning anything.
