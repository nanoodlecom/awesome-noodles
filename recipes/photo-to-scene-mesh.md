## Notes

One photo of a real place becomes a real 3D mesh of that place. A Gemini vision surveyor reads the photo first — setting, camera viewpoint, the main objects and surfaces in near-to-far order, and the tricky geometry a reconstruction is likely to get wrong. MoGe 3 (Microsoft's monocular geometry model, new on NanoGPT 2026-11-07) reconstructs the visible scene from that same photo into a textured GLB you can orbit. A GLM guide writer then turns the survey into a short viewing guide for the mesh: what you got and how to open it, a near-to-far walkthrough of what to look for, and the honest edges.

Why this is not Idea to 3D Model: TRELLIS invents a mesh for an object that never existed, working from a generated concept render — far side included, imagined. MoGe measures instead of inventing. It takes a photograph of a real scene and returns the geometry that one camera position actually saw, which is exactly what you want for capturing a room, a workshop bench, a market stall or a site visit as a navigable model. The flip side is stated in the guide rather than hidden: backsides and anything hidden behind other objects are absent or stretched, thin and shiny objects are approximate, glass reads unreliably, and the scale is relative — the mesh is not measured in metres. The surveyor's "tricky geometry" line exists so the guide can name the weak spots of this specific photo instead of reciting generic caveats.

What the graph surfaces is the GLB and the guide. On the raw API MoGe 3 also returns a colored point cloud, depth and validity maps and camera intrinsics; the graph's 3D node exposes the mesh only, so those stay API-only for now.

Costs and sizes, observed 2026-11-12: MoGe billed $0.015 per started input megapixel in both runs — $0.03 for a 1024×1024 photo (2 started MP), $0.045 for a 1152×2048 portrait (3 started MP) — the Gemini survey about $0.0013 and the GLM guide $0.00, so expect roughly $0.03–0.05 per run at photo sizes like these. The files are heavy: the room mesh came back at 58 MB (1,014,043 vertices, 1,996,394 triangles, one embedded texture) and the portrait mesh at 130 MB (2,289,662 vertices, 4,535,772 triangles). Lower the resolution_level in the 3D node's settings if you want a lighter file at some detail cost. Prices are observations, not caps.

Live proof, 2026-11-12 (NanoGPT updates watch, this graph's first runs): two full runs end to end through the headless runner. Run 1, a miniature attic-room diorama photo (1024×1024), $0.0313 metered total: the guide names the plank floor, brass telescope, sewing table with its red machine, hanging lantern, crescent-moon sculpture and porthole in the photo's own depth order, and flags tripod legs, lantern glass and the sewing needle as approximate. Run 2, a kitchen selfie (1152×2048), $0.0462: the guide walks from the potted basil in hand, to the woman — curly hair called out as the least exact area — to counters and cabinetry, to the windowsill plants. Both GLBs parse as valid glTF 2.0 with real geometry and one embedded texture each; the meshes were verified by parsing, not rendered, so judge surface quality by opening one. Two honest wrinkles: one earlier attempt lost the survey node to a provider read timeout and the clean re-run is the evidence above — a transport failure, not a graph failure; and the portrait guide kept the three-paragraph shape but dropped the paragraph labels the room guide kept. Run reports and outputs: nanogpt-watch/runs/photo-to-scene-mesh-room/ and photo-to-scene-mesh-portrait/ in the nanoodle-dev workspace.

Change the **Scene photo** input: interiors and cluttered workspaces reconstruct best, because depth order is the whole game. A single product-style object on a plain background is Idea to 3D Model's job, not this one's. Keep the survey wired to the guide — an unguided 58 MB mesh is a file most people open once and abandon; the walkthrough is what makes it a result.
