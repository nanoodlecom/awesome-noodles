## Notes

One photo of one object becomes a textured triangle mesh you can actually use downstream. A Gemini survey names the object, its materials and the sides the photo hides; Idol Max Turbo (speridlabs/idol/max/turbo, Sperid Labs) rebuilds it as a 30,000-triangle GLB with UVs and embedded PBR textures; a GLM guide states plainly what you got, where it drops in, and what was invented. This is the mesh sibling of Photo to 3D Splat — same survey spine, opposite output: surfaces, not points.

Why this model, why now: Idol Max and Idol Max Turbo landed in NanoGPT's 3D catalog on 2026-11-18, answering the original Idol's one structural limit — a splat is not a mesh, and no slicer, engine or UV editor accepts one (the base model's own catalog text now says its output is "neither a textured polygon mesh"). Every serious write-up of the splat routed mesh-needing readers to TripoSR or TRELLIS instead. The Demand Watch's cycle 4 found the Max line hours after it landed; the probe below is why this example exists.

The probe came first, and the claim was checked in the file, not the listing: one live Turbo run ($0.10, PBR on) returned a GLB whose first primitive is mode 4 (triangles) with exactly 30,000 triangles, POSITION / TEXCOORD_0 / NORMAL / TANGENT attributes, and three embedded textures (base colour, normal, metallic-roughness). The saved run's GLB parses the same way at 29,992 triangles. PBR is included in the $0.10 — it is a switch (enable_pbr), not a surcharge. Rigging is the model's other switch and this graph leaves it off on purpose: the demo subject is a rigid statue, the catalog warns rigid props may not rig, and a failed rig fails the whole generation (refunded, but wasted). Rigged runs cost $0.125 when a character is the subject.

The graph exports the **Rebuild as a mesh — Idol Max Turbo** model and the **Mesh guide — GLM writes what you got** text.

Defaults are one Turbo generation at $0.10 with PBR on, one Gemini survey and a GLM guide. The saved run reported $0.1029 all-in; the probe before the build cost $0.10. Prices are observations, not caps.

The saved run is in the nanoodle gallery (https://nanoodle.com/examples/gallery/#photo-to-game-ready-mesh): the same golden dragon statue the splat example used, rebuilt as a mesh — antiqued bronze with dark patina, wings, coiled body and plinth all present as textured surfaces, and the guide names the invented sides (dorsal neck and spine, rear wing surfaces, the base's underside) and the budget honestly: fine detail lives in the 2048px texture maps, not in 30,000 triangles, so whiskers, horns and wing membranes are approximate. Unlike the splat, the mesh GLB arrives Y-up and renders in the stock viewer — no points path needed. Swap the 📷 input for your own photo of one object — whole, in frame, uncluttered background works best.
