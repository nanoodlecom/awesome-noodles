## Notes

Some scenes survive being passed hand to hand; most quietly shed their details at every handoff. This flow measures that shedding directly. Give it a seed scene (the default is a small harbour cafe terrace at sunrise) and a GLM director writes a SCENE CARD: the setting, a fixed photorealistic sunrise style, and exactly 6 numbered elements, each one concrete thing with a colour, count or position a reader can check. A second GLM turns the card into a painter prompt and Muse paints generation 1. Then the picture stops travelling and only words move. A blind Gemini reader inventories generation 1 and writes a recreation prompt meant to let another painter rebuild the picture from words alone; a GLM relay clerk extracts that prompt verbatim; Muse paints generation 2 having never seen generation 1. A second reader and clerk repeat the handoff into generation 3. A GLM drift auditor compares the three blind inventories against the card and scores survival at EACH hop - HOP 1, HOP 2 and HOP 3, X of 6 - names the hop at which each element was first lost, and lists anything the chain invented that was never on the card. A Decide judge (PPLX Decider) sees the final generation and all the texts and headlines the trajectory: **survived the relay**, **drifted slowly**, **collapsed mid-relay**, or **mutated beyond recognition**.

Why the loop matters: the catalog already repaints a picture from words once (words only, repainted), and audits that single hop slot by slot. A single hop cannot distinguish a scene that is robust to redescription from one that merely got lucky once, and it cannot say WHERE a detail died. Here the chain feeds on its own output twice more, so loss compounds and localises: an element lost at hop 1 was fragile in the first description, an element lost at hop 3 survived two full cross-modal round trips, and inventions accumulate in the open instead of being averaged into one fidelity score. The audited object is the trajectory, not any one picture.

Honest limits, in the open: the relay is unrolled, not looped - three generations are wired by hand because Nanoodle graphs have no loops, and a fourth hop would need the same reader/clerk/painter block pasted again. The relay clerk is trusted to copy the recreation prompt verbatim; if it silently edits, the next painter receives edited words and the audit still scores the outcome, but the hop's loss is partly the clerk's, and the flow says so rather than hiding the clerk inside the reader. Survival is judged on the readers' inventories, so an element a painter kept but a reader failed to mention scores as lost - that is the relay working as designed, because in a words-only chain an unmentioned element really is gone for the next painter. Counts and positions are the most fragile cargo by construction; the card is deliberately limited to 6 bold elements so the trajectory is readable rather than noise.

Live proof (2026-11-17, flow-hunt 18:30 session): ONE live run, SUCCESS end to end, all nodes done, no errors, no staging spend - $0.0514 balance-verified ($41.2400 -> $41.1886; metered $0.0505: 3x Muse $0.033, Gemini readers $0.00550 / $0.00591 / $0.00610, GLM calls $0.00; Decide unpriced in the report, cost_exact=false). The director's card for the default harbour-cafe seed: six red-and-white parasols, a blue awning, three white tables with yellow chairs, a grey stone quay wall, two green fishing boats, a pink-orange sunrise. Generation 1 is visually verified as exactly that scene. The relay then drifted in the open: reader 1 counted SEVEN parasols, so the auditor's exact-count rule scored element 1 lost at hop 1 even though parasols stand in every generation (they multiply further, to eight closed ones, by generation 3); reader 2 dropped the tables' count ('several'), losing element 3 at hop 2; reader 3 moved the sunrise behind a mountain ridge, losing element 6 at hop 3. The auditor's trajectory: HOP 1: 4 of 6, HOP 2: 3 of 6, HOP 3: 2 of 6, INVENTED: grey stone village houses, green hills, low morning mist - all three inventions are pixel-true (reader 1 described the background houses gen 1 already shows, and each later painter promoted them). Decide v1.2 returned 'mutated beyond recognition', following its own rule (hop 3 keeps 2 or fewer) - an honest overstatement against the pixels, since generation 3 is plainly the same harbour, boats, pier, canopy and chairs; the strict count-and-position scoring, not the scene, is what collapsed, and the NOTE records that rather than rounding the verdict down.
