## Notes

A kaleidoscope is a promise about reflection: one motif, repeated by mirrors, so that what appears in one quadrant is not a copy of its neighbour but its flipped twin. Image models break that promise in a way that is almost invisible. Asked for a four-quadrant mandala, a painter draws the motif once and stamps it into the remaining quadrants unflipped - every shape in the right cell, every colour right, the design handsome and plausible - while the chiral details, the shapes that have a handedness, point the wrong way in half the picture. A crescent that should open toward its own quadrant's corner opens toward its neighbour's; a comma whose tip should point out to the left points right, into the design. Nothing is missing and nothing is misplaced, so a read that scores only shapes and positions certifies the stamp as a mirror. The failure lives purely in orientation under reflection, and it can only be caught by an audit that treats reflection as a law with testable consequences, not as a vibe. This flow makes the law explicit, in a form a blind reader can check cell by cell. Give it a plain backdrop and a GLM director picks one mandala from a menu printed in its own prompt (EMBER, FROST, GARDEN, MIDNIGHT - four flat medallions, each a ground, a centre ring, and one four-shape motif in four colours) and copies it onto a KALEIDO CARD. The card's key move is how it names places: not by picture coordinates, which change meaning from quadrant to quadrant, but by centre-relative cells that mean the same thing everywhere - the CENTRE-CELL nearest the picture's centre holds a filled circle, the CORNER-CELL nearest the quadrant's own outer corner holds a diamond, the EDGE-CELL nearer the top or bottom edge holds a crescent, the SIDE-CELL nearer the side edge holds a comma. Under true reflection, all four quadrants described in those cells read identically; what differs is only absolute direction - the crescent opens toward each quadrant's own corner, and the comma's tip points outward, right in the right-hand quadrants and left in the left-hand ones. A second GLM turns the card into a painter prompt that states the motif quadrant by quadrant in full, each quadrant as the mirror of the top-right with its flips named, and forbids dividing lines: the symmetry must live in the motif, not in a drawn grid the painter can hide behind. Muse paints one flat square medallion. A blind Gemini reader, who never saw the card, cards each quadrant separately in the same centre-relative cells - shape and colour per cell - and reports the two orientations as absolute picture directions: each comma's tip (LEFT, RIGHT, UP or DOWN) and each crescent's opening (one of the four diagonals), plus the centre ring and any seams. A GLM auditor converts those absolute directions back into centre-relative ones per quadrant and scores ELEMENTS (shape and colour in each of the 16 cells), QUADRANTS (all four cells right), CENTRE (the ring present in its carded colour), MIRRORS (quadrants where both orientation checks obey the mirror law) and SYMMETRY (TRUE MIRROR only when every quadrant matches and every orientation obeys the law; STAMPED when the elements are essentially right but the orientations repeat one absolute direction across quadrants - copies, not reflections; PARTIAL or BROKEN otherwise), with SEAMS scored as a leak: a painter that draws the centre cross has drawn a grid, not a symmetry. A Decide judge (PPLX Decider) headlines round 1 as a side output - **mirrors true**, **stamped not mirrored**, **quadrants drifted**, **centre or seams wrong**, or **not a mandala** - but the deliverable is the corrected medallion.

That verdict is not where this flow stops - the loop closes, per the standing rule that a created asset ends in the corrected asset, not in text. A GLM editor reads the card, the read and the audit and writes ONE concrete edit instruction: the full target end state (the ground, the centre ring, the four shapes by cell and colour) plus the audit's specific failures as quadrant-level repaint demands - repaint the drifted quadrant with the carded shapes in the carded cells, repaint the stamped quadrant as a true mirror image with its crescent opening toward its own corner and its comma's tip pointing out toward its own side edge, restore the centre ring, remove any seams - while every quadrant that already matches the card in cells and orientations is left exactly as drawn. The editor is gated in content, a rule this catalog learned the hard way from Molecule Check, whose flawless round-1 model was recoloured by a repair it never needed: on a perfect audit the editor must demand a verbatim unchanged copy. Muse Edit repaints the failing quadrants, a blind re-read cards the corrected medallion, and the same auditor re-scores it against the same card. The flow's final output is the corrected medallion itself.

The honest limits are stated in the open. The audit certifies reflection across the two centre lines - the Klein four-group of flips - not the full dihedral group: a design whose quadrants obey both flips automatically obeys the half-turn, but the diagonal reflections and quarter-turns that would relate a quadrant to its edge-neighbour through the centre point are not separately scored, and the card does not claim them. Centre-relative cells are a deliberate scaffolding: they make the mirror law checkable by a reader who never has to perform a mental flip, at the cost of granularity - each cell is a quadrant quarter, so a shape that sits in the right cell but drifts within it passes, and only the two orientation-bearing shapes, the crescent and the comma, carry the chirality verdict.
