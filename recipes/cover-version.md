## Notes

Every other audio flow in this catalog either builds a song from text or cleans a voice back toward itself. This one hands a finished recording to a different model and asks it to re-perform the song — then checks the one thing a cover is supposed to keep. A GLM lyricist writes a short original song from your brief (one verse, one chorus, a hook line the brief names) and a GLM producer writes the original's music prompt. MiniMax Music 3 sings it as a warm indie-folk ballad, and Whisper transcribes the performance — what was actually sung, not what was written. Both tracks pass through a Trim step that keeps their first 90 seconds before transcription: the music model chooses the song's length, and a long high-bitrate track outgrows the transcription endpoint's size limit, as the first live attempt proved (below). The Remix node (MiniMax Music Cover) then takes that actual audio, the written lyrics, and a style brief that says change everything about the sound and nothing about the song, and re-performs it as bright synth-pop. Whisper transcribes the cover as well, and a GLM lyric auditor scores the cover transcript against the original transcript line by line — KEPT, GARBLED (with the cover's words) or DROPPED for each of the song's 8 lines, INVENTED lines for anything the cover sings that the original never did, ending in "X of 8 lines kept". A ⚖️ Decide judge (PPLX Decider) gives the headline verdict: **lyrics survived**, **words drifted**, **half the song lost**, or **different song**.

Why the loop matters: a cover model is a generative re-performance, not an effect — it re-sings the song with a new voice, tempo and arrangement, and its one contractual obligation is the melody and the words. Nobody can hear a lyric drift from the style brief; the drift only exists between two performances, so the audit compares exactly those: performance against performance, with the written lyrics used only to segment the song into lines. That also keeps the score fair — if the original singer slurs a word, the cover is graded against the slur that was actually sung, not against the page. The nearest catalog neighbour, the clean-voice rescue, runs speech through a denoiser whose ideal output is the same take, cleaner; here the ideal output is a different take of the same song, and the audit has to separate the intended difference (genre, voice, tempo) from the unintended one (lost or garbled words) — a distinction no earlier flow has had to make.

Honest limits, in the open: Whisper is the ear on both sides, and sung vocals are its hardest input — a line it mishears in the original becomes the ground truth the cover is graded against, and a line it mishears in the cover reads as GARBLED even if the singer nailed it; the audit measures transcript-to-transcript survival, which is the checkable proxy for lyric survival, not lyric survival itself. The cover model receives the written lyrics as an input, which stacks the deck in its favour compared with a human cover artist working from hearing alone — that is the model's designed contract (it preserves melody from the audio and words from the lyrics), and the audit still catches what it does with them. Song length is the music model's choice (MiniMax Music 3 has no duration parameter), so the lyricist is capped at 8 short lines, and both tracks are trimmed to their first 90 seconds before transcription — identically, so the comparison stays fair; a performance that saved its chorus for after the 90-second mark would be graded on what came before it. Melody preservation — the cover model's other promise — is not scored at all: nothing in this chain listens for tune, only for words, and a cover that kept every word over a different melody would pass this audit clean.

Expected cost per run: the two MiniMax music calls dominate — the original at the $0.15 observed for Music 3 in this catalog's Sing runs, the cover at its $0.15 catalog minimum — plus two Whisper transcriptions (cents at most) and the GLM calls at effectively $0.00, with the Decide audit unpriced in the run report; roughly $0.31–0.40 all in. Prices are observations, not caps.

Change the **Song brief** to any subject a short song can carry, and the **Cover style** to any genre far enough from indie folk to make the re-performance real — the wider the style gap, the harder the words have to work to survive it. Keep the song at verse-plus-chorus length: a longer lyric gives the cover more lines to lose and the auditor a longer tail to grade.

Session spend $0.3000 balance-verified. The repair is the pair of Trim guards described above (first 90 seconds, converted locally to mono 16 kHz WAV at ~2.9 MB worst case, safely under the limit no matter how long a performance the music model chooses).
