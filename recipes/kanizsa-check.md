## Notes

A Kanizsa figure is a shape that is seen but not drawn: a triangle seems to float over three notched discs, its edges crossing bare ground where no line exists. That makes it a uniquely two-sided audit problem for an image model. A painter asked for the illusion can fail in opposite directions at once - realign nothing and draw nothing, so no shape is perceived at all, or cheat by drawing the triangle outright, so a shape is plainly 'seen' that was never induced. A single read cannot separate those outcomes: a reader who reports the floating shape cannot swear there is no ink under it, and a reader who inventories the discs cannot say whether the shape appears. This flow splits the question between two blind readers who are forbidden each other's evidence, and certifies only their conjunction. Give it a plain backdrop and a GLM director picks one figure from a menu printed in its own prompt (EMBER TRIANGLE from three charcoal discs, FROST SQUARE from four cobalt discs, GARDEN PENTAGON from five plum discs) and copies it onto a KANIZSA CARD: the ground, the discs' colour and count, their corner positions, and the notch law - every disc is a full circle with one wedge notch cut out, the notch being exactly the wedge between the two sides of the perceived polygon meeting at that corner, its straight edges lying along those sides, its mouth facing the picture's centre, so the polygon's corners hide inside the discs, its edges cross bare ground, and no part of it is drawn. A second GLM turns the card into a painter prompt that demands the discs and their aligned notches in full and forbids the shape itself: no outline, no connecting lines, nothing in the centre. Muse paints only the discs.

A blind Gemini PERCEIVED reader, who never saw the card, glances at the picture as a person would and reports the first shape it seems to see floating in the centre, whether its edges look sharp, and whether it seems to float in front of the ground; it is told not to count or analyse, and not to talk itself out of a shape it plainly seems to see. A second blind Gemini LITERAL reader, who also never saw the card, is forbidden to name any floating shape at all: it counts the discs, names their colour, checks each disc's notch - ALIGNED only when the notch's two straight edges lie along the sides of the polygon corner at that disc with its mouth facing the centre - and swears whether any outline is drawn around the centre and whether any ink fills it. A GLM auditor derives the law from the card itself (TRIANGLE needs 3 discs, SQUARE 4, PENTAGON 5) and scores INDUCERS, NOTCHES, DRAWN (CLEAN only when outline and centre ink are both NONE; LEAKED when either is present), PERCEIVED (the perceiver's shape, copied) and ILLUSION: HOLDS only when the perceived shape is the carded figure AND the drawing is CLEAN AND every notch is aligned AND every disc is present; FAKED when the shape is perceived but ink was drawn to make it; UNALIGNED when the drawing is clean and the shape appears but a notch or disc fails; NOT PERCEIVED when the discs are right and nothing floats; BROKEN otherwise. A Decide judge (PPLX Decider) headlines round 1 as a side output - **illusion holds**, **shape drawn not illusory**, **notches unaligned**, **nothing perceived**, or **not a diagram** - but the deliverable is the corrected diagram.

That verdict is not where this flow stops - the loop closes, per the standing rule that a created asset ends in the corrected asset, not in text. A GLM editor reads the card, both reads and the audit and writes ONE concrete edit instruction: the full target end state (the ground, the discs by colour, count and corner) plus the audit's specific failures as repaint demands - realign the stray disc's notch so its edges lie along the shape's sides at that corner with its mouth facing the centre, erase any drawn outline or centre shape back to bare flat ground, restore a missing disc - while every disc that already matches the card is left exactly as drawn, and the editor is forbidden ever to demand the perceived shape be drawn, outlined or shaded in: the repair makes the illusion more honest, never more literal. The editor is gated in content, a rule this catalog learned the hard way from Molecule Check, whose flawless round-1 model was recoloured by a repair it never needed: on a perfect audit the editor must demand a verbatim unchanged copy. Muse Edit repaints, both blind readers re-read the corrected diagram, and the same auditor re-scores it against the same card. The flow's final output is the corrected diagram itself.

The honest limits are stated in the open. The perceiver is a vision model reporting an impression, not a human visual system: PERCEIVED certifies that this reader, prompted to glance and not analyse, named the carded shape - strong evidence the alignment reads at a glance, not proof a person sees it, and a reader that has learned what Kanizsa figures are may complete a shape a naive eye would miss. The literal reader's ALIGNED is a judgement about drawn edges at painterly resolution: a notch a few degrees off can read as aligned, and a crisp notch can be failed on strictness, so NOTCHES is the softest score in the audit. The conjunction cuts the other way too: DRAWN is only as strong as the literal reader's eyes - a very faint outline could pass as NONE, which is why FAKED also requires the perceiver's shape to match before ink is even the question. The three figures are menu variety, not coverage: a painter could induce the triangle and draw the square, and only the run that happens is evidence. One run proves the chain - director, brief, paint, dual blind reads, conjunction audit, gated repair, re-audit - end to end on the figure the director happened to pick; it does not prove every figure on the menu survives its painter.
