## Notes

Every flow in this catalog starts from a still image or from text. This one starts from motion: upload a short clip and it comes back as a comic page — with a score for whether the story still reads in the right order. A frame sampler cuts the clip into 6 frames spread across its length. A Gemini story clerk watches them in order and writes a STORY LOG: one ANCHORS line (the subject, its colours, the setting) and exactly 4 EVENT lines in chronological order, each ending with a caption of 2–4 words. A GLM page designer compresses the log into a single comic-page prompt — a 2 by 2 grid, one event per panel in reading order (top left, top right, bottom left, bottom right), the anchors identical in every panel, each panel's caption copied word for word — and Muse paints the page from that prompt alone. A second Gemini then inventories the finished page panel by panel in reading order, transcribing each caption, and a GLM order auditor scores the log against that read-back event by event — IN ORDER, SWAPPED (naming the panel the event actually landed in) or MISSING, with the caption on the same line marked KEPT, GARBLED or LOST, plus INVENTED lines for any panel action the log never had, ending in "X of 4 in place, captions Y of 4 kept". A ⚖️ Decide judge (PPLX Decider) gives the headline verdict: **story in order**, **panels shuffled**, **events missing**, or **different story**.

Why the loop matters: flattening time into space has one classic failure — the beats survive but their order doesn't, and a shuffled comic tells a different story with the same pictures. No still-image audit can even ask that question. Here the question is mechanical: the clerk's log fixes the chronology before any drawing happens, the reader inventories panels in reading order without seeing the log's verdict, and the auditor compares two texts, so a SWAPPED line names exactly which two beats traded places. The clerk, the designer, the painter, the reader and the auditor are four model families doing four different jobs, and the painter works from the designer's prompt, never from the clip — what reaches the page is what the words carried. The nearest catalog neighbour, the composer that watches your film, also samples frames and has a vision model write a brief from them, but it turns time into music and never checks the result; this one turns time into panels and checks the one thing music doesn't have — sequence.

Honest limits, in the open: 6 sampled frames are a sampling, not the film — a beat that starts and ends between two frames never reaches the log and so can never be scored missing; pick clips whose action is slower than the sampling (the frame gap is 2 seconds at the defaults) or raise the frame count and narrow the gap. Four events is a deliberate cap: a busier clip gets compressed, and the clerk's choice of which four beats count IS the story the audit grades — a different clerk could log a different four. Captions are the lettering risk every image model carries: 2–4 words is short enough to usually survive, and the audit's GARBLED line exists precisely because sometimes they don't. Panel reading order is the grid convention (left to right, top to bottom); a painter that lays the grid out in columns instead of rows will read as shuffled even if its own order is consistent — that, too, is a real finding about the page a reader is handed. The default clip is the gallery's domino-chain film (a coloured chain on a kitchen table that falls in a wave and topples a small silver bell, 10 seconds), reused as the staging input and disclosed here rather than passed off as a fresh shoot; swap in your own clip and the same audit runs. Both GLM calls and the clerk are capped (maxTokens 300–600, reasoningEffort low, the pairing this catalog's repair history landed on) so a detailed log cannot run a generation past the HTTP timeout.

Expected cost per run: one Gemini frame read (6 frames), one Muse page and one Gemini page read dominate — roughly $0.02–0.06 all in, the two GLM calls effectively $0.00, and the Decide audit unpriced in the run report. The frame sampling itself is a local step and costs nothing. Prices are observations, not caps.

Change the **Clip** input to any short clip with a visible sequence of events — a pour, a build, a fall, a reveal. Keep the sampling slower than the action (frames × gap should span the clip), keep the log at 4 events, and keep the reader's panel order identical to the designer's grid, or the order score stops meaning anything.

Live proof, 2026-11-13: the full chain ran end to end on its first attempt for $0.0196 balance-verified ($18.2436 → $18.2240, exactly the metered sum: clerk $0.0053, page $0.0110, page read-back $0.0033, both GLM calls $0.00, Decide unpriced in the report, cost_exact=false; no staging spend — the domino clip is a reused gallery film). The comic page is visually verified: a clean 2 by 2 grid, the domino curve and silver bell consistent across all four panels, and all four captions letter-perfect — "Bell gets hit", "Moving through middle", "Sweeping the curve", "Bell stands still". Both judges agreed at the top: the order audit scored 4 of 4 in place, captions 4 of 4 kept, and Decide returned **story in order**. The honest flaw sits one step earlier, and it is the flaw this flow cannot itself catch: the audit grades the page against the clerk's log, and the clerk's log is an imperfect read of the film — its EVENT 1 has a fallen domino striking the bell "at the track's start", but the sampled first frame shows the whole chain standing with the bell upright (the gallery's own review of this clip records the bell going over mid-run, and a second bell appearing later, which the log folds into its later events). The page is a faithful comic of the log; the log is a compressed, partly rearranged account of the clip. That is recorded here rather than smoothed over: the order score certifies log-to-page fidelity, and the clerk remains the single point of chronological truth.
