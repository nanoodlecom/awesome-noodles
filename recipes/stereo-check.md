## Notes

Depth in a stereo pair is not painted; it is displaced. Two eyes see the same scene from slightly different places, and the brain reads the horizontal shift between the two views — binocular disparity — as depth: near objects shift a lot, far objects barely shift, and whatever sits at infinity does not shift at all. Dedicated stereo generators (GenStereo, ICCV 2025) are judged on exactly that disparity consistency, and the ComfyUI stereo nodes (ComfyStereo, SSStereoscope) build pairs mechanically, by shifting pixels against a depth map — a preprocessor Nanoodle does not have. This flow asks the harder question of a general generator: can it encode depth as displacement itself, in one generation, with nothing but words? Give it a setting (the default is a paper-theatre diorama of a harbour lane, with one round sun painted on the backdrop itself) and a GLM director writes a DEPTH CARD: exactly 5 free-standing pieces chosen from a menu of 10 (lighthouse, cottage, pine tree, rowboat on a stand, bicycle, lamppost, postbox, windmill, fountain, small dog), a distinct colour each, a distinct left-to-right SLOT and a distinct DEPTH RANK from 1 (nearest) to 5 (farthest), with slots and ranks deliberately independent, and a fixed planned shift per rank — 8, 6, 4, 2, 1 percent of the panel width — plus a CONTROL line: the painted backdrop sun, planned shift exactly 0. A second GLM turns the card into one painter prompt and Muse paints a SIDE-BY-SIDE STEREO PAIR in a single generation: two equal panels split by a thin black divider, the same diorama in both, every piece the same size and height in both panels, the only difference a pure horizontal shift in the right panel that shrinks with depth. A blind Gemini surveyor, who never saw the card, reports each piece's centre in each panel as a percentage of that panel's width, plus the sun's. A GLM auditor subtracts the pairs and runs five scores no catalog audit runs: IDENTITY (X of 5 pieces found in both panels), SHIFTS (each piece's measured disparity, printed in depth-rank order), SHIFT (X of 5 disparities within 3 points of planned), DEPTH ORDER (X of 4 adjacent rank pairs where the nearer piece's disparity is strictly greater), CONTROL (the backdrop sun's disparity is 3 or less — it must not move), and FLAT (the largest disparity is 2 or less, meaning the pair is a flat copy). A ⚖️ Decide judge (PPLX Decider) sees the pair and all the texts and gives the headline verdict: **true stereo**, **depth scrambled**, **flat copy**, or **wrong diorama**.

Why the loop matters: every other spatial audit in this catalog measures inside one frame — scale check measures height shares, shadow check a shadow against its caster, mirror check a reflection against its source. Disparity is a quantity that only exists BETWEEN two frames, so a generator cannot fake it with a single pretty picture: it has to hold one scene steady across two viewpoints and move each piece by a different, depth-correct amount, which is the whole of stereopsis in one test. The zero-shift control is the second new mechanism: the painted sun is a baseline inside the same picture, so if the painter or the surveyor drifts globally, the control drifts with it and the audit can see its own measurement noise instead of mistaking it for depth. And FLAT COPY names the signature failure in advance — asked for two eyes, a flat-picture generator paints the same panel twice — a failure no single-frame audit can even state.

Honest limits, in the open: the surveyor's centres are visual estimates, not pixel measurements — expect a few points of reader noise, which is why SHIFT carries a ±3 band, DEPTH ORDER only asks for a strict inequality between neighbours, and the planned shifts are spaced 8/6/4/2/1 so the order can survive noise that the exact values cannot. The sign of the shift is not scored, only its size: the brief asks for a leftward shift in the right panel (the parallel-camera convention), but a consistent rightward shift encodes the same depth, and scoring direction would punish a geometrically true pair for a convention. The two panels are one generation, not two — that is the point (one scene, held steady, displaced per piece) — but it means a painter that simply draws two unrelated dioramas fails IDENTITY before disparity is even computed, and the audit says so rather than averaging the two failures together. Finally, the depth here is staged, not natural: five unoccluding cardboard pieces on one board, because disparity is only cleanly measurable when every piece is fully visible in both panels; a cluttered real scene, where near pieces hide far ones differently in each eye, is the harder problem this test deliberately does not claim.
