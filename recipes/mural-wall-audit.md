## Notes

Mural preview tools paint the whole rectangle: upload a wall, describe a mural, and the render happily paints over the window, moves the door and invents a skirting board — a mural you could never actually paint on that wall. This graph lets the wall set the canvas first. A Gemini surveyor writes a WALL CARD: the wall divided into exactly 5 numbered vertical zones, each marked completely blank or occupied by its fixture, a Fixed line for everything that is not paintable wall, and a Paintable line naming only the blank zones. A GLM designer may paint ONLY those zones — every other zone is KEEP — planning one coherent scene zone by zone with countable motifs at exact counts, and a second GLM compresses the plan into a surgical edit instruction that starts with the keep-exactly list. Muse Edit paints the mural onto the original photo. Then the loop closes: a second Gemini read-back inventories the rendered wall zone by zone with counts, a GLM auditor scores plan against read-back (APPLIED / WRONG ZONE / PAINTED OVER / WRONG COUNT / MISSING, a Fixed-list KEPT-or-CHANGED pass, and an "X of 5 zones, counts A of B exact" summary), and a ⚖️ Decide judge (PPLX Decider) gives the headline verdict: **faithful mural**, **motifs drifted**, **fixtures painted over**, or **barely a mural**.

Why the loop matters: the classic mural failure is not ugly art, it is art in the wrong place — a beautiful valley that eats the window. A text plan alone cannot catch that, and a pretty render alone hides it. Splitting the jobs across four model families is what makes the check honest: the model that surveyed the wall is not the model that designed the mural, neither of them painted it, and the read-back checker never sees the plan — it only reports what is visibly on the wall, so the auditor compares two independent accounts. The zone numbers are the trick that makes "wrong place" mechanically checkable instead of a vibe.

Honest limits, in the open: the zones are equal vertical fifths, a surveyor's grid, not the wall's architecture — a fixture straddling a zone boundary lands in one zone's line and the designer treats that whole zone as unpaintable, which is conservative but can waste paintable wall. The read-back reads a 512 px shrink of the render, so small motifs at zone edges can be counted into the wrong zone, and its "(?)" guesses flow straight into the audit — read the WRONG COUNT and WRONG ZONE lines against the render yourself. Motif counting has the usual drift: the live proof's counts are recorded below whether they flatter the render or not. And the render is a preview, not a painting contract: it proves the design fits this wall's blank zones, not that a ladder, the plaster texture and real paint will cooperate.

Expected cost per run: Muse Edit $0.011, the two Gemini vision calls roughly $0.004–0.009 each, the three GLM calls effectively $0.00–0.011, and the Decide audit is not priced in the 2026-09-12 catalog snapshot — expect roughly $0.03–0.05 all in, plus a staging photo if you generate one. Prices are observations, not caps.

The road there is part of the record: the 10:30 and 14:30 attempts both died at the edit-brief GLM node with the same chat/completions read timeout, and an isolated re-run of that single node (same model, same system prompt, the real designer plan as input) timed out a third time at $0.00, while a trivial GLM call on the same input returned in about 5 seconds — so the call, not the network, the payload size or the model in general. Diagnosis, all at $0.00: the original brief succeeded on a short synthetic plan, a reworded brief still timed out on the real plan, and two variants succeeded in seconds on the real plan — the brief on GLM-5.3 (non-Flash), and the original Flash brief with an output cap. The uncapped Flash generation was running away on detailed real plans, past the 120 s read timeout. Repair applied: the brief node now carries maxTokens 300 and reasoningEffort low (the same pairing other catalog nodes use; its brief is by design under 150 words), system prompt unchanged. In the proof run the surveyor carded the staging wall exactly as built (window zone 1, door zone 5, Paintable: 2, 3, 4); the designer planned KEEP zones 1 and 5 with a dawn valley across 2–4 (exactly 3 dawn clouds, exactly 5 black swifts, exactly 7 wildflowers); Muse Edit's render is visually verified: the mural covers only the blank wall — window, door, ceiling light and floor untouched — with the sun disc centred, 5 birds in the sky and about 7 main wildflowers along the bottom.
