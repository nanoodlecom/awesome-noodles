## Notes

A melody is not a picture of notes - it is a code with a law. Pitch lives in the vertical position of a note head inside the staff alphabet (the third space is C5, the line above it D5 - neighbouring positions are different symbols, not more or less of a quantity), duration lives in an independent second channel (a filled head is one beat, an open head is two), and the time signature imposes an arithmetic conservation law on top: in 4/4, the durations inside each measure must sum to exactly four beats, so a score can look beautiful and still not be the melody - one head filled that should be open and the measure no longer adds up. This flow makes that code a planned, measurable quantity - and then it does something about a violation. Give it a score setting and a GLM director picks one melody from a short menu (MORNING BELL, RIVER STEPS, GARDEN GATE, LANTERN SONG) whose full spec is printed in its own prompt - every note as pitch plus duration, the exact measure split, each measure's beat sum - and copies it onto a SCORE CARD, so the card cannot invent music. A second GLM turns the card into one engraver prompt that demands a single large treble staff on cream paper: five lines, a treble clef, each head on its pitch's exact line or space, filled heads for quarter notes and open heads for half notes, one bar line after exactly the measure 1 notes - and no printed pitch letters, note names, lyrics or title anywhere, because print would cheat the staff code: the melody must exist only as note heads. Muse paints it. A blind Gemini reader, who never saw the melody or the card, enumerates every note head left to right - pitch from the head's line-or-space position, duration from its fill, measure from the bar line. A GLM auditor scores NOTE COUNT, PITCHES position by position, DURATIONS position by position, MEASURE SPLIT, MEASURE SUMS (each rendered measure's beats against the 4 the time signature demands), the DECODED MELODY the heads actually spell, and TEXT LEAK. A Decide judge headlines round 1: plays as written, label cheat, rhythm broken, notes drifted, or unreadable score.

That verdict is not where this flow stops - the loop closes, per the standing rule that a created asset ends in the corrected asset, not in text. A GLM editor reads the card, the read and the audit and writes ONE concrete edit instruction: the full target end state (the card's notes in the card's order, every head on its carded line or space at its carded fill, the bar line after exactly the measure 1 notes, each measure summing to four beats, no print anywhere) plus the audit's specific failures as extra demands - move the third head up one space to C5, open the fifth head's centre to make it a half note, shift the bar line one note to the right - locating each fix by its position in the sequence. The editor is gated in content, a rule this catalog learned the hard way from Molecule Check, whose flawless round-1 model was recoloured by a repair it never needed: on a perfect audit the editor must demand a verbatim unchanged copy and forbid any note being moved, redrawn or restyled. Muse Edit applies the notes to the round-1 picture. The corrected score is the flow's deliverable. A second blind reader re-enumerates it, and the same auditor scores round 2 against the same card, so the fix is itself audited and the reader can see exactly what the edit bought - or cost. The Decide verdict stays, demoted to a side output on round 1.

Why this axis is new: the catalog's nearest neighbour is bar-chart-audit, and the difference is mechanical. A bar's height is an analog quantity read against a continuous scale, scored inside a tolerance band; a note's position is a discrete symbol in a nine-position staff alphabet where one step changes the pitch's NAME, not its amount - and the same picture carries a second, independent symbolic channel (head fill for duration) plus a per-measure arithmetic law (the beat sum) that no bar chart, braille cell or molecule in the catalog enforces. Braille encodes letters by dot position in a cell and molecule-check sums bond orders against valence budgets; neither reads a two-channel notation or checks that grouped durations conserve a total. Image models are notorious for decorative pseudo-notation - staff lines with note-like blobs that spell nothing - and here that gap is not a vibe: it is PITCHES, DURATIONS and MEASURE SUMS, each failure located by sequence position.

Honest limits, in the open: this is notation fidelity only - phrasing, dynamics, articulation, key signatures and accidentals are deliberately out of scope; the menu uses natural notes only (no sharps or flats), one treble staff, quarter and half notes only, because each added symbol is another channel the read must carry. Pitch is judged by the head's centre against the staff grid, so a head straddling a line-space boundary is a genuine naming border for the reader; the read records the call it made. The auditor compares sequences position by position, not by musical similarity: a melody shifted by one note scores badly even if it sounds close, which is the point - the card is exact. The fix round has its own limit: an edit model asked to move one head can nudge a neighbour - that is exactly what round 2's PITCHES and MEASURE SUMS lines are for, and both audits are printed so the reader can see the trade. One repair round is unrolled, not a loop - if round 2 still violates, the flow shows the violation rather than spending again.
