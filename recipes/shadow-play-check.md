## Notes

Every picture in this catalog is audited on something that is in the scene. This one is audited on something that is not: a shadow. Give it a stage setting and a GLM director picks one play from a short menu printed in its own prompt - SITTING CAT, RABBIT, SAILBOAT or BIRD IN FLIGHT - and copies it onto a SHADOW CARD: one bold silhouette shape, exactly four ordinary tabletop objects, the four parts the silhouette must show, a CASTING paragraph saying which object's shadow mainly forms which part, and the side the single lamp stands on. The card's discipline is that no object on it IS the shape: a teapot, a bottle, an alarm clock and a potted herb are just themselves, and the cat exists nowhere except as the combined shadow they cast on the wall. A second GLM turns the card into one shadow-art photograph prompt - the four objects on a table before a pale wall, one lamp on the carded side, their combined shadow forming the silhouette part by part - and Muse paints it. A blind Gemini reader, who never saw the card, reports the objects it can see, the one thing the wall shadow most resembles, the silhouette parts it can make out, the direction the shadow falls, and whether a real specimen of the shape is physically present. A GLM auditor scores OBJECTS, SHADOW SHAPE (MATCH, PARTIAL or MISS), PARTS, LEAK and LIGHT against the card. A Decide judge headlines round 1: shadow tells it, shape half-formed, shadow shapeless, objects wrong, or not a shadow play.

That verdict is not where this flow stops - the loop closes, per the standing rule that a created asset ends in the corrected asset, not in text. A GLM editor reads the card, the read and the audit and writes ONE concrete edit instruction: the full target end state plus the audit's specific failures as demands phrased the way shadows actually change - small moves and turns of the object the casting assigns to the failed part (turn the curved-neck bottle so its shadow curls up behind the body). The editor is gated in content, a rule this catalog learned the hard way from Molecule Check, whose flawless round-1 molecule came back from an unnecessary edit worse than it went in: on a perfect audit the editor must demand a verbatim unchanged copy, and Muse Edit's repaint is then re-read blind and re-audited against the same card, so a copy that drifts is caught rather than assumed away. The corrected photograph is the deliverable.

Why the loop matters: the catalog already audits shadows once, in shadow-check, where a surveyor reads a scene's shadows as forensic evidence about the sun and scores their direction and length. There the shadow carries no content - no shadow in that flow is supposed to depict anything. Here the shadow is the artwork and the objects are only the medium, which makes two failure classes scoreable that no other audit in the catalog can even state. The first is the inverted leak: a real cat sitting on the table, or a cat-shaped teapot, makes the picture easier to read and the flow fail, because the content must arrive by projection or not at all - every depiction flow rewards presence, this one punishes it. The second is a repair aimed at a thing that is not there: the edit round reshapes a silhouette's anatomy (grow the tail, sharpen the ears) by nudging objects whose own appearance must not change at all, so the fix and the thing fixed live in different layers of the same photograph.

Honest limits, in the open: deliberate shadow art is at the edge of what an image model can do - arranging four objects so their combined shadow draws a chosen silhouette is a spatial puzzle the painter must solve in one shot, with no iteration, and a PARTIAL shape with three of four parts is a realistic good outcome rather than a disappointment. The silhouette is read by its outline alone, and outlines are genuinely contestable: a reader who calls the shadow a dog has failed the cat on a judgement call, and the audit records the reader's actual words so you can judge the call yourself. The parts are scored from the reader's feature list, so a part the painter drew but the reader failed to name scores as missing - that is the flow working as designed, because a shadow nobody can read has not told the picture. The casting map is guidance, not a scored axis: which object contributed which part cannot be told from the finished photograph, so the audit scores the shadow's parts, never their parentage. And the light axis is coarse by construction - one lamp, one wall, two directions; it convicts a shadow falling toward its lamp, and claims nothing finer.
