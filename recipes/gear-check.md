## Notes

A gear train is a picture that computes. Three gears meshing in a row cannot turn any way they please: every mesh flips the direction, and the teeth ratio decides how far the next gear turns - a 12-tooth gear driving a 24-tooth gear turns it exactly half as far, the other way, and that gear drives the third back the first way, rescaled again. Nothing in a single gear's portrait knows this law; it lives only in the contact between the gears. Image models paint gears as decoration - handsome cogs whose teeth happen to touch - and asked for the same train after a drive turn, they rotate whichever gear they please, by however much looks lively, in whichever direction feels right. This flow makes the transmission a planned, measurable quantity - and then it does something about a slip. Give it a gear board setting and a GLM director picks one train from a menu printed in its own prompt (BRASS MILL 12/24/12, COPPER PRESS 16/8/16, IRON GATE 12/12/24) and copies it onto a GEAR CARD: the teeth, the drive turn applied to the LEFT gear, and every gear's turn derived through the mesh chain, ending in the compass position each gear's single coloured marker dot (LEFT red, MIDDLE amber, RIGHT teal) must reach. A second GLM turns the card into one painter prompt demanding the same board, gears and viewpoint in both panels of a diptych, gear diameters proportional to teeth, teeth visibly meshed: in the LEFT panel every dot at TOP, in the RIGHT panel each dot at its derived end position. Muse paints the diptych. Two blind Gemini readers then split the evidence by panel - neither can borrow the other panel to invent a rotation: each reports only its own panel's gear count and the three dots' compass positions. A GLM auditor scores the reads against the card: START X of 3 dots at TOP, then per gear MATCH, WRONG WAY (the right angle turned the wrong way), WRONG AMOUNT or STILL, TURNS X of 3 - TRANSMITTED only when all three derived turns landed; SLIPPING when one or two landed, JAMMED when nothing moved, TANGLED when everything moved and nothing landed, UNBUILT when the start panel or the train itself failed. A ⚖️ Decide judge (PPLX Decider) sees the picture and all the texts and gives the headline verdict on round 1 as a side output.

Why the loop matters: the catalog's other coupled-angle audit, clock check, couples two hands on ONE axle by one instrument's time law, and its other two-panel audit, metamorphosis check, subtracts a state change whose size is carded directly. Here the right panel's three marker positions are not carded states but a chain derivation - direction alternates at every mesh, magnitude rescales at every mesh - so a single missed flip or ratio shows up as one specific gear in one specific wrong position, and no single gear's read can certify the train: the certificate is the conjunction of all three turns against the chain. Per the owner's end-in-the-asset rule, the audit is not the deliverable: a gated GLM editor turns any slip into concrete repaint notes for the RIGHT panel only (a transmitted audit demands a verbatim copy; the left panel is never rotated to fake a match), Muse Edit repairs the diptych, and both blind readers plus the same auditor re-score the corrected picture, which is the flow's final output.

What it does not prove: a marker dot's compass position is a blind reader's judgement on painted pixels - the menu is built so every derived turn lands on a cardinal point (TOP, RIGHT, BOTTOM, LEFT), where a reader's call is least arguable, and a dot painted between cardinals can be honestly argued either way. Teeth are never counted by the readers; the ratios enter through the card's derivation and the gears' relative sizes, so the flow certifies transmitted rotation, not tooth counts. One run proves the chain - director, brief, paint, dual blind panel reads, transmission audit, gated right-panel repair, re-audit - end to end on the train the director happened to pick.
