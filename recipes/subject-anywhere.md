## Notes

Relocating a product photo the usual way - describe the product to a generator and let it repaint the whole scene - quietly rewrites the product. The shape survives; the label does not. Fine print warps first, then colour names, then the brand itself, and nothing in the finished picture announces that it happened. This flow refuses the repaint. A Gemini reader first writes a PRESERVATION SPEC of the uploaded photo: what the subject is, its colours and silhouette, and every printed word on it transcribed exactly, line by line. BRIA RMBG-3.0 then cuts the subject out on transparency - it is a matting model, not a generator, so the subject's pixels are lifted rather than redrawn (it is built to hold product text, logos and fine hair through the cut). A Nano Banana edit receives the cut-out and a scene brief and builds the new scene AROUND it: the subject is placed, lit and shadowed into the terrace, the market stall, the snowfield - wherever the scene text says - under an explicit instruction not to alter it. Then the audit: a second Gemini reader, blind to the original, transcribes the placed subject, and a GLM checker holds spec against re-read and rules LABEL PRESERVED or DRIFTED (quoting the differing lines), SUBJECT INTACT or CHANGED, and a headline VERDICT.

Why the loop matters: every other placement tool asks a beauty question - does the composite look good? This one asks an identity question - is it still the same product? The spec is taken BEFORE the move, from the original, so the checker is not grading the composite against its own plausibility; and the re-reader is blind, so it cannot parrot the spec. The failure the flow names in advance is LABEL DRIFT: generative editors re-render text at whatever fidelity the scene needs, and microprint - the roast notes, the weight, the origin line - is always the first casualty, precisely the text a seller is legally and commercially most bound to keep exact. The verdict makes that casualty a printed line in the output instead of a surprise at print time.

Honest limits, in the open: the placement step is still a generative edit, so "untouched" is an instruction the audit verifies, not a pixel-level guarantee - a true alpha-composite node would make preservation exact by construction, and until one exists the checker's verdict is the guarantee's substitute. The audit compares two vision transcriptions, so it inherits reader noise both ways: microprint near the readers' resolution can pass silently or be flagged on a soft-but-correct rendering (the live run below was flagged on a line that is visibly softer but arguably faithful). RMBG keeps the subject's original lighting; the editor harmonises the scene around it, but a noon-lit product dropped into a dusk scene can still look pasted - the checker does not score lighting. One subject per run; a group shot is cut as one subject.

Live proof (2026-11-17, NanoGPT updates watch follow-up): full run SUCCESS end to end, all nodes done, $0.0906 exact (Gemini spec $0.0048, RMBG-3.0 $0.018, Nano Banana placement $0.039, Gemini re-read $0.0288, GLM checker $0.00). The demo subject - a matte black coffee bag labelled NORTH PEAK / DARK ROAST / 250g, its photo itself painted by Muse Image for the occasion ($0.011) since the flow takes any upload - was moved from a kitchen counter to the graph's default alpine cafe terrace. The composite is visually verified: the bag stands on a weathered terrace table at golden hour, a cup beside it, peaks behind, and the headline label is letter-perfect at full resolution. The checker still ruled VERDICT: NEEDS WORK - LABEL: PRESERVED on every spec line, but the blind re-read's CONDITION flagged the bottom microprint line ("ROASTED IN THE MOUNTAINS - 100% ARABICA") as warped, and the pixels agree it is the softest line on the bag. Recorded as caught, not hidden: the audit found real drift at exactly the size of print it exists to catch. A separate RMBG probe the same day (portrait with curly hair, $0.018, ~25 s) verified the cut-out itself: true RGBA transparency, 26% of frame fully transparent with soft partial-alpha hair edges.
