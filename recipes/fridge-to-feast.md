## Notes

The "what's for dinner?" pipeline, rebuilt as a multimodel graph. Apps that do this trick (SlothChef, FridgeAI and friends) run one provider's vision model into the same provider's chat model and stop at a text recipe with a decorative picture. This graph splits the four jobs across four model families, because each job is a different skill: a Gemini vision node is the inventory clerk (it lists only what is actually visible in the fridge photo, with rough quantities, and is forbidden from suggesting dishes), a GLM node is the chef (it invents ONE recipe under a hard constraint — only inventoried ingredients plus salt, pepper, oil and dried herbs; nothing may be shopped for), a second GLM node changes hats and art-directs (it turns the recipe into a single overhead food-photography brief), and Muse renders the plate from that brief.

The piece none of the apps have is the audit at the end. A ⚖️ Decide judge (PPLX Decider) receives the chef's recipe as text and the rendered plate as an image, and has to label the render: **faithful plate**, **minor liberties**, **phantom ingredient** (a main ingredient is visible that the recipe never had — the classic failure of food renders, which love adding lemon wedges and herb garnishes nobody inventoried), or **wrong dish**. The graph exports the **Plated dish — audited** verdict text, and the plate itself rides through the Decide node as its image output — open the Plated dish node to see the render the verdict is about.

Honest limits, in the open: the inventory is a photo read, not ground truth — the vision clerk will miss whatever hides behind the milk and can mislabel a jar, and it cannot judge freshness or safety, so the recipe inherits any misread (the Constraints input is where you correct it: name the thing it missed). The chef's constraint is a system prompt, not a cage; a determined LLM can still reach for an unlisted garnish, which is exactly what the audit exists to catch rather than prevent. The audit judges a 512 px shrink of the plate, plenty for "that is clearly salmon" and weaker for small garnish disputes. And the render is a promise, not a photograph of your cooking — your version will look like dinner, not like a shoot.

Expected cost per run: Muse text-to-image $0.011, the Gemini inventory roughly $0.001–0.006, the two GLM calls effectively $0.00–0.001, and the Decide audit is not priced in the 2026-09-12 catalog snapshot — expect roughly $0.02–0.03 all in. Prices are observations, not caps. Live proof, 2026-11-08: the full chain ran end to end for $0.0170 metered ($43.1097 → $43.0927 balance-verified, the Decide audit's cost unpriced in the report). Because no real fridge photo was staged, the proof input was itself generated with one Muse call ($0.0110) — an open fridge holding eggs, milk, jam, pickles, cheddar, butter, leftover rice, cherry tomatoes, spinach, carrots, peppers and lemons. The inventory read it back almost exactly, including an honest "zucchini or cucumber (?)" for one half-hidden item; the chef answered with One-Pan Cheddar Garden Fried Rice using only inventoried food plus staples; the render matched the brief ingredient for ingredient (lemon half beside the plate, as briefed); and the audit verdict was **faithful plate**. Run report and images: flow-hunt/runs/2026-11-08-fridge-to-feast/ in the nanoodle-dev workspace (staging: flow-hunt/runs/2026-11-08-fridge-staging/).

Change the **Fridge photo** input to any open-fridge photo or a counter of groceries, and edit **Constraints** for who's eating, how long you have, and what must never appear ("no eggs", "vegetarian", "use the spinach first — it is wilting"). Swap the render model in the Plated dish node's ⚙️ settings if you prefer a different kitchen look; keep the audit wired to the recipe text, or the verdict stops meaning anything.
