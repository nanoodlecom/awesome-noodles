## Notes

One product photo becomes a finished sale poster in three chained edits, and the workflow checks the one claim the model is sold on. A Gemini reader writes the product anchors first — every word on the label transcribed — then Seedream 5.5 (bytedance/seedream-v5.5/edit) edits the photo three times in a chain, one change per round: a new backdrop, campaign props, the offer text. Each instruction repeats the preservation clause, because that is the discipline the ComfyUI seller playbooks teach by hand. A second Gemini reader describes the finished poster blind, and a GLM drift report compares anchors, requested edits and re-read: HELD / DRIFTED / CAMPAIGN: COMPLETE or INCOMPLETE.

Why this model, why now: Seedream 5.5 is ByteDance's November 2026 image editor, sold on multi-round localized editing that leaves the rest of the picture alone — a seller playbook updated the morning this shipped builds a whole 12.12 campaign kit from one product shot and advises testing five or six rounds on your own product first. ComfyUI got it as a Partner Node on Nov 11; on NanoGPT it has been in the image catalog since Nov 6 at $0.028 per 1K edit. The example exists because the Demand Watch found sellers asking for exactly this chain — and because a no-drift claim deserves a measurement, not a testimonial.

Two pins matter. The edit nodes' size is set to "1k": Seedream 5.5 takes resolution tokens (1k / 1.5k / 2k), not pixel sizes, so the executor's 1024x1024 default would fail the same way MiniMax H3's did. And the model id is the /edit variant — the base id is the generate route at a different price; /edit is the image-to-image route at $0.028.

The graph exports the finished poster (the **Add the offer text — Seedream 5.5** image) and the **Drift report — GLM grades the chain** text.

Defaults are three 1K edits ($0.028 each), two Gemini reads and a GLM check. The saved run reported $0.0905 all-in; a one-edit probe before the build cost $0.028 and a first full attempt lost to a service restart after $0.059 of billed nodes (its outputs died with the process; the re-run is the saved run). Prices are observations, not caps.

The saved run is in the nanoodle gallery (https://nanoodle.com/examples/gallery/#one-photo-whole-campaign): a glass-jar candle labelled EMBER & OAK / No. 12 / CEDAR + SMOKE / HAND POURED · 8 OZ moves from a plain studio backdrop to a dark wooden table against a green wall, gains two cedar discs and a eucalyptus sprig, and finishes under cream serif poster text — 12.12 SALE, UP TO 30% OFF. The drift report graded the label HELD word-for-word across all three rounds and the campaign COMPLETE. Flaws, honestly: the report's two DRIFTED notes are about the readers' wording, not the pixels (one read called the lid "open", the other didn't; the re-read called the wall "soft-focus" though no blur was requested) — the audit grades from text, and it flags wording wobble as readily as pixel drift. At pixel level the headline picked up a decorative opening quote mark the instruction didn't ask for; the words themselves are exact. Swap the 📷 input for your own product shot, rewrite the three edits, and keep the preservation clause in each one.
