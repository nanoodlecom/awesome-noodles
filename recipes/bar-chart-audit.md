## Notes

Ask an image model for a bar chart and it will happily invent the data: bars that look plausible, numbers that were never yours, a sixth bar nobody ordered — and the poster looks so clean that nobody checks. This graph makes the chart answer for its numbers. A GLM data clerk writes a 5-line dataset from a brief — five real cafe drinks, each with an exact whole number of cups sold between 20 and 90, all five numbers different, each drink with its own bar colour (the default brief is a fictional cafe's 'Saturday in Cups' poster) — a second GLM turns the dataset into a chart-design brief, and Muse renders the poster: five bars on one shared baseline, heights proportional to the numbers, the drink's name under each bar and its exact number in large type above it. Then a Gemini vision chart checker reads only what is actually printed in the render, bar by bar, left to right, a GLM auditor scores the dataset against that read-back line by line (CHARTED / WRONG NUMBER / WRONG ORDER / MISSING, EXTRA lines for any bar that was never in the data, ending in an "X of 5 charted" summary), and a ⚖️ Decide judge (PPLX Decider) gives the headline verdict: **charted to data**, **numbers drifted**, **bars missing**, or **wrong chart**. The poster is not decoration here; it is a claim about five numbers, and two independent reads hold it to them.

Why the loop matters: a chart is the one image whose entire job is to be believed. A swapped digit, a bar quietly taller than its label, a drink renamed to something more chart-like — each is a small lie that survives precisely because the design looks authoritative. Splitting the jobs across four model families is what makes the check honest: the model that wrote the numbers is not the model that drew them, and neither of them reads them back. The printed number above each bar is the contract — bar heights are vibes, but a printed 47 either says 47 or it does not, and the read-back scores the print, not the vibe.

Honest limits, in the open: the chart check reads a 512 px shrink of the render, so small axis figures and tight labels are judgement calls, and the checker's "(?)" guesses flow straight into the audit — read the WRONG NUMBER lines against the poster yourself before you trust them. The auditor scores the printed number above each bar, not the bar's measured height: a bar drawn out of proportion under a correct label passes the number check, and only the Decide judge, looking at the whole image, can catch a grossly wrong height. Drink names are matched loosely (a close spelling counts as the same drink), so a subtly renamed label can pass. The dataset is invented by the clerk — this proves a chart can be made to carry exact numbers faithfully, not that any real cafe sold anything. And 5 bars is a design choice, not a law — edit the clerk's system prompt and the auditor's summary line together if your data runs longer. One deliberate guard from this catalog's own repair history: the chart-brief node is capped (maxTokens 300, reasoningEffort low, the pairing the mural-wall repair landed on), because an uncapped brief generation on a detailed plan once ran past the HTTP timeout three runs in a row.

Expected cost per run: Muse text-to-image $0.011, the Gemini chart check roughly $0.001–0.012, the three GLM calls effectively $0.00, and the Decide audit is not priced in the 2026-09-12 catalog snapshot — expect roughly $0.02–0.04 all in. Prices are observations, not caps.

Live proof, 2026-11-11: the full chain ran end to end for $0.0129 balance-verified ($23.0440 → $23.0311; metered $0.01295 — Muse render $0.011, Gemini chart check $0.00195, GLM calls $0.00, Decide unpriced in the report), no staging input needed — the chart brief is text. The clerk's dataset: Espresso 27 (deep brown), Latte 68 (warm orange), Cappuccino 45 (sage green), Flat White 31 (slate blue), Cold Brew 84 (dusty rose). Muse's poster is visually verified: exactly those five bars in dataset order on one baseline, heights proportional, every name under its bar and every number in large type above it, axis 0–100, nothing else printed. Both judges returned a clean sweep on the chart's first outing: Decide **charted to data** and the audit scored **5 of 5 charted**, the read-back matching the dataset digit for digit. One honest flaw, recorded in the open: the poster is titled "Cafe Drinks", not the brief's "Saturday in Cups" — the title lives only in the brief, which the chart designer never sees (only the dataset flows downstream), so title drift is currently unscored; the numbers, which are the graph's actual claim, all landed. Change the **Chart brief** input for subject, title and constraints ("a bookshop's weekly sales by genre, values in books sold", "five dog breeds by average daily steps, in thousands" — keep one unit and whole numbers, or the read-back stops being a fair test), and the whole chain re-plans around it. Keep the auditor wired to both the clerk's dataset and the chart check, or the score stops meaning anything; swap the render model in the Bar chart node's ⚙️ settings if you prefer a different chart style.
