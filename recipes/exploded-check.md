## Notes

An exploded view is a promise: this is everything inside the object, in the order it comes apart, with nothing hidden and nothing merged. Product photographers and repair manuals live by it, because a reader can rebuild the object from the picture alone. Generators break that promise by default - they draw the object whole, because a whole cheeseburger is what a cheeseburger looks like. This flow makes disassembly a planned, measurable quantity. Give it a setting (the default is a clean studio product shot) and a GLM director writes an EXPLODED CARD: one object from a menu built for separable parts (cheeseburger, French press, camping lantern, desk lamp, hand-crank coffee grinder), decomposed into exactly 6 canonical parts listed top to bottom in disassembly order, each with an identifying detail. A second GLM turns the card into one painter prompt demanding an exploded view: the 6 parts floating in a single vertical column in card order, aligned on the central axis, every part separated from its neighbours by a clear gap of empty background. Muse paints it. A blind Gemini surveyor, who never saw the card, lists the parts it actually sees top to bottom and grades the gap below each one - CLEAR GAP, TOUCHING or FUSED. A GLM auditor scores PARTS (X of 6 planned parts found, with MISSING and EXTRA lines), ORDER (X of 5 adjacent found pairs in card relative order), SEPARATION (X of 5 adjacent found pairs with a clear gap under the upper part) and STILL ASSEMBLED (separation near zero while the parts are all there - a whole object wearing an exploded view's name). A Decide judge (PPLX Decider) sees the picture and all the texts and gives the headline verdict: **fully exploded**, **parts missing**, **order scrambled**, or **still assembled**.

Why the loop matters: the catalog's other structural audits measure relations that hold a scene together - stack check scores whether distinct objects support each other (contact is success), mirror check a reflection's fidelity, turnaround check a predicted back view. Separation is the inverse relation, and it has never been audited: here contact is the failure. The parts also differ in kind from every other audit's objects - they are not independent items placed in a scene, they are one object's own canonical decomposition, so completeness is judged against what that object actually contains, and a painter that quietly merges the cheese into the patty or drops the filter has failed in a way no placed-object audit can state. ORDER adds the second axis: an exploded view whose parts float in the wrong sequence cannot be rebuilt from, which is the entire point of the form.

Honest limits, in the open: the part list is the director's decomposition, not a manufacturer's diagram - the menu is restricted to objects whose 6-part decomposition is common knowledge, and the auditor scores against the card, so a wrong card would be faithfully audited rather than caught. Gap grades are one blind reader's visual judgement; a part pair rendered a few pixels apart can read as TOUCHING, which is why SEPARATION counts only CLEAR GAPs and the headline asks for at least 4 of 5 rather than perfection. Small internal components (screws, springs, gaskets) are deliberately out of scope - the card lists the 6 major separable parts only.

Live proof (2026-11-18, flow hunt): SUCCESS end to end, all nodes done, no errors. The director carded a French press coffee maker - plunger knob, plunger rod, lid, glass carafe, filter mesh, handle frame. The render is visually verified as a true exploded view: five pieces floating on one vertical axis over a plain studio background, every gap clear, nothing assembled. The blind surveyor listed 5 parts (plunger rod topped with its black knob as one piece, lid, beaker, mesh filter assembly, base with handle), all GAP: CLEAR GAP, DUPLICATES: none. The GLM auditor scored PARTS 5 of 6 (MISSING: plunger knob, merged into the rod), ORDER 4 of 5, SEPARATION 4 of 5, STILL ASSEMBLED: NO, and Decide v1.2 returned 'parts missing'. Two honest catches, recorded rather than rounded away: the knob-and-rod merge the auditor flagged is physically truthful - on a real French press the knob is fixed to the rod, so the card's split was the artificial line, and the painter drew the plunger as the single assembly it is; and Decide's headline is stricter than its own stated rule (PARTS 5 of 6 with separation 4 of 5 fits none of its four definitions cleanly - it chose the missing-part reading over the scrambled one). The handle also sits on a base plate the card never listed, which the surveyor folded into the handle part.
