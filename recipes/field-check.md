## Notes

A compass needle does not point north. It points along the magnetic field where it sits — and next to a bar magnet, that is almost never north. Put a compass on the magnet's axis beyond its north pole and the needle points away from the magnet, following the field line out; put one beyond the south pole and it points back toward the magnet, following the same line in — so the two compasses at opposite ends of the board point in the SAME direction, which no row of polite north-pointing compasses can imitate. Above the magnet's middle the needle runs from the north end toward the south end; above the north pole it points north, below the north pole it points south, and below the south pole it points north again. Generators do not reason this way: left to themselves they paint the schoolbook stereotype — every compass on the board pointing obediently up — because that is what compasses look like in pictures. This flow makes the field a derived, measurable quantity, sampled at six probe positions, and then it does something about a violation. Give it a board setting (an empty demonstration board) and a GLM director picks one board from a menu printed in its own prompt (LAB BENCH, CLASSROOM BOARD, WORKSHOP BENCH, MUSEUM CASE — two with the red north end on the left, two mirrored with it on the right) and copies it onto a FIELD CARD: the board, the magnet line, six PROBE lines giving each compass position and the needle direction the dipole law derives for it, and a CONTROL line for the seventh compass in the top-right far corner, too far from the magnet to be turned, which must simply point NORTH. The carded directions were verified against the dipole formula (B ∝ 3(m·r̂)r̂ − m) in code for both orientations before being put on the menu — the axis pair, the centre run and the pole-flank split all fall out of the arithmetic, not out of intuition. A second GLM turns the card into a painter prompt — board, magnet, seven compasses at their positions, and the field law stated once and abstractly as lines streaming out of the north end, looping around outside the magnet and diving back into the south end — and is forbidden to state any individual compass's direction: the painter must trace each needle from the line through its spot. Muse paints the board from directly above. A blind Gemini reader, who never saw the card, reports which end of the magnet is red and, per position, the direction each needle's red tip actually points. A GLM auditor scores PROBES, DIRECTIONS, AXIS (both axis compasses pointing the same carded way — the loop-back signature), CONTROL (the far compass must read NORTH — a compass that passes only by doing nothing) and MAGNET. A ⚖️ Decide judge (PPLX Decider) headlines round 1 as a side output — **field runs true**, **needles scrambled**, **axis pair split**, **control captured**, or **not a field board** — but the deliverable is the corrected board.

That verdict is not where this flow stops — the loop closes, per the standing rule that a created asset ends in the corrected asset, not in text. A GLM editor reads the card, the read and the audit and writes ONE concrete edit instruction: the full target end state (every compass position named with its carded needle direction, the control pointing north) plus the audit's specific failures as needle-repaint demands — the RIGHT compass points east: repaint its needle so its red tip points west, toward the magnet. The editor is gated in content, a rule this catalog learned the hard way from Molecule Check, whose flawless round-1 model was recoloured by a repair it never needed: on a perfect audit the editor must demand a verbatim unchanged copy and forbid anything being repainted or moved. Muse Edit applies the notes to the round-1 picture. The corrected board is the flow's deliverable. A second blind reader re-inspects it, and the same auditor scores round 2 against the same card, so the fix is itself audited and the reader can see exactly what the edit bought — or cost. The Decide verdict stays, demoted to a side output on round 1.

Why this axis is new: the catalog's nearest neighbours are shadow-check, refraction-check and circuit-check, and the differences are mechanical. In shadow-check one global rule — a single sun — sets every object's shadow direction, so the audited directions are all consequences of one shared cause stated once; here there is no global direction at all: the correct direction is a function of each probe's position relative to two poles, the function is non-uniform (six probes, four different directions), and its signature consequence — the axis pair pointing the same way at opposite ends of the board — is one no single-source rule can produce. In refraction-check the audited evidence is a background pattern displaced inside a silhouette; here the evidence is a vector sampled at a point — each compass is a field probe, and the picture is a field map whether the painter meant it to be or not. In circuit-check a per-bulb state is derived from wiring topology in a drawn circuit; here the topology is invisible — no field lines may be drawn — and must be inferred from the magnet's geometry alone, which is exactly the inference the stereotype failure replaces: ALL NORTH, the painter painting what compasses "do" instead of what this field does, is a failure no neighbour can even state, and the control compass is the one place where that stereotype is suddenly correct — a scored non-event with the trap built in, since a painter that turns every needle by the law will wrongly turn the far one too. A position-dependent vector field, sampled by probes, with a loop-back axis signature and a do-nothing control: no existing catalog flow audits any of the three.

Honest limits, in the open: the field is the idealised schoolbook dipole, sampled at generous distances with directions snapped to the eight compass winds — at the carded probe spots the dipole's true directions sit within a few degrees of the carded winds (verified numerically), but a real board's needles also feel the Earth's field, friction and each other, and this flow scores the idealisation, not a laboratory. The control is the idealisation stated loudest: "far" on a painted board is a few magnet-lengths, where a real compass would still feel a weakened pull; the card defines the control as beyond reach and the audit scores that definition, disclosed here rather than hidden. Needle reading is the fragile joint — a slim needle's red tip at a diagonal can be read either way by a blind reader, which is why directions are snapped to the four cardinal winds only, the stylist demands a long bold red tip, and diagonal reads score as the errors they are rather than being rounded into passes.
