## Notes

Storyboard relay — a story handoff, critique, and one visible repair
Authored 12 September 2026. This file describes the graph; inspect the separate
run evidence for observed outputs and whether a particular repair helped.

Opening scene -> Muse image -> Nano Banana next scene -> Gemini draft critique
-> Muse edit repair -> fresh Gemini reading of opening and repaired frame.

The first edit receives the actual opening image and an independent next beat.
The repair receives the actual draft as image 1 and the original opening as
image 2, plus the original story/rules and actual critique. It preserves the new
action while fixing supported continuity problems. Exactly one revision runs,
even if the draft critic reports no mismatch. This is not a conditional loop.
The final reader sees the repaired pixels and original requirements; it does
not receive or recycle the previous verdict.

Why a repair stage was added: the first live draft duplicated the delivered
seedling, leaving one in its capsule while planting another. The draft reader
identified that concrete continuity error. In the selected new run, the repair
removed the duplicate and improved the pose, but the hatch remained closed.
The alternate restored the spool and pink sky while failing the kite-bow count
and placement. These are visible improvements with remaining errors.

Main inputs: First scene; Next beat; Continuity rules.
The executor also exposes the two system prompts as optional inputs named
Draft continuity check and Continuity notes. Keep those defaults for the
documented behavior.
Outputs: Frame 1; Draft frame 2; Frame 2; Continuity notes.
The local fit exports preserve all three images independently of the final
advisory notes. Each fits within 1536x1024 without cropping; small sources are
not necessarily upscaled. Headless JPEG/WebP resizing may require ffmpeg.

Exactly five paid calls, with no prompt-writing call:
1. meta/muse-image/text-to-image, size 3:2, one opening image ($0.01 catalog).
2. nano-banana-edit, size auto, one original image as reference ($0.039 catalog).
3. google/gemini-3.8-flash, draft img1=opening, img2=draft; 1400-token budget.
4. meta/muse-image/edit, size 3:2, image=draft, image2=opening ($0.01 catalog).
5. google/gemini-3.8-flash, final img1=opening, img2=repaired; 2200-token budget.

Public catalog snapshot checked 12 September 2026: Muse supports these ratios
and multiple image references; Nano Banana Edit lists auto at $0.039/image and
up to four references, of which this graph uses one. Gemini supports image
input, with $0.75/M input and $3.75/M output tokens in the snapshot.
Image subtotal is $0.059 plus two vision/text calls. These are estimates,
not a spending cap, observed run price or proof of future model availability.

Visible acceptance:
- Opening, draft and repaired next frame have readable story actions.
- Identity and story-critical props survive the intended change of scene.
- Compare the repair against the actual draft criticism. Report unfixed or new
  errors; retaining the draft lets a visitor decide whether the revision helped.
- Final notes describe repaired pixels, acknowledge unclear evidence and never
  claim an old draft assessment validates the repair.
- All artwork stays downloadable even if final continuity remains imperfect.

fixtures/storyboard-relay.cases.json contains two cases. The first delivers a
seedling to an orbital greenhouse; the second is a cut-paper crab flying a kite
over a salt lagoon. The briefs are unchanged from the original three-call graph
so the fixed revision can be assessed on the same stories. World details live
in editable inputs, not reusable reviewer instructions. The [selected runs and
review](../evidence/README.md#storyboard-relay) preserve opening, draft, repaired
frame and fresh criticism for both stories; they do not claim full continuity.
