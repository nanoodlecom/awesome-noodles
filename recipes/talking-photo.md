## Notes

A photo with a face full of story performs one short line. A Gemini TTS voice (Sulafat) reads the script — direction line and all — the clip is trimmed to five seconds, and LTX-2.5 Fast (lightricks/ltx-2.5/fast) takes the photo and the audio as direct inputs, generateAudio off, and moves the mouth to the supplied voice. A GLM guide closes with what you made, how it works, and the honest edges. The demo subject is a weathered marble emperor who has been silent for two thousand years and has opinions about being woken.

Why this model, why now: talking-photo / lipsync workflows are Civitai's lead LTX-2.5 content (the FLF2V + Lipsync pair, priced by their author at ~20 minutes for 28 s locally on a 3090 after a 22B download), and LTX-2.5 is Hugging Face's #2 model (2.87M downloads). The Demand Watch's cycles 4–5 verified every piece: Fast lists audio among its input modalities, so the driving-audio conditioning is a model input on NanoGPT, not a node chain to rebuild. This is not the catalog's Talking Avatar example — that one runs LongCat Avatar at 480p from a generated presenter; this one runs LTX-2.5 Fast on YOUR photo with a performed line.

The probe came first, and the claim was checked in the video, not the listing: one live run (photo + 5.00 s voice clip) returned a 1080×1920, 24 fps, 5.0 s clip whose embedded audio envelope is a scaled copy of the driving clip, bin for bin — including its silent pause. Frame-checked against that envelope: at the pause (~3.0 s, audio ≈ silence) the statue's mouth is nearly closed; at the loudest word (~4.5 s) it is wide open. The saved run repeats the pattern (near-closed at its quietest bin, wide at its loudest). The mouth follows the audio on NanoGPT's route.

Two pricing facts the catalog listing does not shout, both measured: audio-driven runs render at 1080p even when 720p is selected (the resolution parameter's own fine print), so they bill at the 1080p rate — $0.14 a second, not the $0.10 720p rate. The probe's 5.00 s billed exactly $0.70; the saved run's 4.875 s billed $0.6832, plus $0.0249917 of TTS (balance-verified) and a $0 guide — $0.7081917 all-in. Total ship spend $1.4680079 (two TTS takes $0.0598162 — the first take read the line too slowly at 10.1 s and was discarded — the $0.70 probe, and the saved run).

The graph exports the **Talking photo — LTX-2.5 Fast** video and the **Guide — GLM writes what you made** text.

The honest edges, stated in the guide itself: no current model reliably closes the mouth on pauses — in the cycle-4 frame-counted shootout LTX was best of field at 2 of 6 — so short lines with few pauses forgive most (this demo's pause did close; do not count on it). Keep scripts near ten words: the trim node caps the voice at five seconds, and every second bills. Front-facing faces with an unobstructed mouth work best, and statues and paintings forgive more than photographs of real people. Have your own voice clip? Replace the speech node with an audio upload wired into the same port — the LTX node does not care where the audio came from.

The saved run is in the nanoodle gallery (https://nanoodle.com/examples/gallery/#talking-photo): the marble emperor delivers "Two thousand years silent... and you wake me NOW?" — cracks, chipped nose and all — and the guide prints the per-second pricing and the pause caveat next to the clip. Swap the 📷 input for your own statue, painting or portrait and ▶ Run.
