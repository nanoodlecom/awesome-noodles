## Notes

Water in a wide trough sits flat, but stand a narrow glass tube in it and the water climbs: surface tension pulls the liquid up the tube's walls, and the narrower the bore, the higher the climb - the rise is inversely proportional to the diameter, so a hairline tube lifts its column roughly eight times as high as a tube eight times wider, both drinking from the same surface. That is strong enough to look wrong, and image models paint it wrong in signature ways: every tube filled to the same cheerful level (the communicating-vessels cliché, true everywhere except here), or the widest tube tallest because it plainly holds the most. This flow makes the rise a planned, measurable quantity - and then it does something about a wrong one. Give it a plain laboratory bench and a GLM director picks one rig from a menu printed in its own prompt (IVY TROUGH, BLUE BENCH, COPPER TRAY) - four vertical tubes in a stated left-to-right order, each with a bore class (HAIRLINE 0.5 mm, NARROW 1 mm, MEDIUM 2 mm, WIDE 4 mm) and the column band the inverse law derives for it (TALL about four fifths of the exposed length, MODERATE about half, LOW about a quarter, SLIGHT about an eighth), the three rigs shuffling the same four tubes into three different orders so no left-to-right staircase can be faked by habit - and copies it onto a CAPILLARY CARD with a wide open glass jar beside the trough as the control: too broad for capillary action, its surface must stay level with the trough's. A second GLM turns the card into one painter prompt demanding a straight-on bench photograph with each tube's thickness and risen column stated concretely, tube by tube. Muse paints it. A blind Gemini reader, who never saw the card, ranks each tube's bore class against the others in the same picture and reports each blue column's band and the control jar's surface. A GLM auditor scores BORES and COLUMNS against the card, the INVERSE LAW from the read alone (the reported columns must strictly fall as the reported bores grow - a relation between two things inside the same render that copying the card cannot fake), and the CONTROL: RISE TRUE only when all of them hold; FLAT when no column really rose; INVERTED when the columns rise as the bore grows, the law painted backwards; RISE DRIFTED for any other partial miss. A Decide judge (PPLX Decider) sees the picture and all the texts and gives the headline verdict on round 1 as a side output.

Why this axis is new: the catalog's nearest neighbours are level-check and settle-check, and the differences are mechanical. Level-check's law is equality - vessels joined at the base share one surface whatever their shape - and its signature failure is a stair-stepped row; capillary rise is precisely the place that equality lawfully breaks, so here the four surfaces must all differ, each by an amount derived from its own tube's bore, and a row of equal columns is the failure, not the pass. Settle-check orders liquid layers by density inside one jar; nothing here is ordered by what the liquid is - the same blue water stands at four different heights because of the glassware, not the contents. And the control jar ties the exception back to the rule inside a single frame: the jar passes only by provably doing nothing, its surface level with the trough while the hairline tube beside it climbs. Per the owner's end-in-the-asset rule, the audit is not the deliverable: a gated GLM editor turns any failure into concrete repaint notes for the failing tubes only (a true rise demands a verbatim copy; the bench, the trough and the matching tubes are never repainted to fake a match), Muse Edit repairs the photograph, and a blind re-read plus the same auditor re-score the corrected bench, which is the flow's final output.

What it does not prove: bore classes are judged relative to the other tubes in the same picture (thinnest to thickest), and column bands are coarse by design (eighths to fifths of the exposed length), so this flow certifies the rise's inverse order and band class, not a millimetre height; a column at 60 percent scored MODERATE against a carded TALL is a drift the audit reports, honestly, rather than a near miss it waves through. The meniscus itself - the curved top of a real capillary column - is below the reader's resolution and is claimed nowhere. One run proves the chain - director, brief, paint, blind bench read, law audit, gated tube repaint, re-audit - end to end on the rig the director happened to pick.
