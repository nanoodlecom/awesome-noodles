## Notes

Every image flow in this catalog hands the renderer the picture: an edit model gets the pixels, a video model gets the first frame. This one asks what survives when the picture is taken away and only words may cross. A Gemini describer looks at your photo and writes a SCENE INVENTORY — exactly 8 numbered facts in fixed slots: the main subject, its appearance, its position and action, every secondary element with counts, the setting, the foreground and ground, the light and sky, and one small telling detail that makes this scene itself. A GLM writer turns that inventory into a single image prompt that must carry every fact and add none, and Muse repaints the scene from the prompt alone — it never sees your photo. A second Gemini then inventories the repaint in the same 8 slots, a GLM survival auditor scores the original inventory against that read-back slot by slot — SURVIVED, CHANGED (with what differs), LOST, plus INVENTED lines for anything visible in the repaint that the words never said, ending in an "X of 8 facts survived" summary — and a ⚖️ Decide judge (PPLX Decider) gives the headline verdict: **faithful repaint**, **mostly survived**, **half lost**, or **different scene**.

Why the loop matters: the score separates two failures that every pixel-fed audit confuses. If a fact is missing from the repaint, either the describer never wrote it down or the painter misread the words — and the inventory sits in the graph, so you can see which. That makes this a working test of a description's quality, not just a render's: upload a photo you would hand to someone who cannot see it — an illustrator, a blind collaborator, an alt-text pipeline — and the survival score is how much of the scene your words actually carried. The painter not grading its own work is the point, as in every audit here: the describer, the writer, the painter, the reader and the auditor are four model families doing four different jobs, and the reader never sees the original inventory it is implicitly grading against.

Honest limits, in the open: the inventory is capped at 8 facts on purpose — a longer description would carry more and audit worse; the interesting question is what a tight description keeps, and small facts that never made a slot are invisible to the score (the photo may hold a ninth thing nobody counts). Colour and position words are the known weak joints — "left of centre" and "teal" mean slightly different things to a writer, a painter and a reader, so CHANGED lines on those slots are often naming drift rather than a lost fact, exactly the shade the auditor is told to spell out. The default photo is a generated staging scene (a woman in a yellow raincoat with a clear umbrella, a red bicycle, a teal shop door, three plant pots and a black cat), disclosed here rather than passed off as a found photograph; swap in your own photo and the same audit runs. Both GLM calls are capped (maxTokens 300/400, reasoningEffort low, the pairing this catalog's repair history landed on) so a detailed inventory cannot run a generation past the HTTP timeout.

Expected cost per run: two Gemini vision reads and one Muse repaint dominate — roughly $0.02–0.05 all in, the two GLM calls effectively $0.00, and the Decide audit unpriced in the run report. Prices are observations, not caps.

Live proof, 2026-11-13: the full chain ran end to end on its first attempt for $0.0340 balance-verified, staging included ($18.2777 → $18.2436 once billing settled, exactly the metered sum: staging photo $0.0110, describer $0.0067, repaint $0.0110, read-back $0.0053, both GLM calls $0.00, Decide unpriced in the report, cost_exact=false). The describer's inventory of the staging scene was exact — yellow raincoat, clear dome umbrella, red step-through bicycle, three terracotta pots, black cat at the far right of the ledge, six-plus string bulbs, beaded droplets on the umbrella — and the blind repaint is visually verified as the same scene in the same composition: every inventoried element present, in its slot. The judges split, instructively: Decide returned **faithful repaint**, while the slot audit scored 3 SURVIVED, 5 CHANGED, 0 LOST — most CHANGED lines are the naming drift the limits section predicts (lace-up vs matte boots, navy vs petrol-blue door, windowsill vs stone ledge), but one is a real catch the headline judge missed: the repaint's umbrella is in the woman's other hand. One flaw in the proof itself, recorded plainly: the auditor's output ran into its 400-token cap on its verbose CHANGED lines and truncated mid-way through the slot-8 line, so the closing "X of 8 facts survived" summary never printed — the score above is counted from its own eight slot lines. A future revision should give the auditor a larger cap or terser lines; the graph merged here is the exact graph that ran. Run report and images: flow-hunt/runs/2026-11-13-words-only-repaint/ in the nanoodle-dev workspace (repaint saved as yellow-raincoat-repaint.png; the staging original is flow-hunt/inputs/words-only-repaint/scene.png).

Change the **Scene photo** input to any photo you want stress-tested as words — a busy market stall and a bare landscape will fail in opposite, instructive ways. Keep the inventory at the audited 8 slots and keep the reader's slots identical to the describer's, or the slot-by-slot score stops meaning anything; the painter must stay wired to the writer's prompt only, never to the photo.
