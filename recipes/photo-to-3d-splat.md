## Notes

One photo of one object becomes a 3D Gaussian splat of it that you can spin. A Gemini surveyor reads the photo first — what the object is, the view, its materials and colours, the sides the photo hides, and the fragile details. Idol (Sperid Labs' image-to-3D model, open weights, the Hugging Face trending board's top riser of late October 2026) then rebuilds the object as a splat: hundreds of thousands of soft coloured points that render as a solid-looking thing from every side, including the sides the picture never showed. A GLM guide writer turns the survey into the splat's tour: what you got, how to open it, and the honest edges.

Why a splat, and why this is not the other two 3D flows: Idea to 3D Model invents a textured polygon MESH for an object that never existed (TRELLIS, from a generated concept render). Photo to Scene Mesh MEASURES a real place into a mesh (MoGe) and states plainly that backsides are absent. A splat is a third kind of object: no surfaces, no UVs, no textures — a coloured point cloud dense enough to read as solid, which is exactly the format game/VFX and 3D-viewer pipelines are adopting for captured objects, and the one format neither sibling produces. Idol also guesses the hidden sides instead of omitting them; the guide names that as invention, using the survey's hidden-sides line, rather than letting a plausible back face pass as a measured one.

What the graph surfaces is the GLB point-cloud preview. On the raw API Idol also returns the full PLY splat; the 3D node exposes the GLB, so the PLY stays API-only for now — the guide says so in the output itself, not just here. (The headless runner saves the GLB with a .png filename; the bytes are glTF 2.0 — check the magic before judging the file by its name.)

Costs and sizes, observed 2026-11-17: Idol bills a flat $0.10 per generation at every setting; the Gemini survey about $0.003 and the GLM guide $0.00, so a full run is $0.103. The sneaker run's GLB holds 259,136 points (of the 262,144 Gaussians requested; the difference is the model's own pruning) at 4.1 MB. Raise num_gaussians toward the 1,048,576 cap for denser splats at the same flat price; image_type is "real" for photographs and "synthetic" for tight-cropped renders.

Sample swap, 2026-11-17 evening: at the maintainer's direction the sneaker sample was retired as the example's public face — a shoe undersells a splat — and the graph was re-run, unchanged, on a photo of an ornate golden dragon statue (antiqued bronze with dark patina, wings spread, coiled on a dark grey stone plinth). One run, SUCCESS end to end, $0.1034; the GLB holds 245,428 points at 3.9 MB. The survey named the figurine, the frontal view, the bronze and patina, and the hidden sides (wing backs, rear spine, plinth underside) exactly; the guide kept its three-paragraph shape and the GLB-in-hand / PLY-API-only split. That dragon run is the saved gallery run on nanoodle.com.

Live proof, 2026-11-17 (Demand Watch cycle 1 ship): two full runs end to end through the headless runner, both SUCCESS, $0.1032 and $0.1029. The input was a studio photo of a low-top sneaker (canvas and suede, gum sole). The survey named the object, the lateral view, the materials, and the hidden sides (medial side, tread, lining, heel seam) exactly; the GLB parses as valid glTF 2.0 with one POINTS primitive carrying POSITION and COLOR_0 for all 259,136 points — verified by parsing, not rendered, so judge the look by opening it. The guide kept its three-paragraph shape in both runs and, in the second, stated the GLB-in-hand / PLY-API-only split unprompted by anything but its instructions. The endpoint question this ship was gated on — Idol is Sperid Labs, whose Iris-Z image endpoint dropped every probe run earlier the same day — is answered: 2 of 2 completions, 36–43 seconds each, $0.10 billed exactly as catalogued both times.

Change the **Object photo** input: one object, whole and in frame, on an uncluttered background splats best — product shots, props, toys, tools. Scenes and rooms are Photo to Scene Mesh's job; people and groups are out of scope (one object per run). Keep the survey wired to the guide: a bare point cloud is a file most people open once and abandon; the tour — especially the list of which sides were invented — is what makes it a result you can trust at a glance.
