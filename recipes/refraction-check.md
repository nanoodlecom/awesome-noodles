## Notes

Put four pieces of glass in front of a striped wall and the stripes behind each one have no choice about what to do - the shape of the glass decides. A convex lens, bulging out, spreads the stripes wider apart. A concave lens squeezes them narrower. A triangular prism keeps their spacing but shoves them sideways as a block, so they step at the prism's edges and no longer line up with the stripes outside. And a flat pane of plain glass - the control - does nothing at all: the stripes pass through it at the same spacing, in perfect continuation. Image models do not reason this way: left to themselves they draw glass as a decorative frame with the background running politely unchanged behind everything, or they bend the stripes behind the one object that must not bend them. This flow makes refraction a derived, measurable quantity - and then it does something about a violation. Give it a backdrop setting (an empty striped wall) and a GLM director picks one optics scene from a menu printed in its own prompt (OPTICS BENCH, GREENHOUSE GLASS, HARBOUR LIGHT ROOM, LIBRARY DESK) - four glass objects in a row, one for each effect the law allows, the flat pane named on a CONTROL line as the glass that looks as if it should bend the stripes like the others but must leave them SAME - and copies it onto a REFRACTION CARD in left-to-right order. A second GLM turns the card into an image prompt describing the striped wall and each object's shape and mount, plus the optics law stated once and abstractly - convex wider, concave narrower, prism shifted, flat unchanged - and is forbidden to state any individual object's effect: the painter must work each effect out from the shape itself. Muse paints the scene. A blind Gemini reader, who never saw the card, compares the stripes behind each object with the stripes outside it and reports WIDER, NARROWER, SHIFTED or SAME per object, plus whether the background stripes outside the objects are regular. A GLM auditor scores OBJECTS, EFFECTS (the exact stripe behaviour per object), CONTROL (the flat pane must read SAME - a negative constraint no other check in this family states) and BACKGROUND. A Decide judge headlines round 1: light bends true, effects swapped, control displaced, objects wrong, or not a refraction scene.

That verdict is not where this flow stops - the loop closes, per the standing rule that a created asset ends in the corrected asset, not in text. A GLM editor reads the card, the read and the audit and writes ONE concrete edit instruction: the full target end state (every object named with its carded stripe effect, the background regular) plus the audit's specific failures as stripe-repaint demands - the stripes behind the prism line up unchanged: shift them sideways as a block so they step visibly at the prism's edges, keeping their spacing. The editor is gated in content, a rule this catalog learned the hard way from Molecule Check, whose flawless round-1 model was recoloured by a repair it never needed: on a perfect audit the editor must demand a verbatim unchanged copy and forbid anything being repainted or moved. Muse Edit applies the notes to the round-1 picture. The corrected scene is the flow's deliverable. A second blind reader re-inspects it, and the same auditor scores round 2 against the same card, so the fix is itself audited and the reader can see exactly what the edit bought - or cost. The Decide verdict stays, demoted to a side output on round 1.

Why this axis is new: the catalog's nearest neighbours are shadow-check and thermal-check, and the differences are mechanical. In shadow-check the audited pattern is cast by an object onto a surface - light blocked; here the audited pattern is the background itself, seen THROUGH the object - light transmitted and displaced - so the evidence lives inside the object's silhouette, not outside it, and a painter that draws a beautiful object over a lazy background fails inside the glass where nobody usually looks. In thermal-check one object's colour is derived from a rank on a scale; here four different transformations of the same background are derived from four shapes in one picture, and they are transformations of spacing and alignment, not colours or positions of things. And the control has no analogue in either neighbour: the flat pane is a scored NON-event - the flow passes only if a specific piece of glass provably does nothing, so an over-eager painter that refracts everything fails on the object it tried hardest on. Displacement-through-a-medium, scored per object against a shape law, with a do-nothing control: no existing catalog flow audits any of the three.
