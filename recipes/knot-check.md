## Notes

A knot is not its silhouette. Two ropes can trace the exact same loop on a table and be different knots - or no knot at all - because a knot's identity lives at its crossings: at each place the strand passes over itself, which pass lies on top decides the topology, and flipping a single crossing can turn a figure-eight into an unknot that pulls straight. This flow makes that layering a planned, measurable quantity - and then it does something about a flip. Give it a strand setting and a GLM director picks one true alternating knot from a menu with canonical crossing counts (trefoil 3, triquetra 3, figure-eight 4, pentafoil 5) and writes a KNOT CARD: the strand (one continuous closed rope, no loose ends) and every crossing in clockwise order, each with its zone and the direction of the pass that must lie OVER - described only by direction of travel (left-to-right, top-to-bottom, or one of the two diagonals), never by colour or side, so the audit tests layering, not labels. A second GLM turns the card into a painter prompt that demands the over-pass be drawn unbroken and the under-pass interrupted by a clean gap on both sides - the visual convention that makes over and under readable at a glance. Muse paints it. A blind Gemini tracer, who never saw the knot or the card, reports whether the strand is one closed loop, broken, ended or multiple, and lists every crossing it can find by zone with the direction of the pass visibly on top. A GLM auditor matches trace to card by zone and scores STRAND, CROSSINGS (a crossing counts only when its zone AND its over-direction both match), FLIPPED (right place, wrong pass on top), MISSING and EXTRA crossings. A Decide judge headlines round 1: true knot, crossings flipped, strand broken, or not a knot.

That verdict is not where this flow stops - the loop closes, per the standing rule that a created asset ends in the corrected asset, not in text. A GLM editor reads the card, the trace and the audit and writes ONE concrete edit instruction: the full target end state (every carded crossing at its zone with its carded pass on top, one closed strand, the rope, background and framing unchanged) plus the audit's specific failures as extra demands - swap the layering at crossing 2, uncross the extra crossing at top-left into a smooth run, join the break - naming the crossings already correct as keepers that must be left alone. Muse Edit applies those notes to the round-1 picture. The corrected knot is the flow's deliverable. A second blind tracer re-traces it, and the same auditor scores round 2 against the same card, so the fix is itself audited and the reader can see exactly what the edit bought - or cost. The Decide verdict stays, demoted to a side output on round 1.

Why this axis is new: the catalog's nearest neighbours are maze-solve-audit and stack-check. A maze's topology is a planar route - which corridor connects to which - and its crossings never layer: two paths at an intersection are the same path. Stack-check audits support between SEPARATE objects (which object rests on which), a relation between things, not within one thing. Here the audited relation is a single strand's layering against ITSELF, and the failure it names - FLIPPED - has no analogue in either: the crossing is present, at the right place, with both passes visible, and the knot is still wrong, because the wrong one is on top. It is also the catalog's first purely topological deliverable: nothing is counted in place, placed in a zone grid for its own sake, or coloured to a palette - the zones exist only to give each crossing a name the tracer, auditor and editor can share.

Honest limits, in the open: over and under are judged from the painted gap convention - an over-pass drawn unbroken and an under-pass cleanly gapped. A painter that fudges a crossing into a weave-like merge gives the tracer shading, not structure, to judge, and the trace records it as the tracer called it. Directions are coarse (four travel directions), so two passes running nearly parallel at a shallow crossing can be hard to assign - the card's zones and the clockwise order are the disambiguator, and a genuinely ambiguous crossing will show up as a flip or a miss rather than being rounded into a pass. The director cards a canonical knot from its own knowledge, so a card error is possible in principle - that is why the card is published with the graph's run: a wrong card would be visible as a card-versus-knot mismatch, not hidden inside a score. One repair round is unrolled, not a loop - if round 2 still flips a crossing, the flow shows the flip rather than spending again.
