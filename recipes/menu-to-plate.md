## Notes

Menu apps do one trick: photograph a foreign menu, get a translation back — and then you still order blind, picturing a dish the kitchen may plate completely differently. This graph splits the jobs across four model families and closes the loop: a Gemini vision node is the reader (it transcribes and translates the whole menu into a numbered menu card — every dish in menu order, each description translated component by component, prices kept, unreadable words marked "(?)" — and it is forbidden from recommending anything), a GLM node changes hats into a diner (its order slip must respect the dining brief's hard constraints, name the chosen dish by number, original name and translation, list ONLY the components the menu promises on an "On the plate:" line, and name tempting extras the menu does not promise on a "Not promised:" line), a second GLM compresses the slip into a single photo prompt that starts "Plate exactly:", and Muse renders the dish from the text alone — there is no photo of the food anywhere in the graph.

Then the plate has to answer for itself, twice. A second Gemini vision node reads the render back and inventories only the food components actually visible in it — it never sees the order slip, so it cannot grade generously — a GLM plate auditor scores the slip against that read-back component by component (PRESENT / MISSING, plus an EXTRA line for every prominent unpromised component, ending in an "X of Y promised components present, Z extras" summary), and a ⚖️ Decide judge (PPLX Decider) gives the headline verdict from the slip plus the plate image: **faithful plate**, **plausible but drifted**, **phantom ingredients**, or **wrong dish**. The graph exports the **Plated dish — audited** verdict text, and the rendered plate itself rides through the Decide node as its image output — open the Plated dish node to see the picture the verdict is about.

Why the split matters: the model that read the menu is not the model that chose the dish, neither of them rendered the plate, and neither grades it. The menu card is written *before* any choice is made, so the diner cannot quietly retranslate a dish into something easier to render; the read-back is written *without* the slip, so the auditor compares two independent reads instead of one model's opinion of its own work. The "Not promised:" line is the sharp edge: image models love adding a generic garnish, a sauce swirl or a side the menu never mentioned, and this is the first menu flow that can catch its own renderer doing it.

Honest limits, in the open: the render is a visualisation of the menu's description, not a photo of what this kitchen will actually serve — portion, plating and cooking are the chef's, and the audit checks the render against the menu text, not against the restaurant. Translation is a model's best read of a photograph: glare, handwriting and worn print produce "(?)" guesses, and those guesses flow straight into the choice and the score. The read-back reads a shrunk copy of the render, so a small or partly hidden component is a judgement call. And the staging menu used for the live proof below was typeset locally as a clean French bistro menu card (no spend, no generated menu photo) precisely so the transcription test is legible and checkable word by word — a crumpled real-world menu photo is harder, and the "(?)" marks are where that will show.

Expected cost per run: Muse image $0.011, the two Gemini vision reads roughly $0.004–0.007 each, the three GLM calls effectively $0.00–0.002 combined, and the Decide audit is not priced in the 2026-09-12 catalog snapshot — expect roughly $0.02–0.03 all in. Prices are observations, not caps.

Live proof, 2026-11-08: the full chain ran end to end for $0.0218 balance-verified in ONE live run ($43.0173 → $42.9955; metered $0.0217: menu read $0.00680, order slip $0.00044, photo brief $0.00020, Muse plate $0.011, plate read-back $0.00299, plate audit $0.00027; the Decide audit unpriced in the report, ~$0.00006 by balance gap). No staging spend: the menu was typeset locally, as disclosed above. The reader's menu card was word-perfect against the staging menu — all 9 dishes in order (2 entrées, 5 mains, 2 desserts), every description translated component by component, every price right, no "(?)" needed. The diner picked #5 Ratatouille provençale, correctly the only vegetarian main, and its order slip expanded the menu line into 7 plateable components (diced zucchini, cubed eggplant, crushed-tomato base, red and green pepper strips, slivered onions, herbes de Provence, olive-oil drizzle) with a Not-promised line of grated/goat cheese, rice or couscous, and chili flakes. Muse's render is visually verified as exactly that plate: a glossy ratatouille mound on a speckled ceramic plate, every component identifiable, nothing from the Not-promised line. For once the two judges agree completely: the Decide headline verdict was **faithful plate** and the component audit scored **7 of 7 promised components present, 0 extras** — the read-back even named the dish itself 'Ratatouille (or Caponata)' unprompted.
