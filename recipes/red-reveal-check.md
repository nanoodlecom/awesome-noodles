## Notes

One poster, two messages, each readable only in its own light. The red-reveal (decoder-glasses) print trick is channel coding with ink: a message printed in cyan sits underneath a busy red overprint, and a sheet of red acetate deletes the red channel - red ink fades to the paper colour while cyan ink, which absorbs red light, turns dark. What is left is the secret. This flow makes that channel separation a planned, measurable quantity - and then it does something about a miss. Give it a poster setting and a GLM director writes a REVEAL CARD: a HIDDEN word chosen from a menu (MAGIC, SPELL, GHOST, TRICK, CHARM) to be printed in pure cyan, a different DECOY word from the same menu to be printed in red, and a red motif scene. A second GLM turns the card into one painter prompt demanding a flat two-ink screen print - the hidden word in huge cyan block capitals across the centre, the decoy smaller in red, and the red motifs overprinted densely across the whole poster, crossing the cyan letters, so a casual glance reads the decoy and the pattern while every cyan letter stays complete underneath. Muse paints it. A blind Gemini reads the NORMAL poster and reports the first word a casual viewer reads, every legible word, and any word the cyan shapes form. Muse Edit then performs the filter itself - a fixed colour-remap instruction (red fades to pale ghost, cyan turns charcoal, nothing moves) - and a second blind Gemini reads the FILTERED view and transcribes its darkest word. A GLM auditor scores the two reads against the card on three axes that pull against each other: REVEAL (the filtered view's darkest word is the hidden word, exactly), LEAK (the hidden word must NOT be readable in the normal view - a poster that simply prints both words has no secret), and DECOY (the red word must survive in the normal view, or there is no misdirection). A Decide judge headlines round 1: clean reveal, leaked secret, never reveals, or decoy lost.

That verdict is not where this flow stops - the loop closes, per the standing rule that a created asset ends in the corrected asset, not in text. A GLM editor reads the card, both reads and the audit and writes ONE concrete edit instruction in ink terms: the full target end state (hidden word complete and pure cyan, decoy bold red, motif field dense) plus the audit's specific failure as extra demands - a denser red overprint crossing the cyan strokes if it leaked, purer and more complete cyan letterforms if it never revealed, the purest light red if the decoy ghosted through the filter. Muse Edit applies those notes to the round-1 poster. The corrected poster is the flow's deliverable, and it is immediately rendered through the same red-filter remap, so the reader gets the corrected poster and its reveal view as a pair. Two blind re-reads and the same auditor re-score the corrected pair against the same card, so the fix is itself audited; the Decide verdict stays, demoted to a side output on round 1.

Why this axis is new: the catalog's nearest neighbours hide things in space, not in channels. Squint test hides a heart in a bean flat lay and reveals it by throwing away spatial detail (a 64-pixel downscale) - one hidden pattern, one view transform, no second message and nothing that must stay hidden. Hidden word spells a word from the initials of shelf objects - one message, carried by object identity. Red reveal is the first flow where two messages coexist in the same pixels in different colour channels and the audit is two-view with opposing demands: the normal view is scored for what it must NOT contain as much as for what it must, and the filtered view is not the original picture degraded but a second model's deliberate channel remap - red deleted, cyan inverted to dark. The repair round edits channel behaviour (ink purity, overprint density over letter strokes, red lightness), a vocabulary no object- or position-audit flow can use.

Honest limits, in the open: the filtered view is an edit model's SIMULATION of red acetate, not an optical filter applied to pixels - if the painter's "cyan" drifts toward teal-black or its "red" toward orange, the remap inherits that drift, and the GHOSTS line exists to catch red printing that refuses to vanish. The leak axis is judged from a blind reader's report of what a casual viewer reads first; a determined human staring at the normal poster may pick out cyan letters the reader scored as camouflaged - LEAK measures the glance, not the stare, and the NOTE of a run should say so. The two words are both five letters from a small menu by design: the audit needs letter-exact comparisons, and free choice would spend the run on spelling drift instead of channel behaviour. One repair round is unrolled, not a loop - if round 2 still leaks or still refuses to reveal, the flow shows both audits rather than spending again.
