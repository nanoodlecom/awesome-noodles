## Notes

Regional prompting the ComfyUI way — sequential masked inpainting — rebuilt as three chained Inpaint passes. In ComfyUI you would paint a mask per region and run one inpaint per region so a finished area is never re-rolled by the next prompt; this graph does exactly that. Each pass takes the previous pass's output as its source, its own mask (white = repaint, black = keep), and its own one-line prompt: what goes in that region, and nothing else. The defaults restyle one plain room in three passes: a framed painting, a houseplant, a side table. Change the three **Region — paint this** texts and supply masks that match your own photo, and the same chain repaints any three regions in order — paint large or background regions first, small foreground objects last.

All three passes use Nano Banana Edit for a grounded reason, not a preference: the nanoodle editor only offers the Inpaint node the models on its verified INPAINT_OK allowlist (live-tested 2026-06-26, method and measurements in proof/inpaint-mask-verification.md in the nanoodle repo). Models earn the list by repainting the masked region while leaving pixels outside it essentially untouched; several models that advertise inpainting were measured repainting the whole frame and are excluded. Nano Banana Edit is on the list. If you swap the model in ⚙️ settings, stay on that allowlist or the mask stops meaning anything.

Masks: supply them as PNG inputs headlessly, one per region, black with white where that pass may paint, drawn at your photo's dimensions. In the editor you can instead delete a Mask input and brush the mask directly onto its Inpaint node — the node derives the same mask input. Note the executor difference from the app README of nanoodle-py: the browser composites your mask onto black at the source's pixel size; the headless library passes the mask through verbatim — which is why the graph itself fits the photo and every mask through a local Resize (fit inside 1024×1024) before the passes: the same fit on all four keeps every mask registered to the photo.

Why the Resize guards exist — the first live attempt (2026-11-07) was reported as a Region 2 failure, but a reproduce run with partial results saved showed the root error was at Region 1: 413 FUNCTION_PAYLOAD_TOO_LARGE, $0 billed. The staged photo is a 1600×1600 PNG of ~3.4 MB; sent as a base64 data URL plus its mask, the request body crossed NanoGPT's edge payload limit before any model ran, and Regions 2 and 3 only ever failed as "upstream failed". The guards cap every request well under the limit, and each pass is pinned to 1024×1024 out so a pass's output is exactly the size the next pass's resized mask has. The trade-off: the finished photo is at most 1024 on its long edge, whatever you upload.

Cost is the simple part: 3 × $0.039 for Nano Banana Edit at 1024×1024 (2026-09-12 catalog snapshot, evidence/catalogs-2026-09-12.json) = $0.117, about 12¢ a run; uploads and the local Resize guards are free. Prices are observations, not caps. Live-proof status is recorded in the session log and the recipe history below; the graph is also validated structurally and by a full offline mock run of the executor (which checks that pass 2 really receives pass 1's output, mask 2 and prompt 2).

Honest limits: "untouched" outside the mask is a measured model behaviour on the verification harness, not a pixel-perfect guarantee on your photo — a pass re-generates its output image, so check edges at mask boundaries, where a halo or a lighting mismatch can show. Three passes also means three chances for a seam; if a region needs no repaint, the cheapest fix is a fully black mask and a prompt that describes what is already there.
