## Notes

Every image in this catalog is audited from the viewpoint it was painted from. None asks the harder question: does the scene survive being looked at from the other side? A generator that truly holds a scene in three dimensions should be able to show its back — the row reversed left to right, every face and eye and headlight gone, and a set of features that were never visible in the front view (a wind-up key, a short round tail, a green sepal cup) suddenly present. A generator that only holds a flat picture paints the front again, or invents backs that contradict the fronts. This flow builds that test. Give it a setting (the default is a toy-shop window shelf against a cream wall) and a GLM director writes a FRONT CARD: exactly 5 display pieces chosen from a menu of 6 with canonical front and back features authored in the card itself (teddy bear, toy robot, plush owl, toy car, potted sunflower, cuckoo clock), a distinct colour each, ordered by front-view position. A second GLM turns the card into a front-view painter prompt and Muse paints the shelf from the front. The turn happens on paper: a GLM predictor, working from the card alone and never from the front picture, writes the BACK PREDICTION — back position 1 is front position 5, the order fully reversed, each piece's BACK features moved to SHOWS and its FRONT features to HIDES. A third GLM turns the prediction into a back-view painter prompt and Muse generates the back view from text alone — deliberately not an edit of the front picture, so no pixels are shared and the second image can only agree with the first through the words. A blind Gemini surveyor, who never saw the prediction, inventories the back view piece by piece with the features it can actually see, plus a FRONT FEATURES SEEN line. A GLM auditor scores IDENTITY (X of 5 predicted pieces found with matching colours), ORDER (X of 5 back positions matching the reversed prediction position by position), BACKS SHOWN (X of 15 predicted back features clearly present on the right piece), and FRONT LEAK (how many front features the survey reports seeing anyway). A ⚖️ Decide judge (PPLX Decider) sees the back picture and all the texts and gives the headline verdict: **true turnaround**, **fronts leaked**, **order kept**, **backs drifted**, or **different display**.

Why the loop matters: viewpoint transformation is a mechanism no audit in this catalog expresses. Mirror check reads a reflection inside one frame — the same pixels, split by region. Words-only repaint sends one image through words and back at the same viewpoint, scoring what survives the description. Here the expectation itself is a transformation: reversal of order plus a feature swap (fronts must vanish, backs must appear), authored before the second render exists, and the second render is an independent generation, so agreement between the two pictures is evidence about spatial consistency carried through language, not about copying. The FRONT LEAK axis names the signature failure of flat-picture generators — asked for a back, they paint eyes and faces anyway because the front is the canonical view — and ORDER separates the two ways a back view can be wrong: the right backs in the unreversed order (a mirror of the front arrangement, not a walk around it) versus genuinely drifted backs.

Honest limits, in the open: the back features are authored in the director's menu, not read off the front render — the flow never audits whether the front picture's pieces actually have the backs the card claims, because a flat front view cannot show them; what is tested is whether prediction, second render and blind read-back agree. The front view itself is not independently audited: if the front painter swaps a piece, the prediction (built from the card, not the picture) and the back audit proceed regardless, and a front/back mismatch of that kind is visible to a human comparing the pair but scored by no line. The surveyor's feature naming is the audit's raw material, so near-name calls (a 'wooden back panel' for the predicted 'flat back panel') are absorbed by the auditor's near-wording rule and a strict reader would score lower. Fifteen back features is a lot to land in one generation; the true-turnaround bar (at least 12 of 15, leak 0) is set expecting some loss, and the BACKS SHOWN denominator is printed rather than hidden. Finally, the pieces stand in a row on a shelf by design: with depth, occlusion from behind would hide pieces behind each other and the reversal rule would stop being a clean permutation — the flow tests the transformation where it is mechanically checkable and says nothing about cluttered scenes.
