## Notes

Garden design advice stops at a planting list, and garden inspiration stops at a photo of somebody else's bed in June. Neither answers the only question that matters for your bed: if you plant exactly this, in these rows, at these counts, what will it actually look like — and did the picture you are imagining follow the plan at all? This graph does both halves and then closes a loop neither closes: a GLM planter writes a 6-line planting plan from a brief — two plants per row in a three-row bed (tall BACK plants against the fence, medium MIDDLE plants, low FRONT edging), each line with an exact count and a flower colour, honouring the brief's hard constraints (the default brief is a pollinator-friendly cottage bed in purples, pinks and whites only, and bans invasive plants, vegetables and anything red, orange or yellow) — a second GLM turns the plan into a photo brief, and Muse renders the finished bed in full bloom. Then a Gemini vision bed checker inventories only what is actually visible, row by row with counts, a GLM auditor scores the original plan against that inventory line by line (PLANTED / WRONG ROW / WRONG COUNT / MISSING, EXTRA lines for anything unplanned, ending in an "X of 6 planted" summary), and a ⚖️ Decide judge (PPLX Decider) gives the headline verdict: **planted to plan**, **counts drifted**, **rows drifted**, or **plants missing**. The render is not decoration here; it is the claim, and two independent reads hold it to the plan.

Why the loop matters: image models are enthusiastic gardeners. They fill every gap with the prettiest flower in reach, let the front edging creep up into the middle because the composition breathes better there, turn a drift of five into a vague sweep, and slip a photogenic rose in at the corner. A plan alone cannot catch that, and a render alone looks convincing precisely when it is wrong. Splitting the jobs across four model families is what makes the check honest: the model that wrote the plan is not the model that rendered it, and neither of them grades it. The row rule is the garden-specific twist — cottage-garden planting has been layered tall-back, low-front for a century, because a plant can be present at exactly the right count and the bed is still planted wrong: the lavender swamping the middle row shades out everything behind it, and the foxgloves at the front edge lean over the lawn.

Honest limits, in the open: the bed check reads a 512 px shrink of the render, so individual plants half-hidden behind their neighbours are judgement calls, and the checker's "(?)" guesses flow straight into the audit — read the WRONG COUNT and WRONG ROW lines against the render yourself before you trust them. Plant identification from a render is also fallible: two pink mounding perennials can swap names between the render and the read-back, which becomes a false MISSING plus a false EXTRA. Counting individual plants in a drift is genuinely hard — a clump read as one plant or three moves the count without anything being planted wrong. The auditor is a text comparison of two lists, so a row the vision checker misjudges becomes a false WRONG ROW. And the render is a goal picture of one June, not a photograph of a bed anyone planted: it proves the plan fits one rectangle in full bloom, not that your real plants will hold their rows — real perennials spread, self-seed and lean, and the audit grades a still. And 6 lines is a design choice, not a law — edit the planter's system prompt and the auditor's summary line together if you plant bigger beds. One deliberate guard from this catalog's own repair history: the photo-brief node is capped (maxTokens 300, reasoningEffort low, the pairing the mural-wall repair landed on), because an uncapped brief generation on a detailed plan once ran past the HTTP timeout three runs in a row.

Expected cost per run: Muse text-to-image $0.011, the Gemini bed check roughly $0.001–0.012, the three GLM calls effectively $0.00, and the Decide audit is not priced in the 2026-09-12 catalog snapshot — expect roughly $0.02–0.04 all in. Prices are observations, not caps.

Live proof, 2026-11-11: the full chain ran end to end for $0.0326 balance-verified ($24.4111 → $24.3786, exactly the metered sum once the bed check's delayed billing settled; Muse render $0.011, Gemini bed check $0.02155, GLM calls $0.00, Decide unpriced in the report), no staging input needed — the garden brief is text. The planter honoured every hard constraint (purples, pinks and whites only; nothing invasive; heights matched to rows) and planned 4 deep violet-blue delphiniums and 3 white phlox 'David' at the back, 5 magenta-purple coneflowers and 5 violet 'Caradonna' salvias in the middle, 7 rose-pink garden pinks 'Rose de Mai' and 5 white Carpathian bellflowers 'White Clips' at the front. Muse's render is visually verified: exactly that bed, stone-edged against the fence, and the row system held perfectly on its first outing — every plant in its planned row, back row and the garden pinks at their exact counts. Both judges agree the counts drifted in the middle and front: Decide returned **counts drifted** and the audit scored **3 of 6 planted** — coneflowers read back 3 against 5 planned (the checker counted clumps where the render shows more flowering stems, the drift-counting ambiguity the limits paragraph predicts), salvias 4 against 5, bellflowers 4 against 5.
