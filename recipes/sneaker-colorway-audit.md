## Notes

Sneaker colorway tools do one trick: pick a palette, watch a render of *a* shoe in those colours — and the classic failures are that the recolour lands on the wrong panels, a colour you never asked for appears on the sole, or the "same shoe, new colours" is quietly a different silhouette with a logo it never had, and nothing in the tool ever checks the render against the plan or the original shoe. This graph splits the jobs across four model families and closes both loops: a Gemini vision node is the parts clerk (it writes a shoe card — exactly 7 numbered parts in a fixed order, toe box to outsole, each with its current colour, material and finish, a Fixed line for the silhouette, sole shape, stitching, panel layout, logo state, angle and background that must NOT change, and the current palette as the one thing allowed to change), a GLM node changes hats into a colorway designer (it plans ONE colorway for your brief citing those part numbers — at least 4 parts get a new colour, every part is accounted for exactly once as recoloured or KEEP, and the brief's hard constraints, no neon and no metallic in the shipped brief, win over the designer's taste), a second GLM compresses the plan into a single surgical edit instruction that starts with the keep-fixed list, and Muse Edit recolours the original photo itself, not a text description of a shoe.

Then the render has to answer for itself, twice. A second Gemini vision node reads the render back and inventories only the parts actually visible in it — it never sees the card or the plan, so it cannot grade generously — ending with its own Fixed line. A GLM colorway auditor runs two passes over the card, the plan and that read-back: pass 1 scores every planned colour line APPLIED / WRONG COLOUR / UNCHANGED (a part the plan marks KEEP counts as applied only when the read-back still shows the card colour), and pass 2 compares the Fixed lines, ending in an "X of Y planned colours applied, fixed list kept/changed" summary. And a ⚖️ Decide judge (PPLX Decider) gives the headline verdict from the card plus plan plus the render image: **faithful colorway**, **colours drifted**, **barely recoloured**, or **different shoe**. The graph exports the **Recolour — audited** verdict text, and the render itself rides through the Decide node as its image output — open the Recolour node to see the shoe the verdict is about.

Why the split matters: the model that recoloured the shoe is not the model that wrote the shoe card, and neither of them grades the result. The card is written *before* any colour is chosen, so the designer cannot invent an eighth part to hide a miss in, and the part numbers make both cheats — a designer planning colours for parts that do not exist, and a renderer painting the right colour on the wrong panel — mechanically checkable rather than vibes.

Honest limits, in the open: the shoe card is a photo read, not ground truth — the clerk can misname a material (canvas vs mesh is a judgement call at product-photo distance), and every downstream check then faithfully defends the wrong card, so read the card before you trust the audit. The read-back reads a shrunk copy of the render, so near-neighbour colours (rust vs burnt orange) are judgement calls, and the "( ? )" guesses flow straight into the score. Muse Edit re-renders rather than repaints pixels, so stitching, shadows and background can drift even when every part colour lands — pass 2 and the Decide judge watch for exactly that, but they watch at read-back resolution. And a rendered colorway is a design preview, not a manufacturable spec: dye lots, real suede and licensing are between you and a factory. A generated staging photo was used for the live proof below — no real product photo was staged in this workspace — disclosed here rather than smoothed over.

Expected cost per run: Muse Edit $0.011, the two Gemini vision reads roughly $0.005–0.007 each, the three GLM calls effectively $0.00–0.011 combined, and the Decide audit is not priced in the 2026-09-12 catalog snapshot — expect roughly $0.03–0.05 all in. Prices are observations, not caps.

Live proof, 2026-11-09: the full chain ran end to end for $0.0316 balance-verified across two spends ($30.8057 → $30.7741) — a generated staging photo of a plain off-white low-top ($0.0110 exact, Muse; no real product photo was staged, as disclosed above), then ONE live run of the graph ($0.0206 metered, exactly matching its balance delta once billing settled: shoe card $0.00513, Muse Edit $0.011, read-back $0.00444, GLM calls $0.00; Decide unpriced in the report). The clerk's card was exact against the staging shoe — off-white canvas toe, light-grey suede quarters and stripe, white midsole over a gum outsole, no logo. The designer planned "Forest Floor": pine-green toe and heel, chestnut quarter, a burnt-rust stripe, taupe collar, chocolate laces, sole KEEP — six parts recoloured, none invented. Muse Edit's render is visually verified: the same shoe, same silhouette, angle and studio background, in exactly that palette. The judges honestly split: Decide said 'faithful colorway' while the part audit scored 6 of 7 applied with the fixed list kept — the stripe was flagged WRONG COLOUR because the read-back named it "warm brown suede" where the plan said burnt rust; on the pixels the stripe is a rust-brown, so the one miss is the near-neighbour colour-naming judgement call the limits above predict, recorded rather than smoothed over.
