## Notes

Terrarium kits and build guides stop at the shopping list: layers in order, plants on top, good luck. Photo apps go the other way — a pretty jar, no plan behind it. This graph does both halves and then closes a loop neither closes: a GLM builder writes an 11-line build plan from a brief — exactly 5 layers in bottom-to-top order, each with material and colour, then exactly 6 contents with exact counts — a second GLM turns the plan into a photo brief, and Muse renders the finished jar. Then a Gemini vision bench checker inventories only what is actually visible through the glass, layers bottom to top and every plant or placed object with its count, a GLM auditor scores the original plan against that inventory line by line (PRESENT / MISSING / EXTRA, a "Layers in order: yes/no" line, ending in an "X of 11 present" summary), and a ⚖️ Decide judge (PPLX Decider) gives the headline verdict: **built to plan**, **layers out of order**, **pieces missing**, or **extras snuck in**. The render is not decoration here; it is the claim, and two independent reads hold it to the plan.

Why the loop matters: image models are enthusiastic landscapers. They merge two pale layers into one band, drop the fiddly counted item (the three white pebbles become a scatter), and invent a photogenic extra nobody planned — a tiny mushroom, a second succulent. A plan alone cannot catch that, and a render alone looks convincing precisely when it is wrong. Splitting the jobs across four model families is what makes the check honest: the model that wrote the plan is not the model that rendered it, and neither of them grades it. The layer-order line is the terrarium-specific twist: a jar can contain every planned material and still be built wrong, with the charcoal under the gravel where it does no good and shows nothing.

Honest limits, in the open: the bench check reads a 512 px shrink of the render, so thin layers (a charcoal dusting) and counts half-hidden behind glass glare are judgement calls, and the checker's "(?)" guesses flow straight into the audit — read the MISSING lines against the render yourself before you trust them. The auditor is a text comparison of two lists, so a colour the vision checker misreads becomes a false MISSING. The render is a goal picture, not a photograph of a jar you built: it proves the plan is buildable in one jar's worth of space and light, not that your real layers will sit as neatly. And 5 layers plus 6 contents is a design choice, not a law — edit the builder's system prompt and the auditor's count line together if you build bigger.

Expected cost per run: Muse text-to-image $0.011, the Gemini bench check roughly $0.001–0.006, the three GLM calls effectively $0.00–0.011, and the Decide audit is not priced in the 2026-09-12 catalog snapshot — expect roughly $0.02–0.04 all in. Prices are observations, not caps.

Live proof, 2026-11-10: the full chain ran end to end for $0.0127 balance-verified ($30.6824 → $30.6697, exactly the metered sum; Muse render $0.011, Gemini bench check $0.00168, GLM calls $0.00, Decide unpriced in the report), no staging input needed — the terrarium brief is text. The builder planned a desert-meets-coast jar (pumice gravel, charcoal grit, cream sand, tan cactus soil, white shell grit; one blue-green agave centrepiece, 3 weathered stones, 1 driftwood branch, 4 cockle shells, 2 seed pods, 2 aqua sea-glass pebbles), and Muse's render is visually verified as exactly that jar on a light wooden desk. Both judges agree on the contents: Decide returned **built to plan** and the audit scored **11 of 11 present, layers in order: yes**, with every content count exact. One honest flaw, recorded in the open: the read-back split the sand and soil into finer sub-bands (7 visible layers against 5 planned), so the auditor also flagged 2 EXTRA sub-layers — the renderer graded its layers more finely than the plan did, and the audit kept both readings instead of rounding either away. Run report and images: flow-hunt/runs/2026-11-10-terrarium-build-audit/ in the nanoodle-dev workspace (render saved as desert-coast-terrarium.png).

Change the **Terrarium brief** input for style, jar shape, light and hard constraints ("no succulents — ferns and moss only", "a jar for a child's desk, nothing sharp"), and the whole chain re-plans around it. Keep the auditor wired to both the builder's plan and the bench check, or the score stops meaning anything; swap the render model in the Terrarium node's ⚙️ settings if you prefer a different photographic look.
