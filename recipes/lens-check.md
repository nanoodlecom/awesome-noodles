## Notes

A convex lens does not copy its object onto a screen - it computes one, and the computation is exact. The thin-lens law takes a single input, the object's distance measured against the focal length F, and derives everything about the projected image: beyond 2F the real image is inverted and diminished; between F and 2F it is inverted and magnified; inside F there is no real image at all, because the lens forms a virtual image on the object's own side that no screen can catch - the screen stays blank. Image models rarely paint that law. They paint the magnifying-glass stereotype: a big, upright, friendly image hovering on every screen, whatever the geometry. This flow makes the screen a derived, measurable quantity - and then it does something about a lawless one. Give it a plain bench setting and a GLM director picks one bench from a menu printed in its own prompt (FAR CANDLE beyond 2F, NEAR CANDLE between F and 2F, INSIDE-F CANDLE inside F) and copies it onto a LENS CARD: the candle, its distance class, the lens, the screen, and the screen result the law derives from the distance - PROJECTION, IMAGE ORIENTATION and IMAGE SIZE, carried on the card so the audit is mechanical and no downstream node has to redo the physics. A second GLM turns the card into one painter prompt that states the setup concretely - candle position and spacing, lens, screen - and is forbidden from stating, hinting or describing what appears on the screen: what the light leaves there is the painter's optics to get right or wrong. Muse paints it. A blind Gemini reader, who never saw the card, reports the bench and the screen separately: BENCH complete or not, SCREEN IMAGE present or absent (a glow or a smudge does not count), and if present its orientation against the real candle and its size beside it. A GLM auditor scores PROJECTION, ORIENTATION and SIZE: PROJECTION TRUE only when presence, inversion and relative size all follow the law - and for the inside-focal bench, only when the screen is provably blank. A Decide judge (PPLX Decider) headlines round 1 as a side output.

That verdict is not where this flow stops - the loop closes, per the standing rule that a created asset ends in the corrected asset, not in text. A GLM editor reads the card, the read and the audit and writes ONE concrete edit instruction aimed at the screen's surface only: remove a forbidden image and leave the screen plain blank white, form the missing inverted image at the carded size, flip an upright image flame-down, or rescale a wrong-sized one - while the candle, the lens, the stands and the framing are held exactly. The editor is gated in content, the rule this catalog learned from Molecule Check: on a PROJECTION TRUE audit it must demand a verbatim unchanged copy. Muse Edit applies the notes. The corrected bench is the flow's deliverable. A second blind reader reports the corrected screen, and the same auditor scores round 2 against the same card, so the fix is itself audited and the reader can see exactly what the edit bought - or cost.

Why this axis is new: the catalog's nearest neighbours are refraction-check and mirror-check, and the differences are mechanical. Refraction audits a background pattern displaced inside a lens silhouette - the lens as a window that bends what is behind it; it never forms a second, separate picture anywhere. Mirror-check audits a reflected scene read for order - a single-axis flip of the same scene on the same surface. Lens Check's lens FORMS a new picture on a different surface, a screen across the bench, and that picture's very existence is one of the scored quantities: it is a 180-degree rotation of the object (not a mirror flip), at a size the distance law fixes, and in one carded case it must not exist at all. The inside-focal bench is a scored non-event, a class this catalog has used for controls inside other audits but never as a bench's whole passing condition: the flow passes only if the screen is provably blank, and the painter's favourite image - upright, magnified, helpful - is precisely the failure. Nothing here is placed, counted or recounted free-form; a distance is carded, a screen result is derived from it in the open, and the derivation is certified against the pixels.

Honest limits, in the open: the law is scored in classes, not millimetres - diminished means plainly smaller than the candle in the same picture, magnified plainly larger - so the flow certifies the derivation's shape, not a focal length measured off the render; a painter who sets the candle at the wrong spacing but projects the carded result still passes the screen audit, because distance is read as bench spacing in the DETAIL line, not scored as its own axis. A faint, soft candle glow on the screen is a genuine naming border for the reader: the brief to the reader rules that a glow, bright patch or shadow is ABSENT, and both blind reads apply that rule independently, which is the only guard. The edit round has its own limit: an edit model asked to erase or form an image on the screen can relight the bench around it - that is exactly what round 2's separate object-and-screen read is for, and both audits are printed so the reader can see the trade. One repair round is unrolled, not a loop - if round 2 still violates, the flow shows the violation rather than spending again.
