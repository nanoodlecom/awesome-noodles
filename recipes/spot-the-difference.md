## Notes

Spot-the-difference is the oldest puzzle in the world — two pictures, a handful of quiet changes, find them all — and it turns out to be a sharp instrument for measuring two things generative models are bad at: changing exactly what they were told to change, and seeing what actually changed. Muse paints a cluttered wizard's desk from your brief. A Gemini clerk inventories the finished picture into a SCENE CARD: every prominent movable object, numbered, with its place, colour and material, plus a FIXED line for viewpoint and light. A GLM planner picks five different card items and plans exactly one big, unmissable change each — a REMOVE (the object is gone), a RECOLOUR (a bold new colour), or an ADD (one new object beside an anchor item) — with at least one remove and one recolour in every plan. A second GLM compresses the plan into a single surgical edit instruction that names no card numbers, and Muse Edit applies it to the original picture. Then a Gemini spotter is shown BOTH pictures at once, before first and after second, and lists every difference it can see. The spotter never sees the plan. A GLM auditor scores the plan against the spotted list — SPOTTED or NOT SPOTTED per change, an UNPLANNED line for any spotted difference nobody ordered — ending in "X of 5 changes spotted, Y unplanned differences". A ⚖️ Decide judge (PPLX Decider) sees both pictures and all three texts and gives the headline verdict: **exactly the five**, **five plus extras**, **some changes missing**, or **barely changed**.

Why the loop matters: every other audit in this catalog compares a text against one picture — a plan, a card, a read-back inventory of the render alone. This one compares two pictures directly, pixels against pixels, inside a single vision call. That is a mechanically different audit, and it catches the failures text intermediation cannot: a cushion that quietly turned blue survives nobody's inventory wording, an object that vanished leaves no trace in a read-back of the after picture (you cannot inventory an absence), and an edit model's collateral damage — the jar that moved, the coins that multiplied — is only visible against the original. The source of truth is also a different shape: not a state (what should be there) but a transformation (what should differ), scored in both directions — planned changes the spotter cannot find, and unplanned differences the editor was never asked for. The nearest catalog neighbour, the ADetailer finisher, re-checks one edited image against a defect list; it never diffs the edit against the original, and collateral changes outside the defect list cost it nothing. Here they are half the score.

Honest limits, in the open: a NOT SPOTTED verdict is two failures wearing one coat — the editor may never have made the change, or the spotter may have looked straight at it and missed it — and the texts cannot separate the two; your own eyes on the pair can, which is why the pair is the real output. The same ambiguity runs the other way: an UNPLANNED difference may be genuine edit drift (generative editors repaint the whole frame, so "change nothing else" is an aspiration, not a guarantee) or a spotter hallucination, and subtle global shifts — light, sharpness, texture — can flood a spotter's list with noise or, worse, make it stop looking for object changes. The planner can only change what the clerk carded, so an object the clerk missed is unchangeable and unscoreable. And five changes is a deliberate difficulty setting: enough that a perfect score means something, few enough that a human can play along and beat the spotter.

Live proof, 2026-11-14 — SUCCESS end to end on the first attempt, all nodes done, no errors, $0.0322 balance-verified. Muse's desk is rich and cardable: the clerk listed 12 objects (grimoire, telescope, letters, green potion bottle, candle, grey cat, burnt-orange cushion, tankard, inkwell, herb parchment, wax seals, gold coins). The planner chose REMOVE the cat, RECOLOUR the potion bottle green to electric blue, ADD a wooden spoon beside the tankard, REMOVE the gold coins, RECOLOUR the cushion burnt-orange to vivid purple — and Muse Edit landed all five, visibly and exactly, in a frame otherwise strikingly faithful to the original. The blind spotter, shown both pictures and never the plan, found all five planned changes and two more that nobody ordered: one of the two red wax seals vanished, and the loose feather lying at the bottom centre of the desk vanished with it — both confirmed on the pixels, genuine edit collateral of exactly the kind a read-back of the after picture alone could never catch (you cannot inventory an absence). The GLM audit scored 5 of 5 changes SPOTTED with 2 unplanned differences, and Decide returned 'five plus extras' — the honest headline: a perfect plan score, an imperfect editor. Both pictures are visually verified, and the pair plays as a real puzzle.
