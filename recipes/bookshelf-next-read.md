## Notes

"Pick my next read" sounds like one prompt, and the failure it hides is one every reader knows: the recommender suggests a book you do not own, a sequel whose first volume you never bought, or a 900-page epic for a two-day weekend — and if an image model then draws the recommendation, it draws a cover whose title lettering is confident gibberish. This graph splits the jobs across four model families and audits both places the lie can enter. A Gemini vision node is the shelf clerk (it reads the shelf spine by spine and writes the inventory — every book it can actually read, numbered in shelf order, with title and author exactly as printed, spine colour and one distinguishing detail each, "( ? )" where it is guessing, and no credit for a book it can only guess at from its colour), a GLM node changes hats into a librarian (it picks ONE next read for your brief using only inventory items, copying the title and author word for word and citing the inventory number, defending the pick's finishability inside the brief's time window, copying the book's spine colour onto a Cover line, sketching the first evening's reading scene, and naming two runners-up and why each lost), a second GLM compresses the slip into a single still-life prompt, and Muse renders the pick from that text alone as a cosy reading scene with the title and author printed on the cover.

Then the pick has to answer for itself, twice, at two different checkpoints. A second Gemini vision node reads the still life back and transcribes the rendered cover letter by letter — title and author exactly as printed, "( ? )" where the lettering defeats it — without ever seeing the slip, so it cannot grade generously. A GLM phantom auditor runs two passes over the three texts: pass one checks the slip's pick against the inventory line its number cites (a librarian inventing or retitling a book is caught here, before any rendering), and pass two judges the read-back transcription against the slip (TITLE / AUTHOR, each CORRECT, GARBLED or WRONG — garbled being the honest middle state image models land in most: the right book, its lettering mangled), plus a PHANTOM line for any second titled book the renderer snuck in, ending in an "X of 2 identity marks correct, Z phantoms" summary. And a ⚖️ Decide judge (PPLX Decider) gives the headline verdict from the inventory, the slip and the still-life image together: **right book, legible**, **right book, garbled**, **wrong book**, or **no book visible**. The graph exports the **Still life — audited** verdict text, and the still life itself rides through the Decide node as its image output — open the still-life node to see the book the verdict is about.

Why the split matters: the inventory is written before any picking happens, so neither the librarian nor the renderer can quietly expand your shelf to make the recommendation easier; the inventory-number citation is what makes the plan audit mechanical rather than vibes — a checker does not have to decide whether a suggested book "feels" owned, it compares line #3 against line #3. And cover lettering is the one place a beautiful render most often lies, so it gets its own transcription checkpoint instead of being waved through as part of the picture's general prettiness: a still life can be gorgeous and still be a picture of a book that does not exist.

Honest limits, in the open: the inventory is a photo read, not ground truth — a spine turned away, cropped out or too faded to read does not exist for this graph, and then the librarian is forbidden from picking a book that is standing right there; read the inventory before you trust the audit. The clerk knows nothing about the books beyond their spines — finishability is judged from the brief and general knowledge of a title's length and weight, not from page counts it cannot see, so treat the Why line as a librarian's judgement, not a measurement. The still life is rendered from the slip's text, not photographed from your copy, so it shows the pick's idea — cover colours come from the spine, and a real cover design will differ. And lettering strictness cuts both ways: a transcription marked "( ? )" by a cautious reader can score a correct cover as garbled. The staging shelf used for the live proof below was typeset locally (drawn with PIL, word-perfect by construction, in the open) rather than photographed — no real bookshelf was staged in this workspace — precisely because a generated photo's spines are usually garbled, which would corrupt the inventory before the pipeline being tested even starts; your own shelf photo will not have that problem.

Expected cost per run: Muse still life $0.011, the two Gemini vision reads roughly $0.004–0.008 each, the three GLM calls effectively $0.00–0.002 combined, and the Decide audit is not priced in the 2026-09-12 catalog snapshot — expect roughly $0.02–0.04 all in. Prices are observations, not caps.

Live proof, 2026-11-09: ONE live run of the full graph, end to end, $0.0216 — metered $0.02164 and balance-verified to the digit once billing settled ($30.8273 → $30.8057: shelf read $0.00581, Muse still life $0.011, read-back $0.00483, the three GLM calls $0.00 at flash rates, Decide unpriced in the report; no staging spend — the shelf was typeset locally, as disclosed above). The clerk's inventory was word-perfect against the staging shelf: all 12 books in shelf order, every title and author right, spine colours and base bands right, even the two potted plants noted. The librarian picked THE ALCHEMIST (inventory #8), citing the number correctly, copying the teal-and-white-band cover line exactly, and defending the pick against #11 The Little Prince (over in one sitting) and #7 Siddhartha (denser prose) — exactly the brief's tired-brain logic. Muse's still life is visually verified: the teal book on the armchair arm, rain-streaked window, steaming mug of tea, ribbon bookmark — and the title and author lettering is perfect, which the read-back transcription confirms letter for letter. For once both judges agree completely: Decide returned 'right book, legible' and the identity audit scored TITLE correct, AUTHOR correct, 2 of 2 marks, 0 phantoms. Two honest flaws, both caught in the open: the brief's "bookmark resting at page 60" leaked into the render as a printed '60' inside the cover's white band (the read-back reported it, even though it cost no marks), and the book is physically impossible in a way no model flagged — its front cover is displayed flat while the same book lies open beneath it.
