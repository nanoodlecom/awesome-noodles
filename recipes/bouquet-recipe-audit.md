## Notes

Florist apps and bouquet builders stop at the inspiration photo: a lush arrangement, no recipe behind it, and no way to order the same bouquet twice. This graph does both halves and then closes a loop neither closes: a GLM florist writes a 7-line recipe from a brief — 6 flowers and 1 foliage, each with a colour and an exact stem count, honouring the brief's hard constraints (the default brief bans lilies because there is a cat in the house) — a second GLM turns the recipe into a photo brief, and Muse renders the finished bouquet in a vase. Then a Gemini vision bench checker counts only what is actually visible, flower head by flower head, a GLM auditor scores the original recipe against that count line by line (PRESENT at the exact count / WRONG COUNT / MISSING, EXTRA lines for anything unplanned, ending in an "X of 7 exact" summary), and a ⚖️ Decide judge (PPLX Decider) gives the headline verdict: **built to recipe**, **counts drifted**, **stems missing**, or **extras snuck in**. The render is not decoration here; it is the claim, and two independent reads hold it to the recipe.

Why the loop matters: image models are generous florists. Asked for five coral dahlias they arrange "a bunch", quietly drop the one cool-contrast flower that made the palette work, and slip in a photogenic extra nobody ordered. A recipe alone cannot catch that, and a render alone looks convincing precisely when its counts are wrong. Splitting the jobs across four model families is what makes the check honest: the model that wrote the recipe is not the model that rendered it, and neither of them grades it. The exact-count rule is the bouquet-specific twist: "some blue flowers in there somewhere" is not a recipe a florist can rebuild — 3 stems of one named flower at one named colour is.

Honest limits, in the open: the bench check reads a 512 px shrink of the render, so heads half-hidden behind bigger blooms are judgement calls, and the checker's "(?)" guesses flow straight into the audit — read the WRONG COUNT lines against the render yourself before you trust them. Flower identification from a render is also fallible: two similar pompon shapes in near colours can swap names between the render and the read-back, which becomes a false MISSING plus a false EXTRA. The auditor is a text comparison of two lists, so a colour the vision checker misreads becomes a false WRONG COUNT. And the render is a goal picture, not a photograph of a bouquet you ordered: it proves the recipe fits one vase and one palette, not that a real florist's stems will open as neatly. And 7 lines is a design choice, not a law — edit the florist's system prompt and the auditor's count line together if you build bigger.

Expected cost per run: Muse text-to-image $0.011, the Gemini bench check roughly $0.001–0.006, the three GLM calls effectively $0.00–0.011, and the Decide audit is not priced in the 2026-09-12 catalog snapshot — expect roughly $0.02–0.04 all in. Prices are observations, not caps.

Live proof, 2026-11-10: the full chain ran end to end for $0.0186 balance-verified ($30.6697 → $30.6511, exactly the metered sum once the bench check's delayed billing settled; Muse render $0.011, Gemini bench check $0.00763, GLM calls $0.00, Decide unpriced in the report), no staging input needed — the bouquet brief is text. The florist honoured every ban (no lilies, no roses) and planned a sunset recipe: 3 coral peonies, 4 apricot ranunculus, 4 peach stock, 5 butter-yellow craspedia, 4 butter-yellow freesia, 3 soft-blue nigella as the one cool contrast, 5 silvery-green olive foliage stems. Muse's render is visually verified as exactly that bouquet in a clear glass cylinder on pale linen — every planned kind present, nothing banned, no card or text. Both judges agree it is not exact: Decide returned **counts drifted** and the audit scored **3 of 7 exact** (peonies, ranunculus and craspedia at their planned counts). The honest flaws, recorded in the open: nigella rendered 4 heads against 3 planned and the olive foliage read back as 3 sprigs against 5; and two lines exposed a units trap — stock and freesia carry many florets per stem, so the bench check reported them two ways at once (stock: 23 florets across 2 stems, against 4 stems planned) and the auditor scored them on stem count. Single-head flowers count cleanly in this loop; spray flowers need the recipe written in heads, not stems. Run report and images: flow-hunt/runs/2026-11-10-bouquet-recipe-audit/ in the nanoodle-dev workspace (render saved as sunset-birthday-bouquet.png).

Change the **Bouquet brief** input for occasion, palette and hard constraints ("a white-and-green sympathy bouquet, no scented flowers", "autumn colours only, nothing pink"), and the whole chain re-plans around it. Keep the auditor wired to both the florist's recipe and the bench check, or the score stops meaning anything; swap the render model in the Bouquet node's ⚙️ settings if you prefer a different photographic look.
